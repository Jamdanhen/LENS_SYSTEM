# Axis Limitations: Twenty-Percent Steps

Date: 2026-09-15
Status: user-authorized test; no Axis discount or new limitation schedule adopted.

Correction, 2026-09-15: this test misinterpreted the user's proposal. The intended
model contains five scope dimensions with five limitation steps each (25 total).
Five removed steps total 20%, whether concentrated in one dimension or distributed
one per dimension. The user did not propose an advancement cap. The opportunity

## Candidate

The user proposed graded restrictions in Duration, Area, Distance, Intensity,
and Selectivity. Selectivity includes both inability to choose among eligible
targets and inability to affect certain targets. These are candidate limitation
dimensions within an affected HOLT Axis, not replacement definitions of HOLT
Axes or five new purchased character ratings.

Test a 20%-per-step adjustment to the current 15-point Axis-rank price:

| 20% | 12 | 120 |
| 40% | 9 | 90 |
| 60% | 6 | 60 |
| 80% | 3 | 30 |

This is candidate development pricing, a change from the current full-price
Axis baseline. Whole-HOLT acquisition remains 40 before its separately adopted
whole-package limitations. Do not also discount acquisition for the same loss
in these comparisons. No character balances, refunds, retroactive credits, or
reinvestment permissions change. The five usable prices above do not adopt a
universal 80% ceiling or define the user's proposed sub-rank scale automatically.

## Concentrated Versus Distributed Burden

Use synthetic request sets to see whether four 20% restrictions necessarily
equal one 80% restriction. Each of five dimensions has five equally represented
demand classes, producing 3,125 combinations. These are expressly artificial
request classes, not actual Difficulty bands, observed play frequencies, or
percentages of mechanical Effect output.

For this diagnostic only, a restriction rules out fulfilling the request as
stated. A lesser effect or another route may remain possible. This does not
convert all limitations into binary Access or replace their fictional workings.

| Candidate restriction | Requests ruled out in the stated fixture |
| --- | --- |
| Selectivity: only one of five equally encountered target classes | 80% |
| Four restrictions each excluding 20%, independent demand classes | 59.04% |
| Four restrictions each excluding the SAME 20% of situations | 20% |
| Four restrictions each excluding a DIFFERENT, nonoverlapping 20% | 80% |

The independent case retains 0.8^4 = 40.96% of complete requests. This product
is a property of that deliberately independent fixture, NOT a recommendation
to multiply discounts. Correlated and disjoint fixtures demonstrate why neither
blind addition nor blind multiplication measures all combined burdens.

The user's distributed-80% example works in the disjoint fixture. It is not
universally equivalent merely because four restrictions have different labels.
For example, an effect limited to short range, small area, and brief duration
may lose many of the same large-scale uses repeatedly while retaining its main
close-combat application. Conversely, distinct restrictions may remove four
different important uses. Their narrative interaction must be assessed.

If one 20% restriction in EACH of five categories is blindly added, the discount
reaches 100% and rank price becomes zero. Yet the independent fixture still
retains 32.768% of its complete requests. This is a concrete failure of automatic
addition, not evidence that the capability ceased to exist or must be freely
ranked. No zero-price development or universal adjustment ceiling is adopted.

## Campaign Sensitivity And Selectivity

Suppose an effect can affect only machines, one of five named target classes.
If machines comprise 20%, 60%, or 80% of relevant campaign opportunities, the
same restriction excludes 80%, 40%, or 20% respectively. Counting excluded class
names does not establish the loss of useful capability. These percentages are
illustrative campaign mixes, not population statistics or automatic prices.

The other form of selectivity has a different burden: an effect cannot spare
allies among eligible exposed targets. That may leave the effect usable but
dangerous, with consequences rather than a simple loss of permission. An empty
corridor and a crowded rescue scene make the same restriction matter differently.
Its severity cannot be determined by a percentage of target classes. This
comparison assumes selective use was otherwise available; it grants no new
baseline ability to exclude targets.

Not every restricted quantity has a linear gameplay value. Losing 20% of maximum
distance, maximum duration, or intensity does not necessarily remove 20% of
useful attempts. The five dimensions locate the restriction; they do not yet
assign a mechanically proven price to every degree of restriction.

## Rank-Six Example

Continue the expressly stated comparison interpretation: maximum purchased
Axis Rank 6, with a proposed 40% price reduction. A ceiling on TARGET ratings
would be a different selectivity fixture and should not silently inherit these
results. This test does not settle any different intended meaning of that example.

| Purchase | Development cost |
| --- | --- |
| Six ranks at normal 15-point price | 90 |
| Six ranks at proposed 9-point price | 54 |
| Ten unrestricted ranks at normal price | 150 |

The actual discount on the SAME six ranks is 36. Comparing 150 against 54 also
includes 60 points for four ranks never purchased; that part is not returned
compensation. Acquisition remains separately 40, giving 94 for acquisition plus
the restricted maximum, versus 190 for acquisition plus unrestricted Rank 10.

Rank 6 supplies x1.6 versus Rank 10's x2.0. Removing 40% of the purchasable ranks
therefore lowers the maximum otherwise-identical Expression by 20%, before
flooring and other resolution consequences. At the SAME purchased Rank 6,
both versions provide x1.6; the restricted one is cheaper because it gives up
future development beyond six. For a character never intending to exceed six,
the restriction may have little practical burden. For one seeking high output,
the lost ceiling is real. The arithmetic works; it does not by itself validate
40% as a universal ceiling-discount price.

## Equal-Budget Breadth

With 150 eligible points allocated solely for Axis development, unrestricted
Rank 10 costs all 150. At an 80% discount, Rank 10 costs 30 and leaves 120
unspent while retaining x2.0 when the restricted use applies. The character's
restriction must justify that large saving; it is not automatically fair
because the affected route is narrow on paper. No permitted use of the remaining
120 is newly established here. Protected creation allocations remain protected.

## Recommendation And Decision

Retain 20% as a clean candidate step for assessing the combined burden on an
affected Axis. The resulting prices 15/12/9/6/3 are easy to use. Use the five
dimensions to describe what is lost, then assess the overall limitation once.
This preserves the user's ability to combine restrictions and the earlier
combined-burden principle, without mandatory addition or a substitute product.

Do not automatically award an 80% reduction for four differently named 20%
restrictions, or for excluding four of five target labels. Equally, do not
reject distributed restrictions merely because some overlap: the disjoint
fixture shows the proposed equivalence can hold. Assess actual combined burden.

Keep Rank 6 at a 40% discount as a priced test candidate, not a settled universal
formula converting lost ranks into discounts. Exact sub-rank meanings, individual
limitation placements, buyoff of discounted development, and reinvestment remain
open. No adopted acquisition prices, Axis prices, awards, or balances changed.

The next decision is whether to use 20% steps on the assessed combined Axis
burden as provisional construction guidance, leaving particular placements
subject to Profile-specific testing rather than summing category discounts.
