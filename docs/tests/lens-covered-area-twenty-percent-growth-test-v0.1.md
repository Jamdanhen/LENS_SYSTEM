# Covered-Area Growth At Twenty Percent

Date: 2026-09-13
Status: user-authorized test, not adoption of growth or lookup thresholds.

Subsequent decision, 2026-09-15: the user confirmed the proposed 20%-of-original
covered-footprint conversion as settled and instructed moving on. Baseline and
terminology now record the working conversion, without a universal ceiling.
The test below remains the historical comparison; its lookup thresholds and
physical units were not adopted by this confirmation.

## Candidate And Controls

A successful permitted declaration establishes its declared footprint without
an additional entry expenditure. Test each additional Effect assigned to growth
adding 20% of the original declared covered area:

```text
Resulting area = original area x (1 + 0.20 x Effects spent on growth)
```

This is covered footprint, not radius or map-cell count. Growth does not
compound or restart from the enlarged area. From 25 units, successive totals
are 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50. No new permission, shape, selective exclusion,
time extension, or universal ceiling is introduced.

Effects spent on expansion are subtracted once from generated output. Only
additional Effects are spent: retain at least the initial successful Effect.
For example, reaching 50 from 25 requires six generated Effects: five spent
on expansion, one remaining. This is not an extra area-entry charge.

Compare direct declarations against the existing candidate area ladder:
25/D1, 50/D2, 100/D3, 200/D4, and continued doubling. These lookup values are
test controls, not newly adopted thresholds. Shared distance and duration bands
are identical on both routes and add under the adopted aggregation rule.

The script checks 264 combinations: six capability fixtures, starting areas
25/50/100, targets 1.2/2/4/8/16 times the starting area, and shared bands 0/3/4.
Six combinations that would exceed the current D10 horizon are excluded.

Fixtures use the adopted A + D foundation, Task/Field/Specialty/Precision
multipliers, and one applicable Axis. Ordinary ratings and Axis are respectively
3/3, 5/0, 5/5, 8/8, 10/0, and 10/10, producing raw Expressions 22.27758,
50.625, 75.9375, 302.33088, 320, and 640. These are controlled routes, not
complete equal-budget characters. Axis 0 still has defined Open Access.

All attempts are permitted, at matched Scale and full Function, with no
equipment, contextual adjustments, paid spending, opposition, or mitigation.
The comparison isolates uncertain manifestation checks; routine actions are
not forced to roll. Existing natural-01 continuations are included. Natural 00
produces no successful Effects. A natural 01 does not guarantee enough surplus
to reach the enlarged target.

## Growth Costs

| Starting area | Target area | Additional Effects spent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 25 | 30 | 1 |
| 25 | 50 | 5 |
| 25 | 100 | 15 |
| 25 | 200 | 35 |
| 25 | 400 | 75 |

Each doubling of the original declaration is not another five Effects. Five
Effects add the original area once; fifteen add it three times. This distinction
is why the candidate does not turn every five Effects into a new Difficulty band.

## Chance Of Reaching The Same Target

Area challenge only; start at 25 for the expansion route:

| Ordinary ratings / Axis | Target | Expand 25 | Declare target directly |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 / 5 | 30 | 64% | 44% |
| 5 / 5 | 50 | 24% | 44% |
| 5 / 5 | 100 | 0.23% | 26% |
| 8 / 8 | 50 | 99% | 99% |
| 8 / 8 | 100 | 99% | 99% |
| 8 / 8 | 200 | 0.45% | 63% |
| 10 / 10 | 200 | 99% | 99% |
| 10 / 10 | 400 | 0.0075% | 81% |

These measure attaining the named footprint, not opposed damage. Failing to
reach the expansion target can still leave a successful smaller footprint;
the target-only metric does not erase that result. Direct failure has no
successful declared-area output. Expansion's fallback and post-roll allocation
flexibility are benefits beyond the percentages shown.

The 25-to-30 comparison deliberately checks a boundary crossing. A direct
30 declaration faces the same candidate D2 as 50, whereas expansion costs one
Effect rather than five. This advantage near a lookup boundary is not a
radius/hex conversion error and does not itself settle acceptable overage.

## High Capability And Remaining Strength

At raw 640, with an ordinary roll of 40 and no shared additional demands:

| Target area | Expand from 25: Effects remaining | Direct declaration: Effects remaining |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 50 | 54 | 34 |
| 100 | 44 | 19 |
| 200 | 24 | 10 |
| 400 | Target not reached | 5 |

Expansion can retain substantially more output at high capability. This is not
merely a one-point rounding anomaly. For 25-to-50, the small declaration's
Final Rating is 627 and the direct declaration's is 376. Buying expansion costs
five Effects, equivalent to fifty margin points, which is much less than the
251-point Rating difference. Both routes reach the target on every ordinary
successful roll in this fixture.

Remaining Effects retain only their legitimate uses; this is not automatic harm
or authority to redirect an area-only contributor's output into damage. A
permitted damaging area effect may have materially greater remaining strength,
subject to existing target opposition and protection.

## Same Distance And Duration On Both Routes

Starting at 25, expanding to 50 or declaring 50 directly:

| Ordinary ratings / Axis | Shared demand | Expansion | Direct |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 / 5 | Distance D3 | 0.65% | 9% |
| 8 / 8 | Distance D3 | 13% | 38% |
| 10 / 10 | Distance D3 | 85% | 81% |
| 10 / 10 | Distance D3 + independent duration D1 | 31% | 48% |

For the all-10 route with distance D3, the small declaration faces total D4
and the direct declaration D5. At roll 40, both leave five Effects after
establishing 50 coverage. With duration D1 also added, both become harder,
and direct declaration is more reliable. This uses normal aggregation; it is
not a special surcharge or a ban on expansion at distance.

## Findings And Recommendation

The candidate supports two distinct choices. A lower-output character is
usually better off declaring a substantially larger footprint in advance;
a high-output character can produce enough surplus to expand efficiently.
Far greater enlargement and combined demands restore the usefulness of larger
declarations without an imposed ceiling. Neither route dominates every tested
capability, target, and circumstance.

However, this does not preserve a larger declaration's advantage in every case.
High output can make a smaller declaration both as reliable and substantially
stronger at the same resulting area. Small crossings of a band boundary may
also favor expansion at moderate capability. Both are actual design tradeoffs,
not problems fixed by saying that Effects were spent.

Recommend retaining 20% of original coverage as a viable working conversion
for adoption consideration, if the intended play experience accepts this
capability-dependent advantage. It lets strong characters exploit surplus,
while others must commit to a harder declaration to seek the same large scope.
Do not add a ceiling solely to ensure that direct declaration is always better.
If that high-output advantage is unacceptable, a substantive conversion or
declaration-boundary decision is needed; silently changing coverage to radius,
reintroducing entry costs, or adding post-roll Difficulty would not be neutral.

The immediate user decision is whether to accept this tradeoff and adopt the
conversion as a working rule. No growth ceiling, unlimited permission, or area
Difficulty lookup is adopted by this test. Team pooling, separate sources of
Resonance, and further enhancements can increase attainable output and are
not capped by the controlled one-Axis fixture of 640.

## Verification

The accompanying CJS uses exact rational arithmetic for Final Ratings and the
existing tested Effect-distribution helper for ordinary rolls and natural-01
continuations. Its omitted continuation probability is 1e-12; extremely small
displayed probabilities can round to zero without meaning impossible Access.
JSON results preserve numeric values and assumptions. Target-weighted remaining
Effects in that JSON count zero when the requested larger target is not reached,
not when the actual smaller manifestation is absent.

All 264 comparisons and assertions pass, including linear growth, required
expenditures, conservation, ordinary-roll thresholds, low/high crossovers, and
selected output examples. This is a mathematical declaration comparison, not
a completed encounter or player-behavior playtest.
