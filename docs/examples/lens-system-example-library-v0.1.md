## Purpose

This library preserves examples that explain, test, or expose LENS rules.

The examples are not decorative additions to the system. They show how the
system's abstract language becomes usable fiction and help reveal whether a
proposed rule operates consistently across ordinary, extraordinary, individual,
team, environmental, and countering situations.

An example does not create doctrine by itself. When an example conflicts with
an authority document, the authority document controls until the conflict is
deliberately resolved.

MOTURPG-specific cosmology, characters, artifacts, Domains, Nodes, factions,
and lore are excluded from this system-neutral library.

---

## Example Reading Rule

Each example records:

```text
Rule Demonstrated
The rule or design question being illustrated.

Narrative Functions
What each relevant Lens contributes fictionally.

Dependencies
Which contributions require other contributions to function.

Resolution
How the current LENS architecture handles the situation.

Design Finding
What the example establishes or exposes.
```

Status labels used in this library:

```text
ESTABLISHED
Drawn from locked or high-confidence working doctrine.

CURRENT DISCUSSION CONCLUSION
Conceptually answered in current development but not yet integrated into the
system baseline.

EXPLORATORY
Useful for testing, but not an adopted procedure or mathematical rule.

UNRESOLVED
The example exposes a question the system has not yet answered.
```

# PART I
# UNIVERSAL LENS APPLICABILITY

## Example 1: Opening a Military Lock

Status:

```text
ESTABLISHED APPLICABILITY EXAMPLE
FINAL RATING MATH NOT REPRODUCED HERE
```

### Rule Demonstrated

Every Lens requires narrative applicability, but several applicable Lenses do
not automatically create a Compound Expression.

### Scenario

A character attempts to open a military lock in darkness.

### Narrative Functions

```text
Character
Supplies the acting Source.

Mobility
Supplies the external physical locus of control.

Coordination
Focuses precise physical manipulation.

Security Skill
Supplies learned knowledge of locks and security mechanisms.

Military Locks Specialization
Supplies narrow mastery when the lock falls within that specialization.

Lockpicks
Supply the usable physical implement.

Military Lock
Supplies Task Dissonance through its resistant construction.

Darkness
Supplies Environmental Dissonance when vision matters.
```

### Dependencies

The attempt may depend upon usable tools unless the character possesses another
Lens that permits an alternative method.

The Specialization depends upon the lock actually falling within its scope.

### Resolution

All genuinely applicable Lenses shape one Expression:

```text
Open the lock.
```

The character makes one uncertainty roll using the current Final Rating
procedure.

### Design Finding

Layering is not the same as compounding. This Expression contains several
Lenses, but they focus one function rather than creating several separately
resolvable manifestations.

## Example 2: Synonym Stacking

Status:

```text
ESTABLISHED HIGH-CONFIDENCE WORKING RULE
```

### Rule Demonstrated

Each counted Lens must contribute something fictionally distinct.

### Scenario

A player proposes:

```text
Protection
+ Defense
+ Guardianship
```

### Narrative Functions

If all three terms describe the same defensive work, they supply one
contribution rather than three.

### Dependencies

None. The issue is duplicate description rather than dependency.

### Resolution

Count only the fictionally distinct contribution.

### Design Finding

Narrative language cannot manufacture modifier inflation. A new name is not a
new Lens contribution unless it performs different work.

## Example 3: Strength and Extraordinary Permission

Status:

```text
ESTABLISHED PERMISSION BOUNDARY
```

### Rule Demonstrated

A Derived Attribute focuses ordinary capability but does not independently
grant extraordinary permission.

### Scenario

```text
High Strength
```

is proposed as sufficient permission to catch a mountain.

### Narrative Functions

```text
Strength
Focuses the Might Attribute toward producing physical force.

Classification, Scale, HOLT, Access, and other relevant Lenses
May establish whether mountain-catching is possible and at what natural frame.
```

### Dependencies

The extraordinary attempt depends upon whatever permission-bearing and
scope-bearing Lenses the setting requires.

### Resolution

Strength contributes when relevant, but Strength alone does not create the
extraordinary permission.

### Design Finding

Narrative applicability does not erase layer boundaries. Each Lens performs
the work assigned to its layer.

# PART II
# DIFFICULTY, ENVIRONMENT, AND COUNTER-LENSES

## Example 4: Routine and Difficult Climbing

Status:

```text
ESTABLISHED WORKING DIFFICULTY DOCTRINE
VALUES UNRESOLVED
```

### Rule Demonstrated

Routine is neutral. Difficulty is Dissonance. Ease is Resonance, a threshold
decision, or no roll.

### Scenario

```text
Routine climb
Rain-slick climb
Sheer cliff
Collapsing cliff during conflict
```

### Narrative Functions

```text
Routine conditions
Introduce no additional Task Dissonance.

Rain
Creates relevant environmental interference.

Sheer construction
Creates inherent Task Dissonance through precision and resistance.

Collapse during conflict
Adds instability, danger, and time pressure when relevant.
```

### Dependencies

Only conditions genuinely affecting the declared climb enter its Dissonance.

### Resolution

All relevant Task and Condition Dissonance enters the common Dissonance
procedure. The exact values remain unresolved.

### Design Finding

Difficulty is not a separate subsystem or untyped number. It is resistance
expressed through relevant Dissonance Lenses.

## Example 5: Darkness and Night Vision

Status:

```text
ESTABLISHED HIGH-CONFIDENCE COUNTER-LENS DOCTRINE
```

### Rule Demonstrated

Scene conditions remain objective. Characters answer them through their own
Lenses.

### Scenario

```text
Scene:
Darkness creates Environmental Dissonance.

Character A:
No answering Lens.

Character B:
Night Vision provides relevant Resonance or permission.
```

### Narrative Functions

```text
Darkness
Interferes with Expressions requiring ordinary vision.

Night Vision
Answers that interference according to its established fictional scope.
```

### Dependencies

Night Vision contributes only when the Expression actually depends upon seeing
through the darkness.

### Resolution

The scene's darkness is not recalculated for each character. Character B's
Lens answers the same objective condition that affects Character A.

### Design Finding

The world does not change around different characters. Their available Lenses
change how they can answer the world.

# PART III
# HOLT, AXIS, AND ACCESS

## Example 6: Sea Across Several Axes

Status:

```text
ESTABLISHED HOLT AND AXIS ARCHITECTURE
```

### Rule Demonstrated

A HOLT grants coherent thematic permission and may express through multiple
Derived Attribute Axes without becoming a universal multiplier.

### Scenario

```text
Sea -> Movement
Sea -> Awareness
Sea -> Connection
Sea -> Control
```

### Narrative Functions

```text
Sea -> Movement
Supports movement through, upon, or by means of the Sea.

Sea -> Awareness
Supports perception and understanding of relevant marine conditions.

Sea -> Connection
Supports relationships with relevant marine beings or systems.

Sea -> Control
Supports outward direction or manipulation of relevant Sea expressions.
```

### Dependencies

Each manifestation depends upon coherent thematic applicability and the
applicable Axis Access.

### Resolution

Each Axis is ranked or unranked and has Open, Condition, or Closed Access. No
Axis may exceed its parent HOLT Rank.

### Design Finding

The same Theme can produce distinct characters because Axis development and
Access determine where the Theme is focused.

## Example 7: Riding and Attacking With One Wave

Status:

```text
CONCEPTUALLY LOCKED
```

### Rule Demonstrated

One character creating this Compound Expression uses one roll. Two genuinely
required higher-order Axis contributions use the arithmetic mean of their
applicable modifiers, within one HOLT or across different HOLTs.

### Scenario

```text
Sea -> Movement Rank 4: x1.4 in this example
Sea -> Control Rank 2: x1.2 in this example
```

The character wants to ride a wave while using that same wave to attack.

### Narrative Functions

```text
Movement
Carries the character upon the wave.

Control
Directs the wave as an attack.
```

### Dependencies

The complete declared Expression requires both Movement and Control.

### Resolution

```text
Ride the wave:
Movement Rank 4 applies.

Ride and attack with the wave:
Movement and Control are required.
Combined higher-order modifier = (1.4 + 1.2) / 2 = x1.3.
```

The Axes do not receive separate success thresholds within the one action.

### Design Finding

The mean reduces this compound's modifier relative to Movement alone while
permitting the combined function. It does not average the ordinary Expression
layers or duplicate earned Effect Levels. Both functions require Access.

If Control were Open but unranked, its x1.0 would produce a combined x1.2.
If both modifiers were x1.4, the combined modifier would remain x1.4, without
an invented splitting-focus penalty. Closed Control would prevent this combined
declaration, but would not by itself remove permission to ride the wave alone.

The former minimum-based result is superseded for this two-Axis example.
No formula for three or more contributors is established here.

## Example 8: Fire Underwater

Status:

```text
CURRENT DISCUSSION CONCLUSION
ACCESS VALUES AND SETTING TRANSLATION UNRESOLVED
```

### Rule Demonstrated

Narrative applicability may be established at a higher layer and inherited by
lower layers. Access and immediate Expression dependency are distinct.

### Scenario

A submerged character attempts to create Fire.

### Narrative Functions

```text
HOLD
Establishes what Fire means in the setting and what generally permits or
disrupts it.

Fire HOLT and Axis
Establish the character's thematic and routed access.

Underwater environment
Establishes the objective scene condition.
```

### Dependencies

The attempted Fire Expression depends upon the current Access state.

### Resolution

Possible existing Access relationships include:

```text
Closed Access
This Fire cannot manifest underwater.

Condition Access
The character must satisfy an established condition.

Open Access with Dissonance
Fire remains possible but the environment resists it.

Open Access
This Fire is established as capable of operating underwater.
```

### Design Finding

The correct diagnostic is not merely which immediate Expression is declared.
It is:

```text
At what layer is the relationship established, and which lower layers inherit
its consequences?
```

# PART IV
# ONE-SOURCE COMPOUND EXPRESSIONS

## Example 9: Fire Shield

Status:

```text
CURRENT DISCUSSION CONCLUSION
FINAL COMPOUND MATH UNRESOLVED
```

### Rule Demonstrated

Primary function and load-bearing manifestation are different questions.
Narrative dependency determines whether a component can survive the loss of
another component.

### Scenario

A character creates a shield made from Fire.

### Narrative Functions

```text
Protection
Supplies the defensive function.

Fire
Supplies the actual medium through which the defense manifests.
```

### Dependencies

```text
Protection-as-a-fire-shield depends upon Fire.

Fire does not necessarily depend upon Protection in order to exist.
```

### Resolution

```text
Fire is unavailable or countered:
The declared fire shield cannot exist.

Protection is countered:
Fire may remain, but it is not functioning as a successful shield.
```

Against ordinary arrows, Fire does not automatically improve defense merely
because it is the shield's medium.

Against a frost attack, Fire may also become relevant Resonance when it
directly answers the resistance.

### Design Finding

The fiction determines whether a Lens is an independent effect, contextual
Resonance, or a load-bearing requirement. The system must follow that
relationship rather than treating every named Lens as an optional bonus.

## Example 10: Net Launcher With Winch

Status:

```text
CURRENT DISCUSSION TEST
NO HOLT REQUIRED
```

### Rule Demonstrated

Compound Expressions are not a HOLT-only subsystem. Ordinary equipment,
Skills, and functions can create the same dependency structure.

### Scenario

A character uses a net launcher with a winch to capture a target and pull the
target closer.

### Narrative Functions

```text
Launcher
Delivers the net.

Net
Restrains the target.

Winch
Moves the restrained target.
```

### Dependencies

```text
Restraint depends upon Delivery.

Winch Movement depends upon Restraint.

Delivery does not depend upon either later function.
```

### Resolution

The character supplies one Source and one Compound Expression. The exact
controlling rating for mixed non-HOLT components remains unresolved.

Counters follow the declared structure:

```text
Avoid the net:
No Restraint and therefore no Winch Movement.

Cut the net after capture:
Restraint and Winch Movement end.

Disable the winch:
The target may remain restrained but is not pulled closer.
```

### Design Finding

The unit being resolved is the character's Expression, not each individual
Lens. Every Lens still requires narrative applicability and a distinct
mechanical function.

# PART V
# MULTIPLE-SOURCE COMPOUND EXPRESSIONS

## Example 11: Directed Firestorm

Status:

```text
CURRENT DISCUSSION CONCLUSION
EFFECT VALUES UNRESOLVED
```

### Rule Demonstrated

Multiple acting Sources each commit an action and roll their contributing
Expression separately. Narrative declaration determines what each successful
contribution changes.

### Scenario

```text
Character A:
Creates Fire.

Character B:
Creates and directs Wind.

Combined objective:
Create a directed firestorm across a larger area.
```

### Narrative Functions

```text
Fire
Supplies heat, ignition, and burning effect.

Wind
Supplies delivery, direction, and expanded area.
```

### Dependencies

The directed firestorm requires both contributions. Fire and Wind may each
remain viable independently.

### Resolution

```text
Both succeed:
The directed firestorm occurs.

Fire succeeds and Wind fails:
Fire remains localized.

Wind succeeds and Fire fails:
Wind manifests without burning.

Both fail:
No contributing Expression succeeds.
```

### Design Finding

A second action does not automatically improve chance, magnitude, range,
duration, and every other outcome. It focuses only the aspect justified by
its declared narrative function.

## Example 12: Wave-Delivered Sea Creatures

Status:

```text
CURRENT DISCUSSION TEST
```

### Rule Demonstrated

Multiple Sources may create directional dependency between separately rolled
Expressions.

### Scenario

```text
Character A:
Creates and controls a wave.

Character B:
Commands sea creatures to attack within the wave.
```

### Narrative Functions

```text
Wave
Supplies delivery to the target.

Sea-creature command
Supplies the attacking creatures.
```

### Dependencies

In the declared situation, delivery of the creatures depends upon the wave.
The wave does not depend upon the creatures.

### Resolution

```text
Both succeed:
The wave delivers the attacking creatures.

Wave succeeds and creature command fails:
The wave remains without the coordinated creature attack.

Wave fails and creature command succeeds:
The creatures answer, but the declared wave delivery does not occur.

Both fail:
No contributing Expression succeeds.
```

If the creatures possess another established route to the target, the stated
dependency changes. That alternative cannot be invented after resolution.

### Design Finding

Component success does not guarantee success of the combined objective.
Narrative dependency determines whether a successful contribution can perform
its intended work.

## Example 13: Two Characters and One Gate

Status:

```text
CURRENT DISCUSSION CONCLUSION
GENERAL EFFECT VALUES UNRESOLVED
```

### Rule Demonstrated

Two successful contributions may reinforce the same objective while focusing
different aspects of the outcome.

### Scenario

Two characters commit their actions to lifting or holding one heavy gate so
others can escape beneath it.

### Narrative Functions

The characters must state how their Expressions work together.

Possible declarations include:

```text
Magnitude
Together they lift a gate too heavy for either character alone.

Duration
One character can raise the gate, while the second helps hold it open long
enough for others to pass.

Stability
One character supplies the lift while the other prevents twisting, slipping,
or collapse.

Clearance
The second contribution helps raise the gate farther rather than holding it
longer.
```

### Dependencies

Dependencies follow the selected declaration. A magnitude-based lift may
require both contributions before the gate moves. A duration contribution may
depend upon the initial lift but continue to matter after the gate is raised.

### Resolution

Each acting Source rolls its contributing Expression separately.

Successful contributions create Lenses that focus only the declared aspect of
the shared outcome. They do not automatically improve every aspect.

### Design Finding

The existing Lens rule answers the compound question:

```text
If a contribution changes chance, access, effect, cost, scale, recovery, or
opposition, it is a Lens.
```

Narrative function identifies what the Lens changes. The unresolved work is
general Lens valuation, not a special team-action subsystem.

# PART VI
# RELATIONSHIPS, COUNTERS, AND RESIDUAL EXPRESSIONS

## Example 14: Three Counter Targets

Status:

```text
CURRENT DISCUSSION CONCLUSION
RELATIONSHIP RESISTANCE UNRESOLVED
```

### Rule Demonstrated

A multiple-Source Compound Expression may contain three separately relevant
counter targets.

### Scenario

Two characters create a combined Expression through a fictionally established
relationship.

### Narrative Functions

```text
Target 1
Character A and Character A's contributing Expression.

Target 2
Character B and Character B's contributing Expression.

Target 3
The Lens connecting the two Expressions into the combined result.
```

### Dependencies

The combined result depends upon the connecting Lens. The individual
Expressions may or may not depend upon that relationship for independent
function.

### Resolution

Countering the connecting Lens:

- does not automatically suppress either character;
- does not automatically cancel either individual Expression;
- prevents the Expressions from producing their combined result;
- leaves independently viable Residual Expressions;
- removes dependent results that cannot function without the connection.

The connection may represent coordination, timing, delivery, alignment, a
shared medium, a conduit, formation, or another established relationship.

### Design Finding

Because the relationship changes the combined effect, it is itself a Lens and
may be addressed through the ordinary Counter-Lens grammar. Its exact rating or
resistance remains unresolved.

## Example 15: Installed Brace as a Stable Lens

Status:

```text
CURRENT DISCUSSION TEST
STABLE-LENS PERSISTENCE ESTABLISHED
```

### Rule Demonstrated

A successful contribution may create a stable Lens that persists after the
acting Source stops maintaining it.

### Scenario

One character lifts a gate while another installs a physical brace.

### Narrative Functions

```text
Strength Expression
Creates the opening.

Engineering Expression
Installs the brace.

Brace
Transfers the ongoing load from the character to a persistent physical Lens.
```

### Dependencies

Installing the brace depends upon the gate being lifted far enough for
installation.

Once installed, the brace does not depend upon the original lifter continuing
to act.

### Resolution

The installed brace remains until the fiction disables, destroys, removes,
materially changes, or makes it inapplicable.

Breaking the brace ends its contribution. Damaging it may impose relevant
Dissonance without automatically removing it.

### Design Finding

The compound procedure explains how the stable Lens entered the scene.
Stable-Lens Persistence already explains what happens afterward.

# PART VII
# RECOVERY AND MATCHING COUNTER-LENSES

## Example 16: Typed Recovery

Status:

```text
APPROVED WORKING DOCTRINE
CURRENT EFFECT PROCEDURE ACCEPTED FOR TESTING
FINAL TIME AND SETTING VALUES UNRESOLVED
```

### Rule Demonstrated

Recovery applies a relevant counter-Lens to matching Dissonance rather than
restoring an undifferentiated resource by default.

### Scenario

```text
Restrained
Wounded
Exhausted
Corrupted
```

### Narrative Functions

```text
Restrained
May be answered by escape, Strength, a cutting tool, assistance, or changed
position.

Wounded
May be answered by medicine, rest, regeneration, repair, or another matching
Lens.

Exhausted
May be answered by rest, nourishment, relief, or another matching Lens.

Corrupted
May require purification, stabilization, restoration, or another setting-
appropriate matching Lens.
```

### Dependencies

The recovery Lens must genuinely address the cause or continuing effect of the
Dissonance.

### Resolution

Declare which Condition is being answered and whether the recovery Expression
stabilizes, removes, converts, or restores Access.

Current testing uses:

```text
Successful Recovery Expression
= 1 baseline matching recovery Effect Level
+ 1 additional level for each complete 10 points of Successful Margin
```

The method determines what one attempt means in the fiction. First aid may
stabilize Bleeding without removing Wounded levels. Surgery may remove Wounded
levels over a treatment interval. Rest may remove Exhausted levels.
Regeneration may grant permission to remove Wounded levels directly and
rapidly. Repair may remove matching Damaged or Jammed equipment Conditions.

### Design Finding

Recovery remains inside the one-system principle. Different fictional harms
use the same Effect grammar while requiring different relevant answers. No
generic healing outcome or universal recovery duration is required.

## Example 17: Energy-Conversion Armor

Status:

```text
APPROVED WORKING EXAMPLE
EXACT CONVERSION AND STORAGE VALUES UNRESOLVED
```

### Rule Demonstrated

Each Effect Level follows one traceable path. Conversion, redirection, and
storage change an Effect without duplicating it.

### Scenario

An armor Lens can intercept part of an incoming Electrical Effect, convert it
into Stored Charge, and later use that charge to enhance a compatible
Expression.

### Resolution

```text
Incoming Electrical Effect: 4 levels

Converted to Stored Charge:  2 levels
Mitigated:                    1 level
Applied Electrical Effect:   1 level
                              --------
Total accounted for:         4 levels
```

Later:

```text
Generated Effect from a compatible Expression: 3 levels
Stored Charge consumed as enhancement:          2 levels
Applied Effect:                                  5 levels
Stored Charge remaining:                         0 levels
```

### Design Finding

Conversion changes what an Effect becomes. Redirection changes its target.
Storage changes when it becomes available. None of these functions creates
additional Effect unless another applicable Lens explicitly supplies Effect
Enhancement.

The same grammar may describe armor, wards, kinetic absorbers, reactors,
environmental systems, or character capabilities.

## Example 18: Famous Champion

Status:

```text
APPROVED WORKING EXAMPLE
CHARACTER-CREATION VALUES UNRESOLVED
```

### Rule Demonstrated

A stable character Lens is not inherently a merit or flaw. Its relationship
to the current Expression and fiction determines its role.

### Narrative Functions

```text
Famous Champion

May supply Resonance for public leadership and recognition.
May grant Access to supporters or institutions.
May supply Dissonance against disguise, anonymity, or covert travel.
May expose the Character to identification by enemies.
```

### Design Finding

The complete Lens is one character feature. Its benefits and naturally
connected drawbacks are not purchased or compensated separately. Unrelated
drawbacks remain separate Lenses when they perform genuinely distinct work.

# PART VIII
# CURRENT CROSS-EXAMPLE FINDINGS

The examples currently support the following system-wide statements:

```text
Every Lens requires narrative applicability.

Every counted Lens must have a distinct mechanical consequence.

Several applicable Lenses do not automatically create a Compound Expression.

A Compound Expression contains multiple fictionally distinguishable
contributions, functions, manifestations, or acting Sources.

One acting Source rolls once for its complete declared Expression.

When exactly two higher-order Axis contributions are genuinely required, use
the arithmetic mean of their modifiers, within one HOLT or across different
HOLTs. Both require Access and distinct work; Effect Levels are not duplicated.

Multiple acting Sources each commit an action and roll their contributing
Expressions separately.

Each contribution must state what aspect of the shared outcome it changes.

Additional contributions do not automatically improve every outcome dimension.

Narrative dependency determines whether a successful contribution can function
without another contribution.

The relationship connecting contributions may itself be a Lens and a counter
target.

Successful contributions may create temporary, active, or stable Lenses
according to the fiction.

Existing Applicability, Resonance, Dissonance, Access, Counter-Lens, and Stable-
Lens rules should answer compound situations before a new subsystem is created.
```

# PART IX
# OPEN EXAMPLE NEEDS

Future examples should test:

- a one-Source compound built from several HOLTs with unequal Axis Ranks;
- a one-Source compound mixing ordinary equipment and extraordinary Access;
- a multiple-Source compound in which all contributors increase magnitude;
- a multiple-Source compound focused on duration rather than magnitude;
- a relationship Lens being directly countered;
- a Compound Expression with Condition Access at more than one layer;
- a social Compound Expression;
- an investigative Compound Expression;
- an organizational or Infrastructure Compound Expression;
- Scale differences among contributors;
- general effect valuation once that procedure exists;
- the rating or resistance of a connecting Lens;
- timing when a dependent contribution cannot begin until another contribution
  succeeds;
- recovery applied to a disrupted relationship rather than to either Source.

These are development targets, not missing bespoke subsystems.
