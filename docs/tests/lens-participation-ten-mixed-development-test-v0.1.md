# Participation 10: Mixed Development Test

Subsequent decision: Participation 10 was adopted with the three core packages
on 2026-09-13. See `../design/lens-core-three-package-adoption-v0.1.md`.
The original comparison and pending-decision language below remain historical.

Date: 2026-09-13
Status: Comparison completed; no award change adopted.

## Recommendation

Recommend Participation 10 as the next working award, retaining the four
discretionary categories at 0/3/5 and all tested prices. The mixed plans support
small purchases and longer-term improvements while leaving meaningful saving
choices. This is a recommendation for a faster base pace, not proof of player
satisfaction or complete encounter balance.

Its effect is not confined to funding immediate help. Everyone participating
earns five more, including players who save everything. Over twelve sessions
that is exactly 60 extra points, the cost of one Attribute rank. Do not erase
the increase by automatically raising prices or reducing discretionary awards.

## Controls And Boundaries

Assume Packages 1 and 2 together: additive Attribute plus Derived, unranked HOLT
Access at 40, and Axis development at 15 per rank. Ordinary prices remain
Attribute 60, Derived 20, Task/Field 10, Specialty/Precision 5. No changes to
main adoption, protected creation allocations, character balances, or conversion.

This test compares advancement paths, not new complete character builds or
edits to the saved shopkeeper, guide, or medical specialist. Each illustrative
fixture begins with Attribute 5, two relevant Derived Attributes 3, and an
applicable Task, Field, Specialty, and Precision at 5. A new Field starts at 0;
no HOLT or Axis ranks are initially purchased. Other character capabilities
are held outside this bounded comparison.

The same fixed purchase priorities are followed under both awards. They are
plausible choices, not optimal allocations or mandatory rules. A character waits
for the next chosen purchase rather than automatically buying whatever cheap
rank is currently affordable. Changing that preference changes dates.

All named purchases are assumed narratively eligible at the relevant scene
transitions. A session award does not itself grant permission or justify a
mid-scene improvement. The two HOLT Axes are Open and support the specified
routes; the second HOLT supplies different permitted options. Its thematic
eligibility is assumed, not granted to every character by the test.

Each fixture starts with eight already-held unrestricted points and chooses to
retain eight as a buffer for future play. This is a common opening balance, not
a new starting grant, a required reserve, or a second currency. Help is paid
before the end-session award; purchases use the resulting actual balance. The
script prohibits borrowing and preserves every remainder. Two zero-opening
checks confirm that an unfunded first-session help request cannot be paid from
its future award.

Immediate spending is modeled at 0, 4, or 8 per session, assuming enough distinct
eligible uses. These are not recommended quotas, measured behavior, or permission
to repeat a boost on the same Expression. No milestones, story grants, acquisition
discounts, or forfeitures are included.

## Twelve Sessions With Four Points Spent Each Session

Both awards fund the same 48 points of immediate help. After restoring the
unchanged opening reserve, 120 versus 180 points remain for purchases and savings.

| Development path | Participation 5 | Participation 10 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Broad ordinary development | Task +1, Field +1, Specialty +1, two Derived increases; 55 saved toward Attribute | Same initial improvements, Attribute +1, new Field 3, another Specialty +1 and primary Derived +1 |
| Concentrated HOLT development | Access, Axis 2, Task +1, Field +1, Derived +1, Specialty +1; 5 saved | Access, Axis 4, Task +1, Field +1, Derived +2, Specialty +1, Precision +1; 10 saved |
| Broader HOLT development | Access, Axes 2/1, Task +1, second Derived +1, Specialty +1 | Access, Axes 2/2, same ordinary improvements, new Field 3; 15 saved toward another HOLT |

Savings in this table are above the eight-point operating buffer, which remains
held by each fixture. Extra growth must not be measured from rank expenditure
alone: the old ordinary plan has 55 banked, not 55 lost.

### Broad Ordinary Plan

Its ordered first purchases are Task +1 (10), primary Derived +1 (20), Specialty
+1 (5), Field +1 (10), second Derived +1 (20), then Attribute +1 (60).
It next develops a new Field to 3 (30), another Specialty rank (5), and another
primary Derived rank (20). The full listed sequence costs 180.

At Participation 5 and four help points spent per session, it completes the
first 65 points of small purchases by session 7, then saves 55 by session 12.
The Attribute is the next chosen purchase, not an inaccessible option; continuing
the same pace would buy it in session 13. Prioritizing it earlier could buy it
earlier under either award.

At Participation 10, it completes those small purchases by session 5, raises
the Attribute in session 9, reaches new Field 3 in session 11, and purchases
the final Derived improvement in session 12. This is not merely a narrow-rank
acceleration: it supports a broad purchase and a new interest while using help.

### Concentrated HOLT Plan

It first improves a Task, saves for Access, and alternates Axis purchases with
ordinary development. Access arrives in session 5 versus 4; first Axis rank in
session 7 versus 5. After twelve sessions the primary Axis is 2 versus 4, alongside
the improvements listed above. The second version is stronger, not fully developed.

### Broader HOLT Plan

It buys Access first, then alternates two Axes and ordinary improvements before
developing a new Field and saving for a second HOLT. Access arrives in session
4 versus 3. The larger award reaches Axes 2/2 in session 9 and new Field 3 in
session 11. It has 15 saved toward second Access at session 12; that permission
has not yet been purchased.

## Capability, Not Just Ranks

The shared initial signature raw Expression is 40.5. The following numbers use
the additive foundation, actual purchased training ranks, and one applicable
Axis. Context is neutral, Function complete, Scale matched, and no equipment
modifier is added. D1 is a required-check control, not a claim that these
activities always need a roll.

| Mixed path, four help points per session | Raw output, Participation 5 / 10 | D1 Final Rating, 5 / 10 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Ordinary | 55.30 / 71.81 | 54 / 70 |
| Concentrated HOLT | 66.36 / 91.75 | 65 / 89 |
| Broader HOLT | 55.30 / 55.30 | 54 / 54 |

The broader HOLT plan illustrates why a maximum Expression is insufficient:
its second Axis and new Field improve without changing this primary signature.
These are not claims of equal fictional value or an optimized ordinary-versus-
extraordinary contest. Different priorities buy different coverage.

The mathematical improvement from Participation 10 is meaningful. It does not
merely disguise unchanged growth behind more points, and increased intermediate
capability is already an intended effect of the assumed engine proposals.
No encounter or opposed-output simulation is newly claimed here.

## Savers And Heavier Spending

| Immediate spending per session | Retained over twelve sessions, Participation 5 / 10 |
| --- | ---: |
| 0 | 168 / 228 |
| 4 | 120 / 180 |
| 8 | 72 / 132 |

These totals include development expenditure and unspent advancement savings,
excluding the unchanged opening buffer. With the reference awards the increase
is 35.7 percent more net growth for savers, 50 percent more for four-point
spenders, and 83.3 percent more for eight-point spenders.

The absolute saver-versus-spender gap is unchanged: spending four still costs
48 advancement points over twelve sessions. Its relative burden falls. The
larger award does not reimburse, reward, or require immediate spending.

In the mixed plans, no-spend concentrated HOLT development reaches Axis 4 versus
6. No-spend broader development reaches a second HOLT in session 11 only under
Participation 10. No mixed run reaches Axis 10 within twelve sessions.

Under heavy spending, the ordinary plan reaches Attribute 6 at session 12 only
with Participation 10. The HOLT-focused plan reaches Axis 1 versus 3. It remains
a meaningful choice to spend heavily now rather than develop faster.

### Concentration Control

A player choosing to save exclusively can progress faster than these mixed plans:

| Target | Participation 5, no help | Participation 10, no help | Participation 10, four help |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Specialty 0 to 10, eligible parent already held | 4 sessions | 3 | 4 |
| Attribute +1 | 5 | 4 | 4 |
| New HOLT plus Axis 10 | 14 | 10 | 13 |

The three-session Specialty remains the clearest narrow-development caution.
That does not imply mastery of the whole character. A player spending no points
on help should not be treated as doing something wrong; the larger award must
be acceptable for that player as well.

## Sensitivity And Interpretation

The varied sequence retains the main conclusions. With four-point spending,
ordinary Attribute development still arrives at session 9 only under Participation
10; concentrated HOLT Access changes from session 6 to 4; broader Access from
5 to 3. Final purchased ranks match the corresponding constant-reference runs,
with two more points remaining. Award timing can change purchase dates even
when total awards are similar.

No requested help went unfunded in these 36 runs with the stated opening balance.
That is not a universal guarantee for a long run of low-award sessions. The
zero-opening controls correctly deny first-session requests before an award.

Increasing Participation improves the dependable floor. It does not correct
unfair discretionary recognition or erase differences in narrative opportunity.
Participation plus all four meaningful categories gives 22, so discretionary
recognition still contributes more than half that total. Keep the distinctions
and non-performance safeguards rather than forcing everyone to a total of 19.

Do not add milestones or story rewards to rescue an otherwise undesired pace.
They remain optional additional growth, excluded here so the Participation
change can be judged on its own.

## Decision

Recommend adopting Participation 10 as a working award while leaving 0/3/5
categories and recipient guidance unchanged. It better supports the stated mix
of immediate narrative spending, small development, and larger purchases.
The tradeoff is explicitly faster progression for everyone, including savers.
No proportional repricing, reimbursement, new pool, or replacement advancement
gate is warranted by these results.

The user must decide whether that faster reference is desirable. Until approved,
the main card remains Participation 5. Adoption of this award would not by itself
adopt Packages 1 and 2 or settle their remaining conversion/provision questions.
