# Surplus Duration At Twenty Percent

Date: 2026-09-14
Status: user-authorized comparison only. No conversion or ceiling adopted.

Subsequent decision, 2026-09-14: the user approved 20% of the original declared
duration per additional Effect as the working noncompounding conversion, without
a universal growth ceiling. Baseline and terminology record the rule. The test
below retains its original comparison status and assumptions; intermediate
duration boundaries and starting increments were not adopted by this decision.

Further decision, 2026-09-14: after clarification, the user confirmed the tested
initial covering-band lookup with whole Difficulty bands, not fractional bands.
The core references now adopt this lookup separately. Original test assumptions
below remain as the historical comparison record; starting increments remain open.

## Candidate And Controls

Test each additional Effect allocated to duration as 20% of the ORIGINAL
declared duration:

```text
Resulting duration = declared duration x (1 + 0.20 x Effects allocated)
```

Growth is additive against the original reference, not compounded against the
last extension. For this comparison, success establishes the permitted declared
duration without an extra entry expenditure; retain its initial successful
Effect and spend additional Effects once. This is a comparison assumption,
not adoption of a universal duration-entry procedure.

The adopted finite ladder references one applicable increment at D1, ten at
D2, one hundred at D3, and so on. The Profile or applicable Lens supplies the
increment consistently across scenes. No seconds, minutes, or other starting
unit is adopted here. Intermediate durations use the next covering band ONLY
for this test: for example, more than one and up to ten increments uses D2.
Boundary treatment remains unresolved. Comparisons between exact tenfold
references do not depend on that intermediate-boundary assumption.

Distinct shared challenge bands add before the Difficulty curve is applied
once. Final Rating is floored once from raw Expression x 0.98 x 0.6^(D - 1).
The six controlled routes use A + D and ordinary training ranks all at 3, 5,
5, 8, 10, and 10, with respective applicable Axis ranks 3, 0, 5, 8, 0, and 10.
Their pre-Difficulty Expressions are 22.27758, 50.625, 75.9375, 302.33088,
320, and 640. These are controlled routes, not complete equal-budget builds.
Axis 0 retains defined Open Access; 640 is not a universal output ceiling.

All attempts are permitted, independently persisting manifestations, at matched
Scale and full Function, without equipment, context, opposition, mitigation, or
paid spending. This does not require a roll for otherwise routine certainty.
Natural 01 and its repeated continuations are included; 00 supplies no successful
Effects. Exceptional success does not automatically supply the desired surplus.

The script checks 450 combinations: six routes, shared challenge bands 0/3/4,
original durations 1/5/10/50/100 increments, and targets at 1.2/2/5/10/100 times
the original duration. All total Difficulties remain within D1-D10.

## What Each Effect Buys

| Additional Effects allocated | Total duration | If the original declaration were 10 minutes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 100% | 10 minutes |
| 1 | 120% | 12 minutes |
| 5 | 200% | 20 minutes |
| 20 | 500% | 50 minutes |
| 45 | 1,000% | 100 minutes |
| 495 | 10,000% | 1,000 minutes |

The minutes column illustrates arithmetic only, not a Profile increment or
Difficulty assignment. Five additional Effects double the original duration;
they do not advance a full duration band. Reaching the next tenfold reference
requires 45 additional Effects, or 46 generated Effects including success.

## Equal-Target Comparison

Duration alone, starting at one increment on the extension route:

| Ordinary ranks / Axis | Target increments | Extend shorter declaration | Declare target directly |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 / 5 | 1.2 | 64% | 44% |
| 5 / 5 | 2 | 24% | 44% |
| 5 / 5 | 10 | 0.0000002% | 44% |
| 8 / 8 | 2 | 99% | 99% |
| 10 / 10 | 2 | 99% | 99% |
| 10 / 10 | 10 | 99% | 99% |

The 1.2-increment comparison exposes the boundary advantage: under the test's
covering-band rule, declaring 1.2 takes the same D2 as declaring ten, whereas
extension only spends one Effect. The candidate can therefore reward a smaller
initial declaration. This is a real benefit, not a reason to pretend extension
and direct declaration have identical value.

These percentages measure reaching the target duration. Insufficient surplus
can still leave a successful shorter manifestation. Its remaining Effects may
support other permitted outcomes. The extension route also offers a post-roll
choice about allocation; the table does not price that flexibility.

## High Output And Remaining Effects

At raw Expression 640, duration D1 gives Final Rating 627; D2 gives 376. With
an ordinary roll of 40, the respective outputs are 59 and 34 Effects.

| Target from one increment | Extension Effects spent | Effects left after extension | Direct declaration Effects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.2 increments | 1 | 58 | 34 |
| 2 increments | 5 | 54 | 34 |
| 10 increments | 45 | 14 | 34 |

At this output, modest extension retains substantially more Effects than a
direct declaration across the boundary. For the full tenfold extension,
declaring longer retains twenty more Effects instead. Equal success probability
does not mean equal remaining output. Unspent Effects are not confined to time;
their actual narrative and mechanical applicability governs allocation.

With an additional shared D3 demand, these same routes face total D4 and D5,
giving Final Ratings 135 and 81. Doubling through surplus reaches its target
85% of the time versus 81% directly. A full tenfold extension falls to
0.00000081% versus 81% directly. Both contributors to an actual combined result
must still meet the challenge their contribution faces; this comparison grants
no exemption from distance or other real demands.

For all 108 tested jumps from exact references to ten or one hundred times
their duration, direct declaration is at least as likely to reach the target
and retains at least as many Effects on the same successful ordinary roll.
This is not a universal theorem for unrestricted Expressions or pooled output.
Beyond these fixtures, sufficiently large output can change the comparison.

## Within A Band

Under the covering-band assumption, five and ten increments both use D2.
Declaring ten initially has the same Difficulty as declaring five and costs
no surplus. Extending five to ten spends five additional Effects. Thus the
conversion supplies no numerical advantage within that band. A shorter initial
duration may nevertheless be exactly what the character wants in the fiction.

This also means any eventual boundary policy affects optimization incentives.
The current test does not settle whether intermediate declarations receive
covering-band treatment or another treatment.

## Preserved Boundaries

- A finite duration remains finite. Surplus does not purchase permanence or
  manufacture missing Access.
- An instantaneous result has no declared persistent lifetime to multiply.
  The test does not grant persistence by treating zero duration as a new seed.
- Independent persistence is not immunity to interruption or destruction.
- Extending an actively maintained effect does not remove its maintenance
  requirement or pay its substantial sustaining actions. This test concerns
  independent duration; maintained output retains existing dependencies.
- Natural continuation, such as a fire sustained by fuel, does not acquire a
  maintenance tax or a purchased lifetime through this comparison.
- Changing scene, starting another round, or restating the enlarged duration
  does not reset the original reference or grant another roll for free.
- One Effect cannot both extend duration and supply a second output. Other
  unspent Effects remain available for supported strength, control, or other
  outcomes; the initial purpose is not an exclusive allocation category.
- No universal time rounding, minimum temporal slice, conversion, or growth
  ceiling is adopted. The formula is unchanged by displaying time in seconds,
  minutes, or another unit; changing the actual Profile/Lens increment is a
  capability change, not a display conversion.

## Recommendation And Decision

Advance the 20%-of-original candidate as a useful fine-extension rule. It gives
surplus a meaningful use without making full tenfold duration declarations
redundant in the tested capability range. The evidence does not justify adding
a universal ceiling or losing control merely because a boundary is crossed.

The substantive choice is whether the advantage for modest, post-roll extension
is desirable. Recommendation: accept that tradeoff if adopting the conversion;
do not promise that declaring longer is always numerically preferable. Retain
the separate unresolved duration-entry wording and intermediate-band lookup.
No adopted rules, character balances, awards, or prices changed in this test.
