## Equal Budget Implications

At 120 points, either schedule permits an Attribute increase or four Task
increases if the selected entries have sufficient headroom. The current
schedule permits four Field increases; the candidate permits six.
The candidate buys the guide's six narrow increases for 60 and leaves 60,
enough for three more Field increases across other eligible entries. The
current schedule uses all 120 on those same narrow increases.

This shows both incentives: cheaper narrow expertise, and more room to develop
additional subjects. Do not presume every player spends all savings on the
same narrow route or that every route benefits from all purchased layers.

At low Rank, one Field Rank plus one applicable Specialty Rank costs 50 under
the current schedule and 30 under the candidate. A Task Rank costs 30 in both.
With neutral initial multipliers and both narrow layers genuinely applicable,
the former pair yields x1.21 versus x1.10 from one Task Rank. This is a
scope-versus-specialization tradeoff, not new permission or proof of a dominant
purchase: the Task applies more broadly and a Field/Specialty pair only when
both subject scopes fit. The candidate makes this tradeoff cheaper.

## Assessment And Decision

The Axis change is modest: about 14.29% cheaper per Axis Rank and 4.35% cheaper
for the parent-plus-one-capped-Axis step. Field Ranks cost one-third less;
Specialty and Precision Ranks cost half as much. The largest behavioral change
therefore concerns expertise acquisition and refinement, not extraordinary
power advancement or broad underlying capability.

The candidate clearly separates practiced activity from portable subject
expertise and makes narrow identity less expensive. It also more strongly
rewards narrow multiplier investment. The same mechanical Rating increments
are available sooner; scope still limits their applicability.

Recommendation: Axis 30 and Field 20 are credible next working prices. The
10-point Specialty/Precision price is the substantive pacing choice: retain
it if four-session dedicated progression to the current Rank horizon is now
desired, or preserve their 20-point price if the earlier 8-session minimum
reference remains desired. This optional mixed recommendation is not a third
adopted schedule or a new progressive pricing rule. User judgment is needed
before replacing the current schedule. No additional same-cost arithmetic
tests are necessary merely to restate this tradeoff.
