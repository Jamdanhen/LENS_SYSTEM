# Narrative Help: One-Point Scope Comparison

Status: Completed qualitative case comparison; the user adopted one-point
narrative help with GM agreement, preserved permissions, normal fictional
persistence, no charge for existing provisions, and no automatic additional
boost. This is not a universal price for items, contacts, languages, or lasting
provisions. The training-credit idea below remains a discussion candidate.

## Objective and Authority

Determine what immediate narrative spending can establish using the equipment
and social-context ideas already discussed. Preserve established Access,
Classification, distinct contribution, ordinary equipment, Automatic action,
and story-award rules. Do not reopen award pacing or boost/reroll pricing.

Sources: the baseline's Working Boost and Reroll Sequence, ordinary equipment
bands, Automatic and Trivial procedure, stable Lenses, and distinct-contribution
principle; the equipment/social handoff retained in
`lens-session-awards-and-story-benefits-test-v0.1.md`; and the completed
`lens-one-point-spending-pacing-test-v0.1.md` comparison.

## Candidate Rule

For one point, establish a specific, useful detail consistent with the
Character, Campaign Profile, and established fiction, subject to GM agreement.
Agree on the detail and its immediate contribution before charging the point.
Do not charge for a truth or provision already established or normally included.

The detail may supply equipment, a route of Access, cooperation already
plausible within the situation, or relevant context. Resolve its contribution
through existing LENS rules. It does not itself purchase a Rank, a new HOLT,
blanket authority, guaranteed contested success, or a second numerical boost.

## Case Comparison

| Case | Proposed ruling | Reason and remaining resolution |
|---|---|---|
| A technician already has suitable stun equipment in the recorded kit. | No narrative point. | Use existing equipment and its defined effects. |
| The technician plausibly packed a compatible stun cartridge, but it was not established. | One-point candidate. | Establish the cartridge; firing and its effects use the normal Expression. Its availability remains setting-dependent. |
| The same request in a setting where stun technology is unavailable to this Character. | Not established by this purchase. | A convenient preparation detail cannot invent an otherwise unavailable technology or extraordinary permission. |
| A climber declares an ordinary rope plausibly packed outside the recorded kit. | One-point candidate only if the kit does not already include it. | The rope permits its ordinary uses. It need not disappear after climbing. |
| An established professional kit normally contains the needed ordinary tool. | No narrative point. | Do not turn ordinary kit use or omitted inventory minutiae into a point tax. |
| A Character declares a conveniently packed fortress-destroying device. | Outside the proposed one-point scope. | Material magnitude and extraordinary capability cannot be inferred from the item's small size or one-use description. |
| A Character invokes assistance already promised by an established contact. | No narrative point for the existing promise. | Network and Access already apply; any genuinely uncertain new request still resolves normally. |
| An attendee at a reception knows the Character from a plausible shared professional event and can introduce them to the host. | One-point candidate. | Establish familiarity and an introduction, not obedience, loyalty, or success at the eventual request. |
| A Character notices a shared hobby with a skeptical official, consistent with what is known. | One-point candidate. | Establish conversational common ground. Its applicability follows the actual approach; it does not determine the official's decision. |
| The official is declared secretly loyal and must grant the disputed request. | Outside the proposed scope as stated. | This bundles a durable commitment with control of the contested outcome. Narrow it to an introduction or shared context if appropriate. |
| A hostile official must admit someone showing an already valid credential under an established rule. | No purchase for the existing entitlement. | If nothing remains uncertain, the result can be Automatic. Narrative spending is not needed to preserve a roll. |
| A point is offered to contradict the established search that removed all carried equipment. | Not established as a packed item. | A distinct plausible source might be proposed, but payment alone does not erase established events or a resolved consequence. |

These are adjudication comparisons, not empirical playtests or independent
automated validations. Their value is whether the same scope explains the
different outcomes without inventing an equipment or social subsystem.

## Persistence and Lasting Development

Three interpretations were compared:

1. Everything disappears after one use. Reject: arbitrary for tools and social
   facts, even though consumption is natural for a cartridge.
2. One point buys a permanent package of equipment, support, or competence.
   Reject: confuses an immediate declaration with separately priced development.
3. The established fact persists as the fiction warrants; only the agreed
   provision or assistance is established. Recommend this interpretation.

A rope can remain an ordinary possession. Meeting someone remains an event
that happened. Neither creates an automatic replenishing supply, guaranteed
future assistance, an increased Assets/Network Rank, or a cash refund.
An ongoing relationship can develop through subsequent play and be recognized
under existing story-award practice; do not bill again merely for remembering
the person. Lasting provision purchases remain a separate unresolved lane.

## Effects, Access, and Double Counting

Narrative help is not guaranteed to be numerically equivalent to x1.20. A rope
can make a crossing possible where no Rating increase would supply the means;
an introduction can open a conversation without making persuasion succeed.
Conversely, a boost needs no new inventory or accommodating social history.
These are different benefits and need not deliver identical returns.

The declared item supplies its ordinary defined function, including any
already established equipment modifier. A social detail is evaluated through
the same applicability and Resonance rules as other actual social context.
The purchase does not add x1.20 merely because a point was spent, nor should
valid fictional consequences be stripped away to force numerical equivalence.
If a valid detail removes the remaining uncertainty, an Automatic outcome is
acceptable. The prohibition is on buying the contested outcome by declaration,
not on useful fiction making an easy problem easy.

A detail whose entire proposed benefit is the already defined numerical boost
should use that purchase, not become another route around its one-per-Expression
limit. Multiple descriptions of the same advantage are not distinct numerical
contributions. Combining genuine different sources of Resonance remains an
existing open rules question, not solved by counting narrative declarations.

Equipment help and a boost can make distinct contributions: having a stun
cartridge permits its use; the purchased boost improves the permitted shot.
Under the one-point candidates, equipment help plus boost plus paid reroll
would total three points. The adopted two-point maximum belongs only to boost
and reroll together, not to every possible expenditure related to an Expression.
No new cap on narrative declarations is adopted by this comparison.

## Recommendation and Next Boundary

Use the candidate rule above, including ordinary persistence and no charge
for existing provisions. One useful, plausible detail is the unit being
proposed, not a universal one-point item price or a license to invent any fact.
GM agreement remains necessary because magnitude, compatibility, and the
established fiction vary by Campaign Profile.

The user adopted this scope at one point; it is now recorded in the baseline
and terminology reference. Award arithmetic is already covered and requires
no repeat pass. Special-result eligibility and general Resonance aggregation
stay unresolved.

## Subsequent Idea: Discretionary Training Credit

Status: User-proposed possibility, not adopted as an automatic spending return.

When narrative help aligns particularly well with something the Character is
already developing and advances the story, a generous GM could redirect that
spent point into earmarked training credit toward the relevant development.
This uses the existing credit concept rather than creating a new ability,
ranked layer, or currency. The user's equipment illustration is not a proposed
named ability or a fixed development route.

Under this interpretation, the immediate help happened and the point now also
counts toward the agreed development purchase; it is not restored as spendable
cash or counted twice as training credit. A completed purchase still follows
its normal price and narrative eligibility. This would deliberately soften
the advancement tradeoff in a GM-selected case, not reimburse every narrative
expenditure or revise the default spending tests.

Retain the idea for discussion without another numerical test or adoption
of an automatic conversion, qualification checklist, or reward frequency.
