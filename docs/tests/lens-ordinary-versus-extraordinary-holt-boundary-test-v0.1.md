# LENS Ordinary-Versus-Extraordinary HOLT Boundary Test

Version: 0.1  
Status: First-Pass Boundary Test; No Final HOLT Scope Adopted  
Date: 2026-09-01

---

## Purpose

Test whether an ordinary Character theme needs the HOLT architecture or
whether the existing ordinary construction layers already represent it.

The specific candidate is an ordinary, non-supernatural martial tradition:

```text
Iron Gate Martial Tradition
```

The test compares the same ordinary warrior built:

```text
A. without a HOLT; and

B. with Iron Gate treated as a hypothetical ordinary HOLT.
```

The test does not assume that a character concept is a HOLT. It asks whether
the candidate performs a distinct job that cannot be performed cleanly by:

- Attributes;
- Derived Attributes;
- Skills;
- Specializations;
- equipment;
- Leverage;
- stable character Lenses.

---

## Boundary Criteria

A candidate HOLT should satisfy all of the following:

```text
1. It represents one coherent theme rather than the whole Character concept.

2. It operates coherently across multiple lower-order Lenses.

3. It grants thematic permission not already supplied completely by ordinary
   construction layers.

4. Axis Rank measures development distinct from an Attribute, Derived
   Attribute, Skill, Specialization, equipment rating, or Leverage rating.

5. It possesses meaningful Access, boundaries, limitations, dependencies, or
   counterplay.

6. Removing it leaves a mechanically important capability or permission
   missing.
```

Merely applying one additional multiplier across several existing actions is
not sufficient. That may describe a broad discount or an extra advancement
track rather than a distinct Lens layer.

No Difficulty, active Resonance, active Dissonance, Scale difference, Load,
armor, or weapon modifier is included. The purpose is to isolate the proposed
ordinary HOLT layer.

The exact HOLT purchasing economy remains unresolved. For one diagnostic only,
Version B exchanges twelve Training Points for four hypothetical HOLT Ranks
and eight hypothetical Axis Ranks at one point per Rank. This exchange is not
an adopted construction rule.

## Permission Comparison

| Expression or truth | Version A | Version B | HOLT required? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strike with a sword | Yes | Yes | No |
| Fight from a shield formation | Yes | Yes | No |
| Move in armor | Yes | Yes | No |
| Rally trained allies | Yes | Yes | No |
| Maintain discipline under fear | Yes | Yes | No |
| Invoke order membership | Yes | Yes | No |
| Request institutional assistance | Yes, when Leverage and fiction support it | Yes | No |
| Suffer order obligations or enemies | Yes | Yes | No |
| Perform an impossible force or supernatural technique | No | No | Would require a separate extraordinary permission |

Every ordinary permission proposed for the Iron Gate tradition already has an
owner in the ordinary architecture.

If Iron Gate instead permits a Character to split a boulder with an empty-hand
strike, remain standing against impossible force, project a protective field,
or perform another capability unavailable to ordinary training, it begins to
pass the HOLT test. That change would make the permission higher-order even if
the setting describes the discipline as learned rather than supernatural.

---

## Duplication Test

The hypothetical HOLT creates three problems.

### 1. Training Duplication

Melee, Athletics, Tactics, Command, and their Specializations already measure
the Character's learned Iron Gate practice. An Iron Gate Axis then measures
the same practice again through another multiplier.

### 2. Permission Duplication

Skills and the fiction already permit ordinary trained maneuvers. Membership,
Leverage, equipment, and stable character Lenses already provide the order's
institutional and personal relationships.

### 3. Concept Promotion

Treating the complete phrase `Iron Gate warrior` as a HOLT risks promoting any
coherent character concept into another ranked layer. A `Crime Fighter`,
`Master Smith`, `Royal Envoy`, or `Wealthy Noble` could then claim a multiplier
across the ordinary components already used to construct that concept.

This would make HOLT a reward for giving existing capabilities one common
name.

## Positive and Negative Controls

### Clear Higher-Order Candidates

```text
Fire
Creates permission to generate, shape, project, perceive, or endure Fire in
ways unavailable through ordinary training alone.

Sea
Creates permission for thematically coherent manifestations involving water,
currents, marine life, pressure, adaptation, or other setting-defined Sea
relationships beyond ordinary Skills.

Strength
Creates permission for impossible force, weight, impact, propulsion, or
projection beyond the ordinary Strength Derived Attribute.
```

These themes may still require a setting-facing HOLD, Access, Axis investment,
and limitations. Their ordinary-looking actions do not erase their distinct
higher-order permissions.

### Clear Ordinary Non-HOLT Candidates

```text
Crime Fighter
Character concept assembled from capability, training, equipment, Leverage,
motivation, and stable character Lenses.

Wealthy Noble
Primarily Assets, Authority, Reputation, Background, and stable identity,
relationship, obligation, and exposure Lenses.

Royal Envoy
Primarily Authority, Network, Reputation, professional Skill, and office-based
Access.

Master Smith
Primarily Skill, Specialization, equipment, Assets, professional Access, and
stable identity.
```

These labels may remain mechanically meaningful without becoming HOLTs.

## First-Pass Result

```text
IRON GATE AS AN ORDINARY HOLT:
FAILS THE DISTINCT-FUNCTION TEST.

The ordinary architecture already supplies every tested permission.
The HOLT primarily adds a thematic multiplier across existing training.

CHARACTER CONCEPT:
NOT AUTOMATICALLY A HOLT.

ORDINARY CHARACTER:
DOES NOT REQUIRE A HOLT OR STARTING HOLT RANKS.
```

The test supports the following leading boundary:

```text
A HOLT is used when a setting-facing HOLD permits a rated Character theme to
grant higher-order permissions that the ordinary Attribute, Derived Attribute,
Skill, Specialization, equipment, Leverage, and stable-Lens architecture does
not already provide.
```

`Higher-order` should not be equated with rare, magical, or socially unusual.
It describes an architectural boundary. A capability may be common in its
setting and still require higher-order permission. A profession, title,
tradition, identity, or concept does not become a HOLT merely because it is
important or coherent.

This is a first-pass result, not final adopted doctrine. It challenges the
current baseline sentence stating that a HOLT may be ordinary or extraordinary
and the ordinary examples attached to that sentence.

---

## Doctrine Implication If Adopted

If this result is adopted:

1. Remove `Wealthy Noble`, `Royal Envoy`, and `Crime Fighter` from the default
   HOLT examples.

2. Remove professions, social stations, and ordinary traditions from the
   default HOLD examples unless a setting defines genuinely higher-order
   permissions through them.

3. Retain stable character Lenses for persistent identity, relationship,
   resource, commitment, obligation, and vulnerability truths.

4. Build the first complete ordinary 25 / 50 / 75 Character without HOLT Rank
   or Axis Rank.

5. Resume HOLT provisioning with an actual higher-order Character after the
   ordinary construction test succeeds.

---

## Exact Next Decision

Determine whether to adopt this boundary as working doctrine:

```text
Ordinary Characters do not require HOLTs.

A character concept is not a HOLT.

A HOLT represents a rated theme granting higher-order permission not already
provided by the ordinary construction architecture.
```
