## User agreement after the test

On 2026-09-15 the user agreed to the reported recommendation. Record the
following as this branch's working direction for the Aspect package:

- One experience point adds 0.20 to the applicable Aspect multiplier for one
  roll, repeatable up to the Aspect's x2.0 maximum, including from Access alone.
- This temporary Aspect expenditure can coexist with the existing single
  ordinary Resonance boost. GM adjudication of narrative applicability remains.
- Narratively meaningful expenditure can contribute strongly toward permanent

  Recognition concerns the whole fiction, not a mandatory receipt-based award.
- Temporary ranks do not automatically become permanent; experience is not
  automatically refunded. Actual sacrifice and other character choices may

The user agreement does not adopt the 20-point rank-value proxy, 75/100-percent

The test record below preserves its original controls
and provisional language as evidence, not contrary decisions. Core integration
remains outside this branch's current write scope.

## Question and result

Can a character spend experience to temporarily raise an applicable Character
Aspect, while the GM also recognizes that sacrifice toward permanent development?

Yes, the numerical controls support this as a viable candidate. Recognizing
experience expenditure at close to its equivalent advancement value is very
different from making the purchased temporary ranks permanent. The former can
remain modest; the latter can finish the entire development range in one scene.
Actual narrative significance remains GM-adjudicated and is not established by
this calculator. Expenditure can be genuine sacrifice without being sufficient
evidence for every award, and non-spending choices retain a development route.

No baseline, terminology, pricing, award card, Leverage structure, or character
record was changed. Only this report and its uniquely scoped test script were
created. Earlier exploration scripts were not modified.

## Controls and boundaries

- Candidate permanent Aspect factor: 1 + rank/10, ranks 0-10. Rank 0 has Access
  without numerical enhancement; the ordinary developed starting example is 1.
- Candidate push: one point adds two temporary ranks (+0.20 to the factor),
  capped at x2.0. Purchases do not compound with one another.
- Coexistence with the existing one-point, +0.20 pooled Resonance boost is a
  test assumption. Existing core rules do not yet authorize this second spending
  channel. The ordinary boost remains usable only once per Expression.
- The selected Aspect is determined from the fiction under GM adjudication,
  informed by the player's character-based recommendation. No stacked Aspects.
- Full spending is deducted once, with no automatic cash refund or extra award
  currency. Permanent recognition is separate from temporary rank application.
- The user's question, "Is it narratively relevant to the fiction?", precedes
  advancement recognition. Informed sacrifice, accepting disadvantage, and
  consistent alignment can matter; ordinary use alone does not mandate growth.
- Separate Aspects can develop in one session. No global one-award quota or
  consecutive-session requirement is inferred. Shared evidence does not turn a
  spent point into two spendable points; valuing an event for several Aspects
  remains a narrative question, not settled by this test.
- Awards can become appropriate during action or reflection. Applying them
  after the tested roll merely isolates feedback in this simulation; it is not
  a between-scenes rule.
- Only the final Expression is floored. Required permitted unopposed checks
  have 1-99% success bounds. Closed Access grants no attempt; Aspect Access does
  not create missing means, Scale, or extraordinary permission.
- Whole-point payment at an odd rank clips the last temporary increment to the
  cap. The user subsequently confirmed that the cost is still one point; no
  fractional purchase, carryover, or refund is introduced. Existing reroll
  handling retains the enhancement.

Current references: the ordinary boost and reroll each cost one point, reference
session award is 19, and current unrestricted Axis ranks cost 20. HOLT Access
also now costs 20. This supersedes the 40/15 prices used in earlier discussions;
the earlier three-to-four-session Aspect guideline remains a candidate narrative
pacing reference, not a newly proven economic equivalence to HOLT Access.

## Immediate output

Use an illustrative Rating contribution of 40 after other factors but before
context and Aspect, with neutral context and an Access-only Aspect. These values
are controls, not a calibrated meteor, encounter, or character build.

| Aspect points | Temporary factor | Rating alone | With ordinary boost | Combined cost |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | x1.0 | 40 | 48 | 1 |
| 1 | x1.2 | 48 | 57 | 2 |
| 2 | x1.4 | 56 | 67 | 3 |
| 3 | x1.6 | 64 | 76 | 4 |
| 4 | x1.8 | 72 | 86 | 5 |
| 5 | x2.0 | 80 | 96 | 6 |

An ordinary roll of 35 produces two Effects at Rating 48 and seven at 96.
The same six-point full push takes starting contributions of 20/40/80 to
48/96/192. Success at 192 remains 99%; output can continue increasing. A specific
goal's sufficient output and opposition are not modeled by raw success chance.

The two one-point benefits are numerically equal only under particular
conditions. At starting factor x1.0 with base 40, context x0.5 gives Rating 28
with the ordinary boost versus 24 with one Aspect point; context x1.5 gives
68 versus 72. Independent Aspect multiplication cannot lift zero context by
itself. Netting +0.20 Resonance first can sometimes make a zero factor positive.

The existing boost plus a reroll of failed attempts gives 72.96% eventual
unopposed success from boosted Rating 48 for at most two points. This remains
a cheaper success-oriented option than a full push, but does not produce the
same higher Expression or Effect distribution. No Monte Carlo encounter or
natural-result continuation simulation is claimed here.

## What does "almost the same rate" mean?

No permanent Aspect purchase price or automatic conversion has been adopted.
To compare economic consequences, the script uses analytical rank-value proxies
of 10, 20, and 60 points, with 0%, 75%, or 100% recognition of Aspect expenditure.
These are test dials, NOT required GM percentages, currency, or an eight-wedge
scoring system. The 20-point control uses the current unrestricted Axis rank
as a numerical-development comparator; equal value or breadth is not asserted.
The 60-point control is a slower-development sensitivity, not an Aspect price.

With the 20-point comparator, a five-point Aspect expenditure supplies:

- 75% recognition: 3.75 points of equivalent development, or 18.75% of a rank.
- 100% recognition: 5 points of equivalent development, or 25% of a rank.

This measures the expenditure's contribution alone. The actual choice and its
other costs can justify more recognition; these fractions do not constrain the
GM's assessment of the scene or establish a maximum award.

Two deliberately aggressive alternative readings were also tested from x1.1:

| Literal conversion control | Full pushes to x2.0 | Total expenditure, including ordinary boosts |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| One permanent rank per Aspect point | 4 | 13 |
| Both temporarily purchased ranks become permanent | 1 | 6 |

Neither is attributed to the user as a chosen rule. They demonstrate why
point-value recognition and rank-for-rank conversion cannot be interchanged.

## Twelve-session expenditure-only comparison

All runs start with x1.1, a 20-point bank, and 19 points awarded after each
session. A full push to x2.0 plus one ordinary boost occurs either once every
four sessions or once each session. All those expenditures are affordable
before the session's award. No other purchases or ordinary narrative rank awards
are included: this isolates development attributed to expenditure alone.

The following uses the 20-point equivalent-value comparator. Analytical
remainders persist to permit a fair cumulative comparison, not to prescribe a
new campaign ledger. "Recognized" is a stipulated GM input, not an algorithm
which determines sincerity or sacrifice.

| Pushes | Recognition | Total spent | Permanent factor | Progress toward next rank from expenditure |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 3 | 0% | 18 | x1.1 | 0% |
| 3 | 75% | 18 | x1.1 | 56.25% |
| 3 | 100% | 18 | x1.1 | 75% |
| 12 | 0% | 72 | x1.1 | 0% |
| 12 | 75% | 66 | x1.3 | 2.5% |
| 12 | 100% | 64 | x1.3 | 60% |

Increasing permanent rank reduces the later expenditure needed to reach x2.0,
so the total spent differs across recognition cases. At full recognition, the
frequent-push character retains 184 unspent points versus 248 for a no-spending
control. Of the 64 spent, 52 went into Aspect pushes and 12 into ordinary boosts.
Recognizing those 52 points' value does not restore their spending freedom.

However, if that recognition is treated as fully equivalent advancement, those
52 points have been redirected rather than wholly sacrificed. The temporary
benefit then accompanies lasting development at little net equivalent-value
loss beyond the ordinary boosts. This can be intentional; it should not be
advertised as losing all 64 points of development value.

Recognizing all six-point-scene spending, including ordinary boosts, instead
of Aspect spending alone produces x1.4 after 12 frequent pushes in the same
20-point model. Whether ordinary boosts, rerolls, or other sacrifices count
depends on narrative significance; the script does not impose a universal
exclusion or automatic inclusion.

## Sensitivity and long-run implications

With a full recognized push every session for 12 sessions, the 10/20/60-point
proxies produce x1.5/x1.3/x1.2 respectively. Thus a claim of "balanced permanent
growth" would be premature without an understood scale of recognition.

At the 20-point proxy and 100% recognition, 36 frequent-push sessions reach
x1.6, with 90% analytical progress toward the next rank. Nine pushes spread
over those 36 sessions reach x1.3. Neither reaches the cap through expenditure
alone. This does not predict total growth once other narrative awards are added.

The model does not create an unlimited cash loop: points leave the bank once,
and a fixed award funds further uses. Permanent development lowers future
costs, but also lowers available temporary headroom and associated spend-based
recognition. At x2.0 there is no Aspect headroom to purchase. General story
development, other Aspects, or fulfillment remain separate possibilities.

## Narrative schedules, held separate from conversion arithmetic

Four illustrative scripts stipulate GM decisions rather than deriving them from
a price. In these cases the whole-scene award already includes expenditure as
evidence; no second automatic spend-credit award is layered over it.

| Twelve-session fixture | Ending factor | Experience spent |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Quiet consistency; GM recognizes development at three relevant points | x1.4 | 0 |
| Three significant costly scenes; GM recognizes one rank at each | x1.4 | 16 |
| Routine full pushes without stipulated development recognition | x1.1 | 72 |
| Sensitivity: GM gives a whole rank after every full push | x2.0, reached at session 9 | 37 |

The equal ending rank of the first two is a control, not a claim that sacrifice
must earn no more than quiet consistency. It demonstrates that spending need
not be mandatory and that immediate benefits and permanent recognition can
coexist. Additional scene significance may change the award.

A failed attempt and a successful attempt can receive the same stipulated
recognition when both involve the same meaningful choice and cost. Deliberate
point-burning without meaningful alignment has no assumed recognition. These
inputs encode the agreed distinctions; they do not prove that a live GM will
apply them fairly or that a player cannot manipulate broad Aspect descriptions.

## Recommendation and actual decision boundary

Continue with the one-point/+0.20 temporary Aspect push and ordinary-boost
coexistence as the working test candidate. The immediate benefit is substantial
and bounded. Count narratively meaningful expenditure as evidence for permanent
development, potentially close to its equivalent advancement value, rather than
automatically converting temporary ranks into permanent ranks.

Use the 20-point comparison as a calibration example, not a required price or
conversion table. A five-point sacrifice can be a meaningful contribution to
development without itself granting five or ten ranks. Equally, a defining
choice can justify a whole rank without being limited to its cash-equivalent
fraction. GM judgment must encompass the whole fiction.

The quantitative test is complete at this scope. The remaining substantive
boundary is how the GM interprets recognition strength: a contribution toward
development versus a whole-rank award. This can remain guided judgment rather
than a fixed percentage. No additional restriction or automatic refund is
needed merely because the test exposes that distinction.
