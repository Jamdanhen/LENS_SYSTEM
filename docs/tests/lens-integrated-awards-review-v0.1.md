# Package 3: Integrated Awards And Advancement Review

Subsequent decision: all three packages were adopted into core LENS on
2026-09-13, including the later tested increase to Participation 10. See
`../design/lens-core-three-package-adoption-v0.1.md`. The review below retains
its original Participation-5 control and recommendations as development history.

Date: 2026-09-13
Status: Discussion assessment. No rules, prices, balances, allocations, refunds,
or award recipients changed.

## Review Basis

Following the user's explicit instruction before Package 3, assume Packages 1
and 2 together for this evaluation: additive Attribute plus Derived foundation,
Access-only HOLTs at 40, and directly purchased Axes at 15 per Rank. This is a
conditional review baseline, not adoption into the main engine. The package's
caution against automatic adoption remains satisfied.

Retain Attribute 60, Derived 20, Task/Field 10, Specialty/Precision 5; the current
award card; one-point immediate purchases; protected creation allocations;
narrative eligibility; and applicable caps. Removing the parent HOLT Rank does
not remove Axis ranks, change Scale, or grant extra permissions.

## Recommendation

Keep the current award architecture and prices as playtest controls. No numerical
finding justifies reducing everyone's awards to recover the old HOLT pacing.
The two engine changes intentionally improve intermediate development and remove
an expense. Neither requires restoring that expense somewhere else.

The most important decisions are experiential: whether the shared-currency
opportunity cost is desirable, and whether limitation compensation should remain
primarily an acquisition benefit. The user's immediate limitation concern should
be resolved first. Do not treat that concern as evidence that awards are wrong.

## 1. Are Limitations Still Worthwhile?

Their established discount does not decrease. Current rules discount only initial
acquisition, not later parent or Axis development. Removing later parent purchases
therefore removes an undiscounted expense; it does not remove a stream of
limitation savings.

| Whole-HOLT burden | Access-only acquisition | Saved | With one Axis 5 | With one Axis 10 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| None | 40 | 0 | 115 | 190 |
| Modest | 30 | 10 | 105 | 180 |
| Substantial | 20 | 20 | 95 | 170 |
| Severe | 10 | 30 | 85 | 160 |

The 25/50/75 percent reductions apply to the acquisition charge, not to the
developed package totals. These are whole-HOLT comparisons; a partial-Axis
limitation does not automatically receive a whole-HOLT discount.

For a Substantial limitation and one Axis 10, the old price was 550 versus 530:
20 saved, about 3.6 percent. The candidate is 190 versus 170: the same 20 saved,
about 10.5 percent. Its relative purchasing benefit grows, rather than shrinks.
Its actual fictional burden need not shrink.

The savings are tangible at the retained prices:

- Modest saves one Task/Field rank, or two Specialty/Precision ranks.
- Substantial saves one Derived rank, or other eligible purchases totaling 20.
- Severe saves two Axis ranks, or other eligible purchases totaling 30.

These are alternative uses of eligible funds, not free ranks, a second grant,
or permission to convert protected starting money. Narrative spending is also a
possible alternative when the saved funds are unrestricted.

At the previously tested 125 HOLT-eligible points, the candidate permits a
highest single Axis of 5/6/7/7, leaving 10/5/0/10 points respectively. The old
parent structure allowed Axis 2 in all four cases, leaving different remainders.
The discount can now reach purchased Axis ranks without first funding headroom.

At 190 points all four candidates can reach Axis 10, leaving 0/10/20/30. The
Rank horizon does not erase the savings: they can support another eligible
purchase rather than higher rank on that Axis.

### The Genuine Concern

A restriction continues to matter after the one-time savings are spent. At ten
saved points per session, a Severe limitation saves three sessions' worth of
funds. Whether that is attractive compensation for a whole campaign of denied
or disrupted capability cannot be established by the price table alone.

For five Axes at 10, a Severe limitation reduces candidate cost from 790 to 760,
only about 3.8 percent. This dilution with breadth already existed and is less
extreme after removing parent expense, but the issue remains: does a one-time
permission discount adequately recognize the actual lasting burden?

Do not equate a 50-percent price reduction with being unusable 50 percent of the
time. Frequency, player control, severity, preparation, and the consequences of
failure all affect burden. Do not invent expected availability probabilities.

Several eligible HOLTs can also supply alternate approaches when one is limited.
That is not automatically an exploit: the alternate Access was purchased. But if
overlapping permissions make a supposed Severe limitation almost irrelevant,
its assessed burden deserves scrutiny. Freed parent expenditure makes such
portfolios more affordable; this is another reason to test the whole character,
not price a limitation from its dramatic description alone.

Recommendation: retain acquisition-only discounts for the controlled comparison.
They remain financially meaningful. Do not automatically introduce cheaper Axis
ranks or recurring point awards for having a limitation. A development discount
would become larger with every covered Axis purchase and would require its own
valuation and buyoff procedure. It would not merely preserve a deleted benefit.

There is a separate unresolved provision: closing or conditioning specific Axes
may return HOLD-defined development value. Its numbers were never settled, and
its reinvestment ceiling currently references parent Rank. Package 2 must replace
that boundary before using such credits. Do not silently discard the concept,
invent compensation for already-unavailable Axes, or count the same loss twice
through acquisition savings and an additional return.

Eligible buyoff can retain the acquisition difference. Paying 20 after buying
Substantial Access for 20 restores a total acquisition payment of 40, without
retrospectively repricing Axis ranks. Actual eligibility is still necessary.

## 2. What The Award Card Recognizes

Retain the five categories for the first integrated playtest. Their distinctions
remain useful and neither engine proposal requires different recognition:

| Category | Practical evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Participation | Taking part; not a performance grade. |
| Progress | Advancing a chosen objective or resolving opposition. |
| Discovery | Learning something consequential that changes understanding or options. |
| Character Expression | Making identity, motives, commitments, or contradictions matter. |
| Narrative Engagement | Developing shared relationships, threads, choices, or consequences. |

Progress does not require fighting, and Narrative Engagement does not require
obedience to a planned story. A failed attempt can still create consequential
discovery or character development; failure itself is not an automatic award.

Character Expression asks what became meaningful about this character. Narrative
Engagement asks what the player helped develop in the shared story. They can
occur in the same scene through distinct contributions; renaming one contribution
is not a second award. This boundary needs usable examples, not new categories.

The existing group Progress/Discovery and individual Expression/Engagement model
is workable as a control, not demonstrated fair merely because its wording is
fair. Quiet players can qualify fully. Awards are not competitive single-winner
prizes. No new reward for rules mastery or tactical optimization is needed.

### Recipient Risk Is Distinct From Award Size

If group Progress and Discovery are meaningful, everyone participating receives
11 before individual categories. Individual awards can then yield 11, 14, or 17
with zero, one, or two meaningful categories. Over ten identical sessions, the
11-versus-17 gap is 60 points, an Attribute rank at current prices.

That arithmetic is demonstrated. It does not demonstrate that quieter players
will receive less, nor that equal awards are necessarily the intended policy.
It shows why recurring differences deserve an explanation grounded in choices
and actual opportunities rather than airtime. Raising every award does not
correct systematically unequal opportunities or double credit.

Keep the stated 0/3/5 distinctions. Observe actual award reasons and opportunities
in a short playtest debrief; do not create a second scoring rubric. Absent-player
and milestone-recipient policies remain unresolved.

## 3. Concrete Progression Benchmarks

Each row starts from zero savings toward that target, saving for that target
alone. Values are whole sessions, rounded up. No milestones, story grants,
discounts, or starting subsidies are included. These are affordability dates,
not automatic acquisition permission or mid-scene rank activation.

| Purchase | Cost | Save 14 | Save 10 | Save 6 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Specialty/Precision +1 | 5 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| Task/Field +1 | 10 | 1 | 1 | 2 |
| Owned HOLT Axis +1 | 15 | 2 | 2 | 3 |
| Derived +1 | 20 | 2 | 2 | 4 |
| New Task/Field 0 to 3 | 30 | 3 | 3 | 5 |
| New unrestricted HOLT, no Axis ranks | 40 | 3 | 4 | 7 |
| Specialty 0 to 10, Field already held | 50 | 4 | 5 | 9 |
| Attribute +1 | 60 | 5 | 6 | 10 |
| Another Axis 0 to 5, HOLT already held | 75 | 6 | 8 | 13 |
| Task/Field 0 to 10 | 100 | 8 | 10 | 17 |
| New HOLT with one Axis 5 | 115 | 9 | 12 | 20 |
| New HOLT with one Axis 10 | 190 | 14 | 19 | 32 |
| New HOLT with three Axes 5 | 265 | 19 | 27 | 45 |

Affordability of repeated purchases must use retained remainders; do not treat
each independently rounded session count as a recurring cooldown. For example,
150 saved points buy ten Axis ranks, even though 15/14 rounds to two sessions
for a first isolated rank. Fourteen points per session reaches 150 in eleven
sessions, not twenty.

### What Those Purchases Actually Buy

At A5/D3 with all four applicable Training ranks 5, raw output is 40.5 before
Difficulty and other factors. One Attribute rank and one Derived rank each
raise this particular route to 45.5625, a 12.5-percent improvement. The Attribute
also improves all five Derived routes beneath it and raises their ceilings;
the Derived purchase affects only its route. Its cheaper price buys less breadth.

One Task, Field, or Specialty rank from 5 to 6 improves its applicable Expression
by about 6.67 percent, not ten percentage points of success. Their coverage
differs: the Task is an activity, the Field is portable expertise, the Specialty
is narrower. None creates extraordinary permission. A new Rank-3 Task or Field
adds x1.3 where applicable; it is an early useful interest, not completed mastery.

An owned, Open Axis from 0 to 1 adds x1.1; Axis 5 gives x1.5 and Axis 10 x2.0.
An Axis can support several manifestations through its Derived relationship;
it is not necessarily one named power. An additional HOLT adds qualitatively
different permitted options even with no purchased Axis bonus.

Under Package 1, focused development has greater low/middle impact, as intended.
Raising all development prices or lowering awards simply to cancel that effect
would undo the proposal rather than evaluate it. Package 2 changes affordable
Axis development, not output at identical Axis ranks.

### Intended Feel Recommended For Discussion

Recommend frequent small choices, a broad improvement requiring several sessions
of priority, and deep extraordinary development unfolding over sustained play.
At ten to fourteen saved points, the card already offers small purchases after
one or two sessions, useful Rank-3 interests after three, an Attribute after
five or six, and acquisition plus Axis 10 after fourteen to nineteen.

This is a proposed feel to judge, not a new target schedule. Rank-10 Specialty
in four saving sessions was already acceptable to the user and does not make
the whole character an expert. A character who distributes spending progresses
in several directions instead of completing each of these targets on schedule.

## 4. Shared Currency And Award Sensitivity

At gross 14, spending four retains ten: 28.6 percent less saving, or 1.4 times
the continuous saving time. Spending eight retains six: 57.1 percent less saving,
or about 2.33 times the time. Whole-purchase rounding can vary these ratios.

| Gross award | Spend | Retain | Attribute +1 | New HOLT + Axis 10 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 10 | 4 | 6 | 10 sessions | 32 sessions |
| 14 | 0 | 14 | 5 | 14 |
| 14 | 4 | 10 | 6 | 19 |
| 14 | 8 | 6 | 10 | 32 |
| 17 | 4 | 13 | 5 | 15 |
| 25 | 4 | 21 | 3 | 10 |

These are sensitivities, not instructions to force the card to an award total.
Seventeen is all four meaningful categories, not an unusual exceptional card.
Fourteen should not quietly become a ceiling or a purported observed mean.

Immediate spending has real utility: it can change a current result or establish
a useful narrative detail. Less rank development alone is not proof the spender
received less value. Conversely, repeated spending is not financially trivial:
five one-point purchases equal one Specialty rank. A player's opportunity cost
and the table's enjoyment are separate questions.

Possible hoarding, unequal exposure to spend-worthy situations, and regret are
untested behavioral concerns. No live evidence establishes that these occur or
that a second pool would improve play. Retain the shared pool and one-point
prices for comparison; do not automatically reimburse spending or charge the
group for another player's choice. Test whether players find using help worth
the visible development tradeoff.

Net-savings rows are planning controls, not first-session credit. A point must
already be available when spent. If an opening scene has no spendable balance,
its later session award cannot fund an earlier boost. Existing carryover and
actual permitted starting funds supply any opening balance; no new float is given.

## 5. Story Rewards And Milestones

Keep story-delivered benefit distinct from new spendable points. A relationship,
piece of equipment, position, discovery, permission, or explicitly granted rank
can change the character even when their point ledger does not increase.

Finding a teacher or source can establish eligibility without granting paid
development. An explicit development grant is already received, not a second
cash award. Do not require a price for every story event, deduct it automatically
from session awards, or treat narrative discovery as automatically free HOLT Access.

The same event can support category recognition and yield a fictional benefit
under the existing rule. That is different from awarding the benefit's purchase
value again in spendable points. Record what was actually received and any
remaining purchase requirement without making a compulsory valuation ledger.

Milestones materially affect longer purchases. For a hypothetical eligible
recipient saving ten per session, a 12.5-point milestone every four sessions
changes acquisition plus Axis 10 from session 19 to session 16. Every six changes
it to session 17. These timings credit milestones only when reached, not in
advance. Frequency and recipients are illustrations, not adopted policies.

The optional 12.5 amount also remains fractional despite the whole-point card.
That is a usability question for later judgment, not a mathematical defect or
permission to silently round it. No milestone schedule is required.

## 6. Character Diversity And Diagnosis

The Package 2 comparison already tested ordinary and extraordinary alternatives
using the same 125 additional eligible points and the additive foundation. A
moderate all-five route favored ordinary purchases numerically: raw 137.18
versus 112.64 for its best acquisition-plus-Axis alternative. The developed
guide's signature favored the HOLT alternative: 363.89 versus 326.40. Those
single-route results are not a total valuation of either character or Access.

At 190, possible eligible portfolios remain one HOLT with Axis 10; one HOLT with
five Axes 2; or four HOLTs and two total Axis ranks. Their different strengths
are concentration, several numerical routes, and permission breadth. The loss
of parent headroom's breadth economy is real, not evidence that the portfolios
must be equally effective at one chosen action.

| Concern | Variable to examine first |
| --- | --- |
| Limited HOLT unattractive for its actual burden | Limitation valuation, covered scope, and compensation; not global awards. |
| One Axis purchase dominates comparable investments | Axis applicability and relative price; not every character's award. |
| All routes develop too fast or too slowly | Award reference and actual net savings, including milestones. |
| Some players consistently receive less without fair opportunities | Recognition and recipient practice. |
| Immediate help feels too expensive to use | Shared-currency preference and immediate price, informed by play feedback. |
| Story grants overwhelm paced purchases | Actual Profile grants and their frequency, not a universal deduction rule. |

## First Decision And Smallest Change

Follow-up decision, 2026-09-13: the user agreed with acquisition cost as the
limitation benefit. Retain acquisition-only reductions in the Access-only
comparison, with full-price Axis development. This confirms that direction,
not wholesale adoption of the engine packages or a resolution of partial-Axis
compensation. The next discussion concerns the progression experience below.

No numerical schedule change is justified yet. Retain the award card, flat
prices, shared currency, and protected allocations for the integrated playtest.
Clarify gross awards versus saved advancement and one-time acquisition discounts
versus unresolved Axis-specific compensation in the review guidance.

The first substantive choice raised by the user is whether a limitation's
reward should principally be cheaper entry to restricted permission, or should
also increase with continued restricted development. Recommend the former as
the default: current savings remain meaningful and the simpler procedure still
works. If an actual restricted package is unattractive, assess that package's
burden and compensation before changing all Axis prices or everyone else's awards.

The next award-specific judgment is whether frequent small improvements alongside
the measured savings tradeoff is the desired experience. That should precede
selecting a replacement award total. Both choices remain discussion proposals.
