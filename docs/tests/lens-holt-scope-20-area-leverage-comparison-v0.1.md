# HOLT Scope: Twenty-Area Reference and Leverage Comparison

Status: Arithmetic experiment only. No HOLT pricing or scope rule adopted.

## Question

The user's starting idea was twenty Derived Attribute areas, at five percent
each, as a possible basis for pricing HOLT scope restrictions. Leverage was
identified as an unresolved complication, not an instruction to replace that
idea with twenty-five equally priced areas.

This bounded series compares reference choices. It does not assign actual
Domains to areas or decide which areas a HOLT can affect.

## Assumptions

- Original areas: the twenty under Might, Mobility, Psyche, and Influence.
- Additional areas: the five under Leverage.
- Equal per-area weighting is an experimental assumption.
- Values below are reference units, not XP, Expression multipliers, or Load.
- The ten-area physical example is illustrative, not a restriction adopted
  for Strength or any other HOLT.
- No fixed HOLT acquisition cost, Axis cost, or limitation discount is set.

## Comparison

| Illustrative scope | Keep twenty areas = 100; five units each | Make twenty-five areas = 100; four units each |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| One original Attribute family: five areas | 25 | 20 |
| Physical families: ten areas | 50 | 40 |
| Original four families: twenty areas | 100 | 80 |
| Leverage only: five areas | 25 | 20 |
| Mixed: three original plus two Leverage areas | 25 | 20 |
| Original twenty plus one Leverage area | 105 | 84 |
| All twenty-five areas | 125 | 100 |

The first inclusive model preserves the original five-percent increment by
treating 100 as a reference, not a ceiling. The second changes the reference
to the full twenty-five-area set. Neither is an adopted solution.

For any count n, 5n = 1.25 x 4n. Consequently these two models produce the
same relative scope prices. They differ in the reference used to convert
those proportions into actual prices.

The physical illustration remains half of the original twenty-area reference.
It is two-fifths of full twenty-five-area coverage in either inclusive model.
Thus its discount relative to all twenty-five would be 60%, not 50%.

## Boundary Controls

Ignoring Leverage entirely leaves Leverage-only coverage unpriced by this
formula. That is a missing valuation, not evidence that such capability is
worthless or should be free.

Capping the five-unit formula at 100 assigns no additional scope cost to
areas twenty-one through twenty-five. This was a diagnostic control, not a
user proposal.

A zero-area result is zero for this scope component only. It does not settle
the price of thematic permission, unranked Access, or a fixed acquisition
charge.

Counting areas cannot by itself distinguish two HOLTs that cover the same
areas but grant different permissions within them. This series verifies the
arithmetic, not equal gameplay value or purchase balance.

## Verification

Companion script:
`lens-holt-scope-20-area-leverage-comparison-v0.1.ps1`

Executed successfully: 126 possible count combinations (0-20 original areas,
0-5 Leverage areas), with 595 arithmetic assertions passed. These are count
combinations, not exhaustive fictional applications or combat simulations.

## Return Point

The five-percent increment is mathematically available if the original
twenty-area total remains a reference rather than a maximum. This does not
decide whether Leverage should participate in the same scope calculation.

The next substantive discussion is what the reference price represents:
coverage of the original twenty areas, or coverage of the entire permitted
scope including Leverage. No baseline or terminology changes were made.
