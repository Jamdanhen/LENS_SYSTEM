# Benefits and Drawbacks: Main Review

Date: 2026-09-17.
Status: Main review preserved as decision history. The framework was subsequently
adopted as working core rules on 2026-09-17, as recorded below. Individual
catalogue valuations remain provisional.

## Subsequent Adoption

The user approved the Benefits and Drawbacks framework with traceable actual
compensation and provisional catalogue valuations, preserving Profile eligibility,
existing buyoff logic, the forty-point starting compensation ceiling, and
deferred species balancing. Baseline, terminology, construction references,
and the source package now carry the adopted framework. The findings below
retain their review context; adoption is no longer pending.

No existing character records, ordinary development prices, session awards,
or protected allocations were changed. Flight ten remains a test price, and
the source package's other valuation candidates remain provisional. Aspects
and their deferred extensions were not reopened.

The originating task's handoff distinguishes approved branch decisions from
provisional valuations. This review preserves that distinction.

## Recommendation

Advance the framework to main working adoption, with the integration guidance
below. Retain the 40-point starting compensation ceiling as a playtest control,
not proof that every compensated build is balanced. Do not promote illustrative
benefit prices, drawback valuations, or restricted-permission estimates into a
universal catalogue. Personal Flight at ten points remains a working test price.

This is a useful extension of existing Stable Character Lens doctrine, not a
reason to introduce another ranked layer. Narrative eligibility, contextual
pooling, permission boundaries, and existing buyoff logic do most of the work.

## Findings and Integration Issues

### 1. Fixed Resonance Prices Need Comparisons Against Developed Characters

Source: package lines 258-281, Fixed Resonance Comparisons.

The package already identifies this issue correctly. A cheap fixed benefit
can outperform additional narrow training and may cover more situations. That
is a valuation risk, not a defect in permitting benefits or a reason to change
contextual pooling.

With 40 before Precision, Precision 5, and neutral context, the Expression is
60. Ten points in two Precision ranks gives 68. A hypothetical ten-point +0.20
Resonance benefit instead gives 72 when applicable. At contextual factor 1.5,
the alternatives tie at 102; at factor 2.0, training gives 136 and the benefit
132. At factor 0.2, training gives 13.6 and the benefit 24 before final flooring.

The benefit's relative value therefore changes with context. Its permission,
breadth, applicability, and ability to counter interference matter as well as
its neutral-case increase. A permanent +0.20 is not priced by multiplying the
one-point temporary boost price, and ten points does not universally buy it.

Recommended integration: retain provisional status and compare each proposed
entry against development that applies to the same actual activities. No
universal benefit price or award adjustment is justified by these controls.

### 2. The Cap Requires Actual Compensation to Be Traceable at Buyoff

Source: package lines 28-40 and 79-87.

The package correctly uses received compensation rather than a hypothetical
catalogue value. But a total-only record becomes insufficient if several
drawbacks exceed the cap and one is later removed.

For example, two burdens assessed at thirty each can award only forty in total.
Their actual credited shares need to be recorded with GM agreement. If twenty
was credited to each, ordinary full buyoff of one uses twenty, not thirty.
That equal split is an illustration, not a mandatory allocation formula. Do
not assign the full forty to both, retrospectively change the credit to alter
buyoff, or redistribute unused compensation automatically after removal.

Recommended integration: record actual compensation attributed to the relevant
drawback or agreed combined burden, together with prior buyoff payments. Where
burdens cannot be valued separately, retain that relationship for adjudication.
Use the existing narrative eligibility and difference-based buyoff principle;
do not invent a new removal currency, surcharge, or subsystem. Partial easing
must identify what credited burden is actually relieved.

### 3. Benefit Labels Must Preserve Permission and Duplicate-Contribution Rules

Source: package lines 183-256 and 258-271; baseline Stable Character Lenses,
Distinct Contribution Rule, and Working HOLT Purchase Prices.

A benefit can itself define a permission. That does not require buying broad
HOLT Access and the same narrow permission twice. Conversely, calling personal
flight a benefit does not make it available in a Profile that prohibits that
capability or disallows those funds for that extraordinary provision.

The package preserves setting-specific eligibility. Carry it through explicitly
to benefit entries and the creation interface: approved scope and source matter,
not which shopping heading the player chooses. A restricted-permission comparison
does not grant unrelated HOLT manifestations, Axes, or later expansion for free.

Night Vision at four points is arithmetically correct under the proposed 80%
reduction. That does not establish that 80% is justified. The current core
expressly says narrower thematic scope alone does not automatically earn a
discount. These are assessed comparison fixtures, not a naming-based formula.

Likewise, Sea Legs and Natural Balance do not automatically yield +0.40 for
the same balance contribution on a moving vessel. Apply the existing distinct-
contribution test to their actual provisions. Genuinely distinct contributions
may combine; do not create a blanket one-benefit limit. The same principle
prevents paying twice for an existing language or another existing provision.

### 4. A Forty-Point Ceiling Limits Compensation, Not Optimization

Source: package lines 153-181.

The published spending-side controls are correct. Forty extra points can raise
one applicable ordinary Expression from 25.74 to 46.80, an 81.8% increase before
Difficulty. With one relevant 20% Dissonance contribution and otherwise neutral
context, the result is 37.44, still 45.5% above the original value.

This does not prove unfairness. The purchased Specialty and Precision must
apply, other actions may suffer, and actual drawback consequences can remove
options rather than merely reduce a roll. Nor does the example imply all
selected drawbacks always apply or may be collapsed into one 20% penalty.

It does show why the ceiling alone cannot validate the trait prices. Keep
the already-approved finished-character review: actual burden, meaningful
applications, alternative means, equipment, and powers. Do not automatically
erase a real restriction because the player prefers the restricted style, or
invent new burdens because the player found a legitimate workaround.

## What Already Fits the Core

- Defined benefits need no separate advantage ranks. Their permitted use can
  draw on existing character capability and training.
- Guaranteed applications make drawback agreements concrete without making
  those examples exclusive boundaries or penalizing every use of a named Field.
- Severity, breadth, frequency, and countermeasures inform negotiated value;
  no points-per-Field or percentage-to-price formula is needed.
- Forty limits total starting compensation, not drawback count. Thirty and
  sixty remain comparison candidates, not alternate adopted defaults.
- Ordinary flexible funds and awarded compensation can support the branch's
  approved purchases without breaching protected allocations or Profile rules.
- A cost and a compensating restriction are valued together. A limitation
  already funding a discounted capability cannot earn another credit for the
  same burden. Existing provisions are not charged again.
- Eligible benefits can be bought in play at creation prices. An explicit GM
  story grant needs no second point payment; an opportunity is not automatically
  a grant. No automatic refund or debt follows from a later unwanted injury.
- Chosen bonds and duties remain with the adopted Aspect treatment for this
  initial comparison. Flaw-funded Aspects and temporary enhancement stay deferred.

## Independent Arithmetic Check

Enumerated legal increases from both supplied starting arrays, enforcing
Attribute 10, Derived <= Attribute, Training 10, and current flat prices.
Specialty and Precision retain their existing Field prerequisite without a
Field-rank cap. The supplied starts already contain that Field. No ranks were
reallocated or reduced. Each comparison spends at most its named added budget.

Across budgets 0, 30, 35, 40, and 60, the enumeration examined 1,313 legal
allocations counting repeated allocations across separate budget controls.
All published optimum outputs match. Results precede Difficulty and flooring.

| Added budget | A5 D5 T5 F3 S2 P1 | A5 D5 T5 F5 S8 P8 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | 25.74 | 72.90 |
| 30 | 40.95 | 96.00 |
| 35 | 43.875 | 97.28 |
| 40 | 46.80 | 102.40 |
| 60 | 59.67 | 115.60 |

One first-build optimum at forty is A5 D5 T5 F3 S5 P6: three Specialty ranks
cost fifteen and five Precision ranks cost twenty-five. One second-build optimum
is A5 D5 T6 F6 S10 P10: twenty in Task/Field and twenty in narrower expertise.

The provisional 5 + 10 + 20 selection totals thirty-five. Ceilings 30/40/60
therefore produce actual awards 30/35/35 and flexible totals 155/160/160, not
155/165/185. Full forty-point compensation would produce 165. The 125 base is
unchanged. No additional species allowance is part of these numbers.

Permission comparisons also calculate correctly: 20 reduced by 80%, 50%, and
60% gives 4, 10, and 8 respectively. Arithmetic does not validate those burdens
or settle encounter balance. No new movement baseline is inferred from Flight.

## Minimal Integration Guidance

The framework can be integrated without a new subsystem if its records carry:
the actual provision or burden; relevant circumstances and guaranteed examples;
approved permission/funding eligibility; agreed price or actual compensation;
and prior buyoff payments or explicit story-grant status where relevant.

These are character-sheet and future app data requirements, not a request to
implement the interface now. Existing stable-Lens records can hold them. They
do not create a universal application-count formula or an additional point pool.

## Decision and Next Work

The first substantive main decision is framework adoption, separate from
catalogue calibration. Recommend adopting the branch-approved working rules
with the clarification above that actual credit must be traceable. Preserve
Flight 10 as a test price, the other illustrative valuations as provisional,
and the compensation ceiling as a playtest reference.

HOLD/species-package balancing, human provisions, and variants remain explicitly
parked. They are not dependencies for this adoption. Do not reopen those lanes,
the lasting-Aspect rules, or their deferred extensions through this review.

After the decision, the next useful deliverable is a compact playable packet
with a draft character sheet and app-facing data requirements. Include these
benefit/drawback agreements in a small set of playable characters rather than
expanding an exhaustive catalogue. The resulting playtest can observe actual
burden, benefit use, and the visibility of costs and permissions. This review
does not authorize that build, modify live character balances, or adopt prices.
