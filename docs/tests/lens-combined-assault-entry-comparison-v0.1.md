# Combined Assault Entry Comparison v0.1

Date: 2026-09-12.
Status: Narrative boundary comparison and proposal, not a new adopted entry rule.

Subsequent decision, 2026-09-12: the user explicitly approved feasible coordinated
execution as the entry guidance, without physical fusion or a requirement that
every contributor succeed. The baseline and terminology reference now record
that guidance. The comparisons below remain the supporting discussion. Work
returns to complementary output and area growth; this does not adopt any area
rate or complementary-output conversion.

## Why This Comparison Is Needed

Working rules now give a genuine combined assault one defensive Expression,
with actual scope limits, bounded cancellation allocation, and at most one
supported defensive-surplus counter even if another contribution gets through.
Each contributor still commits an action and rolls their own full Expression.

The unresolved application question is what makes the assault genuinely
combined. Mere shared target, simultaneous timing, or saying "teamwork" is not
enough under the adopted boundary. Requiring physically fused attacks or failure
of every contributor when one fails would instead exclude previously supported
shared-output cases without justification.

## Candidate Operational Question

What are the participants doing together that makes their contributions one
coordinated application of force or effect, rather than independent attempts
against the same target?

Require a concrete account of shared execution before rolls, with the actual
timing, means, declared functions, and action commitments needed to do it.
Do not require an extra coordination roll merely to confirm that account.
If the description still establishes independent attacks, resolve them as such.

This is proposed adjudication guidance, not a new numerical gate. Whether the
following examples supply sufficiently clear teaching boundaries is the next
user decision. Fictional acceptance cannot be proven by an arithmetic script.

## Paired Examples

### 1. Independent Shots at the Same Target

Two shooters each take their own shot when their opportunity occurs. They
share an enemy and want the same ultimate result, but neither shot's execution
has been arranged with the other.

Assessment: separate attacks with ordinary defensive responses. A common goal
such as "defeat that enemy" does not by itself combine their Expressions.
Describing their turns as happening close together is not enough to change this.

### 2. A Joint Sustained Barrage

Two permitted beam users align their attacks and maintain their pressure
together against the same defended point as one declared assault. Their
timing and aim are deliberately coordinated to combine pressure there.

Assessment: candidate genuine combined assault, with one defensive Expression.
The beams need not change substance or become an invented new power. If one
contributor fails or loses their ability to maintain the attack, the other's
independently viable output remains; this is shared reinforcement, not mandatory
all-or-nothing dependency. Actual means and timing still need to support it.

Merely firing two unrelated beams at the same opponent is no more automatically
combined than the independent shots in the preceding example.

### 3. A Paired Weapon Assault

Two weapon users coordinate a paired strike, arranging their approaches and
timing to bring joint pressure against the defender's guard in one assault.
Both contribute the declared harm rather than claiming an unrelated setup
result and then converting its entire output into damage.

Assessment: candidate genuine combined assault on the same basis as the joint
barrage, provided their actual positions and means support that execution.
The swords do not need to merge into one object. Distinct hits still encounter
protection according to actual scope; one defense does not erase that identity.

Two unrelated swings at the same enemy remain separate. This distinction must
be established from the described coordination before rolls, not chosen after
seeing which exchange structure would produce a better outcome.

### 4. A Feint Followed by a Strike

One actor creates a distraction or opening; a second uses it to attack.

Assessment: cooperation does not automatically make this one shared damage
pool. If the first activity establishes a preparatory benefit and the second
later exploits it, use the existing preparation and action rules. If the
declared construction is genuinely complementary within one assault, preserve
those distinct functions and dependencies. Opening output does not become
damage solely because it helped a damage-producing activity.

This example is sensitive to the actual declaration and timing; the word
"feint" alone does not settle every case. No automatic Effect-to-Resonance
conversion or new preparation value is introduced.

### 5. A Transported Harmful Contribution

One actor creates and sends a wave; a second places a permitted harmful
contribution in it so the same advancing wave brings both to the target.

Assessment: a clear candidate combined assault when declared and performed
as one shared construction. Ordinary travel and impact of the wave do not
require another mandatory delivery roll. The carried contribution depends on
the wave where it has no independent route, while the wave can remain useful
if the additional contribution fails. Preserve actual function and protection.

If the wave already completed its attack before the second actor acted, their
later activity is not retroactively part of that resolved incoming event.

## What the Comparison Rules Out

- No automatic privilege for beams over swords or technology over ordinary
  capability. Equivalent shared execution receives equivalent treatment.
- No requirement for physical fusion, a named special move, or every
  contribution to fail together. Those can describe some combinations, not all.
- No retrospective pooling after rolls or after the target already resolved
  a separate attack. Actual declaration and shared execution must precede it.
- No free extra baseline Effect, coordination bonus, or conversion of every
  supporting function into harm. Existing conservation remains in force.
- No automatic permission to combine merely by naming a target together.
  A declaration must explain and be capable of carrying out the joint activity.

## Tradeoff and Recommendation

The strongest usable boundary is coordinated execution, not a specific weapon
type or a physical-fusion requirement. Action commitment and actual timing
provide the existing mechanical investment. Do not invent another cost solely
because that investment is not a separate character-sheet purchase.

This is still a narrative judgment. Players may reasonably choose genuine
combined assaults frequently when concentrated output serves their objective;
frequency alone does not invalidate them. Equally, an empty label cannot be
allowed to take away defensive opportunities. The examples should help the
table recognize the difference without a new catalogue of legal maneuvers.

Recommendation: accept a combined assault when participants establish and
perform one coordinated application of their contributions before resolution,
with feasible shared timing and actual commitment. Shared target or timing
alone is insufficient; physical fusion and all-or-nothing dependency are not
required. Keep separate or complementary results in their actual functions.

No new adoption follows from this record. Once the user settles this boundary,
return to the outstanding complementary-output and area-growth work rather
than generating more approval steps for already-resolved cancellation rules.
