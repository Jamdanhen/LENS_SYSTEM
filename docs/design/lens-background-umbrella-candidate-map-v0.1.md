# LENS Background Umbrella Candidate Map

Version: 0.1
Status: Superseded category map; retained as a vocabulary source
Date: 2026-08-31
Source: Current LENS background discussion and background peer survey

> Supersession note: The later Leverage fifth-Attribute test replaces these six
> umbrellas as the current leading mechanical categories. This map remains a
> vocabulary bank. Resources inform Assets; Connections inform Network;
> Standing informs Authority and Reputation; Origin informs Heritage;
> Profession and Disposition are redistributed according to their actual Lens
> functions.

---

## Purpose

This document organizes candidate background content beneath six provisional
umbrellas:

```text
Resources
Connections
Standing
Profession
Disposition
Origin
```

It is a candidate bank, not an exhaustive catalogue.

The map records both:

- ways a background truth may support, enable, or enhance a Character; and
- ways it may constrain, expose, burden, or reduce what the Character can do.

`Limitation` is being used informally in the discussion. This document uses
`constraints and exposures` so it does not silently extend the formal HOLT
Limitation and compensation procedure.

---

## Shared Controls

```text
The six umbrellas are organizational candidates, not adopted mechanical
categories.

An entry may reasonably touch more than one umbrella.

Place it according to the principal fictional truth being established.

Separate it when its parts can be independently granted, invoked, lost,
transferred, damaged, removed, or countered.

No entry is permanently positive or negative.

Its relationship to the current Expression and fiction determines whether it
creates permission, Access, Resonance, Dissonance, resource availability,
obligation, vulnerability, consequence, or no current contribution.

Nothing in this map grants an automatic numerical modifier, construction
point, compensation value, Skill Rank, equipment rating, or HOLT permission.
```

current relationships, even when those relationships began in the Character's
origin.

## Cross-Umbrella Routing Guide

Some recurring concepts belong under different umbrellas according to their
source and mechanical function.

### Habits

```text
Disposition by default.

Profession when the habit is specifically an occupational practice.

Origin when it is primarily a continuing cultural custom or formative routine.
```

### Obligations and Responsibilities

```text
Resources
Debt, upkeep, employees, tenants, or conditions attached to property.

Connections
Duty to family, allies, patrons, dependents, communities, or organizations.

Standing
Duty of office, jurisdiction, oversight, public accountability.

Profession
Duty of care, ethics, confidentiality, employer requirements.

Disposition
Personal oath, promise, principle, chosen cause, or compulsion.

Origin
Inherited duty, cultural expectation, lineage burden, legacy obligation.
```

### Reputation

```text
Standing by default.

Connections when the reputation exists only within a particular network,
community, or organization.

Profession when a professional reputation is inseparable from the practice,
although public recognition may still need a separate Standing truth.
```

### Community

```text
Connections when it describes an ongoing reciprocal relationship.

Origin when it describes the community that formed the Character.

Both may exist as separate truths when the formative origin and current
relationship can change independently.
```

### Credentials

```text
Standing when the credential supplies recognized qualification, clearance,
license, or permission.

Profession may establish why the Character holds it, but the credential can
remain separate when it may be revoked, suspended, or transferred without
erasing the profession or training.
```

### Authority

```text
Standing by default.

Profession or Connections may be the source of the authority, but authority
should be recorded separately when it can be lost while the profession or
affiliation remains true.
```

### Possessions and Heirlooms

```text
Resources for ownership, control, and availability.

Origin for inherited identity, history, or legacy.

Equipment, Artifact, or Infrastructure rules for what the object does.
```

### Enemies and Rivals

```text
Connections when the ongoing hostile relationship is itself the truth.

Standing, Profession, or Origin when the enemy is primarily an exposure
created by one of those truths.
```

### Secrets

```text
Not a separate umbrella by default.

Place the secret under the truth it conceals: Resources, Connections,
Standing, Profession, Disposition, or Origin.
```

### Dependents

```text
Connections for the relationship.

Resources when material support is the principal pressure.

Another umbrella may identify why the responsibility exists.
```

### Oaths and Codes

```text
Disposition when personally chosen and internalized.

Connections when owed to a person, group, or institution.

Standing when attached to office or authority.

Profession when part of professional ethics.

Origin when inherited from culture, lineage, or legacy.
```

## Current Map Summary

```text
Resources
What the Character owns, controls, can draw upon, or must maintain.

Connections
Who and what groups the Character remains related to.

Standing
How the Character is publicly, socially, legally, or institutionally situated.

Profession
What field, vocation, occupation, discipline, or practice identifies the
Character.

Disposition
What the Character consistently values, wants, fears, believes, or tends to
do.

Origin
What the Character comes from and what of that past remains true.
```

Each umbrella can contain:

```text
support
permission
Access
Resonance
resource relationships
constraints
exposures
obligations
vulnerabilities
Dissonance
consequences
```

No umbrella is inherently an advantage category or a disadvantage category.
