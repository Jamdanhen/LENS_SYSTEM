# Dwarf and Elf Starting-Package Comparison

Status: The user approved retaining the proposed elf package as a working
comparison candidate and examining its limitation against Dense Build. This
is not final package adoption or a finding of equal value. The user clarified that dwarf provisions
have not changed. The stone-sense exchange remains an unadopted candidate and
is not used here. Human provisions remain parked.

## Controlled Packages

The dwarf is unchanged from its working reference:

- Darkvision: sensory Access, precise limits still setting-defined.
- Natural-poison Resistance: applicable x1.10 Resonance.
- Stone Observation: applicable x1.10 Resonance involving stone, stonework,
  gems, or precious metals.
- Dense Build: applicable x0.90 Dissonance for jumping, buoyancy, and
  weight-sensitive crossings. No blanket Mobility penalty.

The elf is retained by user approval as a working comparison candidate:

- Low-light sight: use available faint light where ordinary sight would be
  insufficient; does not supply sight in total darkness. Existing illustration.
- Snow passage: travel over loose snow without sinking under the Character's
  ordinary personal load. Does not grant balance on ice, immunity to cold,
  unlimited cargo support, or general weightlessness. Existing illustration,
  with these bounds supplied for the fixture.
- Keen Hearing: x1.10 Resonance to Observation through available sound.
  Working candidate; no mind-reading, through-wall permission, or silence bypass.
- Light Build: x0.90 Dissonance when low bodily mass hinders holding position
  against an external physical force. Working candidate; no general damage,
  Strength, Toughness, or Stability penalty. Adequate anchoring can answer it.

The equal-looking feature counts are not a valuation method. Neither dwarf
density nor elf lightness receives an additional unlisted benefit here.
Both packages use the same ordinary construction budgets and starting Scale.

## Paired Probes

For numerical rows, hold the pre-trait rating at 60 for an uncertain permitted
attempt. These are isolated checks, not assigned universal Difficulties or
opposed-combat win probabilities. Routine activity remains automatic where
appropriate. Different methods of reaching an objective remain possible.

| Situation | Dwarf | Candidate elf |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sight in total darkness | Darkvision grants sight within its eventual bounds | Low-light sight alone does not |
| Sight in faint light | Darkvision can serve within its bounds | Low-light sight can serve |
| Notice available stone tool marks visually | 66% | 60% |
| Notice an available faint approaching footstep | 60% | 66% |
| Resist a naturally occurring poison | 66% | 60% |
| Jump a gap where Dense Build applies | 54% | 60% |
| Hold footing against a force where low mass is a disadvantage | 60%; no free density bonus | 54% |
| Cross loose snow under ordinary personal load | Ordinary approach; density penalty only if genuinely relevant | Granted snow passage addresses sinking |
| Cross slick ice over a strong support | No automatic density penalty | Snow permission does not answer slipperiness |
| Cross a fragile support | Density Dissonance if applicable | No automatic lightness bonus or permission |
| Buoyancy-dependent attempt | 54% where Dense Build applies | 60%; no automatic flotation benefit |
| Negotiate unrelated terms | No package modifier | No package modifier |

No percentage is assigned to permission alone. Ordinary characters can seek
light, snowshoes, alternate routes, and other legitimate answers. Traits do
not impose penalties after the actual problem has been resolved.

## Findings

1. Neither package dominates all the tested situations. The candidate elf has
   useful routes independent of the dwarf's stone and poison strengths.
2. Matching x1.10/x0.90 does not establish equal provision. It only makes the
   numerical size of one applicable trait comparable at the same base rating.
3. Darkvision is more permissive than low-light sight in the tested lighting
   situations. Snow passage is an additional, differently scoped permission;
   its value cannot be inferred from having the same number of listed traits.
4. Campaign emphasis matters. Stone exploration and natural toxins exercise
   dwarf advantages; snowy travel exercises an elf permission. These examples
   establish relevance, not invented encounter-frequency weights.
5. The proposed Light Build burden may be less frequent than Dense Build's
   combined jumping, buoyancy, and support concerns. An occasional displacement
   hazard should not be presumed equal to those recurring traversal burdens.
   The fixture therefore does not justify declaring the packages balanced.

Verification: twelve narrative applicability probes and reuse of the existing
percentile enumeration. At rating 60, x1.10 yields 66 successes per 100 rolls;
x0.90 yields 54, versus 60 without a trait. No empirical session playtest or
whole-package frequency simulation performed.

## Decision Boundary

The user subsequently retained this as a second working comparison package.
Final setting adoption and equal starting provision remain unresolved. Do not alter the dwarf,
invent additional dwarf weaknesses, price Access from trait counts, or create
a mandatory provision pool to settle this comparison.

## Follow-On: Practical Limitation Burden

Purpose: compare opportunities for application, consequences, and credible
countermeasures without changing provisions. These are narrative scenario
probes, not empirical frequency measurements or assertions about real species
physiology. Use each package's specified fictional relationship.

| Scenario | Dense Build | Light Build | Countermeasure or scope control |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Jump a broken stair or gap | Applies to the jump | No penalty from this trait | A usable plank or alternate route can replace the jump |
| Cross a support sensitive to the dwarf's weight | Applies if an uncertain crossing remains possible | No automatic bonus | Reinforcement or another route can answer the load problem |
| Swim where maintaining buoyancy is the problem | Applies | No automatic flotation bonus | Adequate flotation or a suitable boat can answer buoyancy |
| Resist an opponent's attempt to shove the Character from position | No automatic density bonus | Applies when low mass hinders that resistance | An effective anchor or different response may avoid reliance on bodily mass |
| Hold a position against a strong current | Not automatically a buoyancy problem; depends on method | Applies where current displaces the unanchored Character | Securing to a sound anchor can answer displacement |
| Stay upright during violent deck movement | No blanket balance penalty | Applies if that movement creates the defined mass-related displacement disadvantage | Secure support can answer displacement; lightness alone does not penalize ordinary walking |
| Resist injury from an ordinary blow | Neither a listed Dense Build penalty nor a free bonus | No automatic damage penalty | An attempt to displace is distinct from merely causing injury |
| Walk on solid ground, make a normal attack, or converse | Inactive | Inactive | Do not create checks to make limitations appear |

These eight rows are not equally probable events and must not be tallied as
equal units of disadvantage. A shove remains subject to the existing opposed
Expression procedure; no new check, defense rule, damage rider, or success
probability is assigned here.

### Burden Assessment

- Breadth: Dense Build has three explicit triggers. Light Build has one
  physical relationship that can recur through opponents and environmental
  forces. Three labels versus one label is not a three-to-one valuation.
- Exposure: Dense Build can complicate choosing a traversal route. Light
  Build can become a weakness an opponent deliberately exploits when holding
  position matters. Neither is restricted to contrived scenes, but neither
  has a measured session frequency.
- Stakes: either can matter greatly near a drop or during a crossing. Neither
  grants an automatic catastrophic result; consequences still follow the
  declared action and ordinary resolution.
- Countermeasures: both can be bypassed or addressed. Route changes, flotation,
  supports, and anchoring may require equipment, opportunity, time, or another
  approach. No new mandatory action cost or equipment price is assigned.
- Persistence: a countermeasure works only while it answers the actual
  problem, but must not be denied merely to preserve a balancing penalty.
- Numerical severity: both retain x0.90 when applicable. Their per-check
  arithmetic is identical at identical incoming ratings; practical burden is
  not. No magnitude change is recommended from these probes.

### Whole-Package Interactions

Stone Observation can help the dwarf identify a weak stone support before
committing to it; identifying the problem does not reinforce the support.
Keen Hearing may alert the elf to an approaching opponent; it does not itself
anchor the elf against a later shove. Snow passage addresses sinking into
snow, not displacement by an external force. Darkvision and low-light sight
may reveal safer routes without automatically cancelling a bodily limitation.

### Conclusion and Next Boundary

Light Build is a credible, exploitable limitation, not merely decorative.
Its practical burden is not established as equal to Dense Build. In a
traversal-focused adventure Dense Build has more explicitly named triggers;
in positional conflict Light Build may recur through deliberate opposition.
These are conditional expectations, not observed frequency claims.

Retain both working packages unchanged. There is no basis here to broaden
Light Build into general frailty, increase its penalty, or reduce dwarf
provisions. The remaining equivalence question concerns complete packages
within an intended Campaign Profile, not matching the two drawbacks in
isolation. Further isolated limitation probes would not establish that common
starting provision by themselves.
