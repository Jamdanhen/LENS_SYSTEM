# Simple HOLT Scope Estimate Test

Status: Approved as a provisional HOLT scope-estimation aid. Actual prices and
Leverage treatment remain unresolved. The original test below is preserved;
its illustrative packages are not adopted HOLT definitions.

## Objective

Test an easy estimate, not an exact pricing formula:

```text
Count the distinct Derived Attribute areas the defined HOLT package permits.
Multiply that count by 5.
Use the result as a rough scope reference.
```

The original twenty areas remain the 100-unit reference for this experiment.
Including Leverage at the same rate is a comparison assumption, not a decision
that Leverage must be priced this way. Units are not actual advancement points.

## Source and Fixtures

The baseline draft's Axis section lists these illustrative relationships:

- Strength: Strength, Movement, Stability, Projection.
- Sea: Movement, Awareness, Connection, Control, Assets.

Those examples are not exhaustive permission maps. The test treats each list
as the complete scope of a deliberately bounded sample package. It does not
declare that every Strength HOLT has four areas or every Sea HOLT has five.
Other packages below are explicit count fixtures, not setting decisions.

## Arithmetic Results

| Sample package | Areas | Estimate units |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Strength, limited to the four listed routes | 4 | 20 |
| Sea, limited to the five listed routes | 5 | 25 |
| Strength, limited to Strength and Stability | 2 | 10 |
| Permission through Might and Mobility only | 10 | 50 |
| Permission through Leverage only | 5 | 25 |
| Original four Attribute families | 20 | 100 |
| All five Attribute families | 25 | 125 |

The first two estimates say only that five counted routes receive a higher
scope estimate than four. They do not establish Sea's full value relative to
Strength. The two-route variant halves the four-route estimate. The physical
ten-route fixture retains the user's illustrative half-reference result.

## Application Checks

### Repeated Uses

Lifting, pushing, and striking through the same Strength area count once, not
three times. Task, Field, and Specialty choices do not multiply the area count.
Adding a distinct Toughness permission to the four-route package changes its
estimate from 20 to 25.

Result: the estimator measures breadth of areas without requiring a catalogue
of every application.

### Permission Versus Investment

A character who has developed only one of four permitted Axes still has a
four-area package for this scope estimate. The baseline explicitly permits
Open, unranked Axes without a HOLT numerical modifier. Counting only purchased
Axis Ranks would understate the package's permission and future development
scope. This is a scope estimate, not a proposed Axis advancement price.

An area available under a stated Access Condition still belongs to the
package's possible scope. Counting it does not settle the value of that
limitation. Temporary Closed Access does not retrospectively change what was
purchased. Areas the package never permits are absent from its scope.

Result: count the defined package, not the character's current scene or spending.

### Different Permissions in the Same Area

A package permitting only underwater movement and one permitting movement
through air and water could both count Movement once. Holding other aspects
equal, the latter has extra permission that the area count cannot distinguish.

Result: equal estimates identify comparable counted breadth, not identical
capability. This is a concrete reason to retain the user's estimate status;
no weighted subcategory formula is added to compensate.

### Uncertain Areas

A disputed fifth area changes a four-area estimate from 20 to 25: five units,
but a 25% relative change. The same additional area changes 100 to 105: 5%.

Result: small packages are more sensitive to routing judgments. Where one
route remains unsettled, show 20-25 rather than invent a fractional weight or
claim a precise result. The arithmetic remains one count and one multiplication.

### Overlap Inside One Package

The two source sample lists share Movement. Their union contains eight areas,
for 40 units, not nine areas for 45. This is a duplicate-count check within one
hypothetical package. It is not a discount or combination rule for separately
purchased HOLTs, whose thematic permissions can remain distinct.

## Verification and Assessment

The companion PowerShell script executed successfully: seven expected package
totals plus three duplicate, expansion, and union checks passed. Narrative
application checks above are design analysis, not simulated play results.

Assessment: viable as a simple first-pass scope estimate for a defined package.
The calculation is easy, responds predictably to added or removed areas, and
does not need weighted categories. The remaining work is defining the permitted
scope, which cannot be obtained from the HOLT's name alone.

Decision: the user approved count x 5 as a provisional estimation aid for
package comparisons. Do not derive actual XP prices, automatic limitation
discounts, or final Leverage treatment from this approval.

## First Comparison After Approval

Use the four-route Strength fixture above, holding its manifestation permissions,
Scale, and development assumptions constant except for the stated variation.
The Access Conditions below are test fixtures, not adopted setting packages.

| Variant | Permitted areas | Scope estimate | Distinct consideration |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| All four routes Open | 4 | 20 | Reference package |
| Projection absent from the package | 3 | 15 | Narrower available scope |
| All four available only with a removable enabling artifact | 4 | 20 | Conditional Access; artifact dependence remains to evaluate |
| All four available only during the full moon | 4 | 20 | Conditional Access; timing restriction remains to evaluate |

Removing a permitted route reduces the scope estimate directly. Restricting
when otherwise identical routes are accessible does not remove their potential
scope. The equal estimates for the conditional variants are therefore not a
conclusion that their limitations have no value.

This comparison separates two questions without adding a formula: how much
scope is available, and under what conditions it can be used. A removable
artifact and a full-moon restriction also cannot be ranked by their names
alone; their significance depends on the actual package and campaign.

Next substantive discussion: how to recognize meaningful Access restrictions
when comparing packages. No restriction discount or final price is assigned.
