# LENS Defense Method Task Test v0.1

Date: 2026-09-03

Status: Experimental comparison, with the subsequent user-confirmed working
decision that Shooting and Throwing are distinct Tasks. Other candidate Task
names, boundaries, and purchases remain under discussion. No baseline rules
are changed by this record.

## Question

Can Evasion, Guarding, Parrying, Withstanding, and Countering serve as distinct
practiced Tasks without duplicating each other or recreating universal Defend?

## Controls And Limits

- Task, portable Parent, and parent-bound Branch remain the candidate Training
  layers. A Branch must have a ranked Parent; it may exceed that Parent's Rank.
- Rank 0 supplies x1.0. Each Rank adds 0.1; Rank 10 supplies x2.0.
- Each Rank costs one Training Point. The active test pool remains 100 points.
- Unranked ordinary attempts remain available through the applicable Attribute
  and Derived Attribute. Missing training is not Closed Access.
- Permission, Scale, equipment, and fiction must support the actual method.
- Every defensive method uses the existing single opposed resolution. No
  additional damage, soak, parry, or resistance contest is introduced.
- No automatic damage reduction, counterattack, recovery, or free action is
  awarded for selecting a method.
- The examples are an applicability map, not an exhaustive permission list.

This is a conceptual scenario test and an exact local point-allocation
comparison, not a combat simulation or proof of whole-game balance. The
numerical comparison holds Difficulty, Scale, equipment, current conditions,
and opposition out of the calculation. Its outputs are prepared partial-stack
ratings, not hit chances or final outcomes.

Two qualifications matter when interpreting the preceding Attack and Defend
tests:

1. A Task with physical scope does not itself create a separate subsystem.
   Universality means shared rules, not that every Task must apply in every
   Attribute family. Its scope must still be justified.
2. A portable Parent can contribute outside defense. Comparing a broad Task
   with a Parent and Branch in one defensive use does not measure their entire
   value. Nor does natural toughness or training in one subject automatically
   give a Character ranks in a general defensive Task.

## 1. Evasion

Tested activity: avoid an approaching threat by changing where or when the
Character is exposed to it.

The existing Dodging teaching record supplies the starting cases:

| Route | Expression |
|---|---|
| Mobility x Responsiveness | Move out of a swing as it develops. |
| Mobility x Maneuverability | Twist through a narrow gap in the attack's path. |
| Psyche x Insight | Read the opponent's setup and leave the threatened position before the strike. |

The practiced contribution is managing exposure, timing, and an escape route.
The Derived Attribute identifies whether reactive speed, bodily maneuvering,
or anticipation is carrying the particular attempt.

Boundary case: a pinned Character who cannot move out of the threatened space
cannot evade that blow merely by having a high Evasion Rank. Another defense
may remain available. This is a particular fictional limitation, not a blanket
closure of every Mobility Expression.

Extraordinary case: a permitted short-range displacement could express the
same evasive intention. Evasion does not grant displacement or supply the
necessary HOLT Axis development. Whether existing evasive practice contributes
depends on how that displacement is actually controlled.

Non-example: calling a speech an evasion does not by itself make physical
Dodging practice applicable. A genuinely relevant unexpected route can still
be argued through the ordinary applicability rule.

Result: strong Task candidate. Keep Evasion as the current test label;
whether it replaces or broadens the already documented Dodging is unresolved.
No need to require separate reactive and anticipatory purchases solely because
they use different Derived Attributes.

## 2. Guarding

As an unrestricted word, Guarding can mean protecting anything by any method:
watching a door, concealing a secret, avoiding a blow, hiring security, or
maintaining a magical ward. That broad reading returns to the Defend problem.

Tested narrower activity: put and maintain a usable body, tool, barrier, or
permitted effect between a threat and what it would affect.

| Route | Expression |
|---|---|
| Might x Strength | Hold a shield against the force trying to drive it aside. |
| Mobility x Coordination | Place a shield or weapon where it intercepts an incoming strike. |
| Mobility x Stability | Maintain the blocking position when impact threatens to displace it. |

A bodyguard can interpose on behalf of someone else using the same activity.
Whom the Character protects does not require a second Task. Covering several
people does not become free; existing scope and Effect rules still apply.

Boundary case: a shield cannot intercept a psychic intrusion merely because
both situations are defensive. A separately permitted mind ward may offer a
valid barrier, but the Task does not provide that ward or automatically prove
that shield training operates it.

Extraordinary case: a permission-bearing force barrier can be deliberately
placed across an attack's path. It uses the appropriate capability and learned
field; it does not gain another defensive roll because it is a barrier.

Result: viable when it means interposition or blocking, not all protection.
Guarding is only the working label for that narrower scope. No rename is
adopted.

## 3. Parrying

Tested activity: contact or otherwise engage an incoming threat and redirect
it away from its intended line.

| Route | Expression |
|---|---|
| Mobility x Coordination | Turn a blade away with precise placement. |
| Mobility x Responsiveness | Intercept an unexpected strike within its brief opening. |
| Psyche x Insight | Anticipate the attack line and arrange the deflection in advance. |

Parrying differs fictionally from stopping a force outright. That distinction
can change what equipment, capability, room, timing, or permission applies.
It does not automatically require a separately purchased broad Task.

A shield can redirect; a sword can stop or hold. Both blocking and parrying
involve placing an available intermediary to manage the incoming threat. The
portable Parent can identify Swordsmanship or Shield Fighting, and a
parent-bound Branch can distinguish a practiced form of deflection.

Boundary case: a sword that can turn another blade is not automatically able
to redirect an explosion or an intangible manifestation. Rank does not erase
the fictional requirements for engagement with the threat.

Result: clear method, but no demonstrated need for a separate mandatory Task
alongside the tested interposition version of Guarding. Compare one shared
Task with distinct Parents and Branches before charging for both Tasks.

When Parrying appears as a Branch in this experiment, it is bound to the
relevant Parent, not owned by the Task. Do not count an identically scoped
Task and Branch twice for the same trained contribution.

## 4. Withstanding

Preserve the accepted teaching definition:

> Practiced methods for enduring impact, pain, pressure, or punishment while
> preserving function or intended purpose.

| Route | Expression |
|---|---|
| Might x Toughness | Receive a blow while preserving bodily function. |
| Mobility x Stability | Remain planted while an external force tries to knock the Character aside. |
| Psyche x Resolve | Maintain intention under coercion or intimidation. |

The shared activity is maintaining function or purpose while exposed to the
pressure. It is neither moving clear nor placing an intermediary in its path.
This is narrative transfer of practiced endurance, not a claim that all
physical and mental training has identical real-world procedures.

Toughness supplies bodily durability. Resolve supplies internal intention.
Withstanding supplies the practiced response to receiving pressure. A naturally
tough Character does not automatically possess Withstanding Ranks; conversely,
Withstanding Ranks do not manufacture bodily toughness or psychic immunity.

Boundary cases:

- Withstanding does not heal existing wounds or remove Load simply because
  the Character continues functioning. Recovery remains a different action.
- Remaining in contact with a blow does not automatically concede the opposed
  roll or impose Wounded. Preventing the declared harm while receiving contact
  can be the defense's successful fictional result.
- Bodily endurance does not automatically neutralize poison, confer fire
  immunity, or answer a challenge to property ownership.

Extraordinary case: a Metal Form HOLT supplies the relevant permission and
Axis contribution for extreme durability. Withstanding may enhance the
practiced response without adding a second soak contest or automatic mitigation.

Result: strong Task candidate, consistent with the accepted anchor work.
Keep it available across justified physical and mental routes without turning
it into a synonym for every kind of defense.

## 5. Countering

The baseline already describes Countering as the immediate action or force
opposing an Expression. That function can be performed by evasion,
interposition, withstanding, a permitted opposing manifestation, and many
noncombat actions.

Examples:

- A shield intercepts a strike.
- A permitted control Expression disrupts a hostile manifestation.
- A Character challenges a false claim using evidence and an applicable
  learned activity.

These are all counters, but they do not establish a shared learned procedure
that needs an additional universal Countering multiplier.

Result: retain the existing rule/function. Do not add universal Countering as
a separate Task or stack it merely because another method opposed something.
Specific trained countermeasures remain possible through applicable Tasks,
Parents, Branches, equipment, or permission-bearing Lenses.

## Same Threat, Different Methods

A heavy strike approaches:

| Method | Declared approach | What could invalidate it here? |
|---|---|---|
| Evasion | Leave the threatened space. | No usable route or time to leave. |
| Interposition / blocking | Put a usable shield or weapon in its path. | No suitable intermediary or ability to bring it to bear. |
| Parrying | Engage and redirect the strike. | No way to engage it or redirect its path. |
| Withstanding | Receive it without losing the threatened function. | The declared endurance exceeds available permission or capability. |

Numerical difficulty and opposition still resolve hard but permitted attempts;
these are not automatic exclusions based merely on a threatening description.

All use the same opposed procedure. There are no type-specific dice, automatic
retaliations, or extra damage contests. A failed or inapplicable method does
not silently grant a second defensive attempt.

An account such as "I shift my footing and turn his blade" may describe one
coherent Expression. Listing two verbs does not itself earn two multiplied
Tasks. Genuinely distinct combined contributions use the existing compound
logic rather than a special rule for defenses.

## Local Point Test: Shared Versus Separate Blocking And Parrying

Use the same diagnostic core for each row:

```text
Attribute 5 x Derived Attribute Rank 5 (x1.5) = 7.5
```

Equal cores isolate Training choices; they do not claim every actual defense
uses the same Attribute. All builds spend twelve Training Points.

| Model | Task Ranks | Swordsmanship Parent | Parrying Branch |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Shared Task | Interposition/Guarding 6 | 4 | 2 |
| Split, balanced | Blocking 3; Parrying 3 | 4 | 2 |
| Split, parry specialist | Blocking 0; Parrying 6 | 4 | 2 |

In the split models the Branch represents a narrower sword-parrying technique,
not a second copy of the complete Parrying Task. Labels are diagnostic only.

| Model | Sword parry | Sword block | Shield block, no relevant Parent | Unrelated dodge | Sword attack, unranked attacking Task |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Shared Task | 20.16 | 16.80 | 12.00 | 7.50 | 10.50 |
| Split, balanced | 16.38 | 13.65 | 9.75 | 7.50 | 10.50 |
| Split, parry specialist | 20.16 | 10.50 | 7.50 | 7.50 | 10.50 |

Example arithmetic for the shared model:

```text
Sword parry: 7.5 x 1.6 x 1.4 x 1.2 = 20.16
Sword block: 7.5 x 1.6 x 1.4       = 16.80
Shield block: 7.5 x 1.6           = 12.00
Unrelated dodge: 7.5             =  7.50
Sword attack: 7.5 x 1.4          = 10.50
```

The portable Swordsmanship Parent still supports a relevant sword attack. It
was not priced solely for defending. For the unrelated-dodge control, no
sword-specific trained contribution is invoked; a different fictional case
could justify one.

Findings:

1. A shared interception Task does not create universal defense: its Rank is
   absent from the unrelated dodge and bodily or mental endurance attempts.
2. Parent and Branch still differentiate sword parries, other sword defenses,
   and use of an unfamiliar shield.
3. Splitting one Rank 6 Task into two Rank 3 Tasks reduces the matching ratings
   by 18.75 percent in this equal-cost comparison.
4. A specialist in the split model can keep the same peak by giving up broader
   blocking development. Splitting is a breadth cost, not an inherent reduction
   of achievable power.
5. These numbers do not prove the split unfair. The fictional transfer test
   must establish whether that extra breadth warrants a separate purchase.
   The present examples have not demonstrated that need.

## Recommendation

Retain three provisional practiced responses for the next Task mapping:

```text
Evasion
Avoid exposure through position or timing.

Interposition / blocking
Engage the threat with a usable intermediary, including stopping or
redirecting it. Guarding remains the current working label under review.

Withstanding
Maintain function or intention while exposed to the pressure.
```

Keep Parrying as a valid method within the interception candidate, with
specific technique development available through the existing Parent/Branch
architecture. Keep Countering as the established opposing function, not a
universal Training purchase.

This is a recommendation, not a locked three-defense catalogue. It does not
reopen the Derived Attributes, require every defense to fit one of three
labels, or prohibit a narratively justified alternative. The substantive next
decision is whether the shared interception scope earns one Task before naming
it or expanding the broader Task map.

## Follow-Up: Paired Attack And Defense Cases

Status: Further scope testing requested by the user. The candidate Task names
below are not a new Skill catalogue or a separate combat subsystem. No
Difficulty, damage, or action-economy rule changes are included.

The user has also introduced Protect for defending another Character or an
object, and Counterattack as a compound Expression. Protect remains a Task
scope candidate; the counterattack cases do not independently settle action
costs or permit automatic retaliation.

### Heavy Blow, Placed Strike, And Headbutt

```text
Might x Strength x Striking
An overpowering blow with a heavy weapon.

Mobility x Coordination x Striking
A carefully placed weapon strike or punch.

Might x Toughness x Striking
A headbutt whose effectiveness comes from the durable striking body.
```

Different Parents and Branches distinguish weapons, styles, and narrower
methods. The Task does not automatically require Strength, and the headbutt
does not require a special damage procedure.

Against any of these attacks, an opponent might evade, intercept, or withstand
the threatened Effect when the particular circumstances support that defense.
There is no prescribed attack-type-versus-defense-type matching chart.

Finding: Striking is a strong candidate for practiced delivery of direct
impact or a weapon blow. Specific weapon handling and expertise still belong
in the relevant field and narrower development.

### Rifle Shot And Fire Bolt

```text
Mobility x Coordination x Shooting x Firearms x Rifles

Influence x Projection x Shooting x Fire Magic x Fire Bolt
```

These are candidate routes, not a declaration that every fire manifestation
uses Shooting. A directed bolt has a plausible shared targeting-and-release
activity. A surrounding fire wall, spontaneous ignition, or engulfing aura is
not automatically the same activity just because it can harm an opponent.

The fire case requires its own permission-bearing Lens and applicable Axis
contribution. Those are not supplied by the displayed Training fragment.
Likewise, the firearm requires usable equipment and an available shot.

Evasion may avoid either attack. A barrier may intercept either only when it
is actually suitable. Withstanding is possible only through a coherent
capability and permitted attempt; a high Training Rank does not establish
immunity. Ordinary defensive equipment is not assumed to stop a fire bolt
because it can stop something else.

Finding: Shooting remains plausible across directed projectile and
projectile-like manifestations. Targeting should not become an additional
blanket multiplier merely because both attacks have a target. Whether
Targeting is a better name or a differently scoped Task is not decided here.

### Thrown Weapon Or Object

```text
Might x Strength x Throwing
Hurl an object with force.

Mobility x Coordination x Throwing
Place a thrown object accurately.
```

The same Task might place a rope, pass supplies, or throw a weapon. Its Rank
is not inherently offensive. The relevant field and Branch distinguish a
rescue throw, a sporting practice, and particular weapon training.

Throwing and Shooting both involve trajectory and release, but Throwing can
also involve bodily generation and shaping of the launch.

Subsequent user decision, 2026-09-03: Shooting and Throwing are definitely
distinct. Keep them as separate Tasks in the working model. The earlier
merge-versus-separate question is resolved; it is not pending another test.
This decision does not finalize every example, Parent, Branch, or remaining
Task boundary in this record.

### Grappling In Both Directions

```text
Might x Strength x Grappling
Overpower an opponent within a hold.

Mobility x Coordination x Grappling
Apply or undo a hold through placement and bodily leverage.
```

A wrestler may use Grappling to impose restraint, contest an incoming grip,
or escape an existing hold. The activity is managing bodily contact and holds;
its role changes with the declared Expression.

Evasion or interception may prevent initial contact. Once a hold is
established, an escape must address the actual restraint. Merely renaming a
preventive dodge does not erase existing Restrained Load.

Finding: Grappling is a strong Task candidate with both offensive and
defensive uses. A separate Defend purchase is not required solely because
this particular grapple opposes another Character's action.

### A Task Is Not Owned By An Offensive Or Defensive Role

A strike that knocks an attacking weapon aside may be a defensive use of
Striking when that is genuinely the trained action performed. A shot against
an incoming threat may be a defensive use of Shooting if the fiction permits
that attempt. These are possible applications, not automatic techniques or
permissions to intercept every projectile.

Likewise, Grappling may restrain or release, Throwing may harm or deliver aid,
and Withstanding may answer physical or mental pressure through its established
routes.

Finding: Evasion, interception, and Withstanding are useful defensive
candidates, not mandatory containers for every defensive Expression. Requiring
them solely because a Character is defending would recreate the ownership
problem that this Task architecture is intended to remove.

### Protect And Compound Counterattack

Protect case: a Character places a shield between a blow and an ally or
fragile object. Interception remains a plausible trained activity despite the
different protected target. Protect may be the declared purpose or a separately
developed Task candidate; the test does not require buying an extra Task
solely because the shield now protects someone else. The purchase scope remains
open.

Counterattack case: catch an incoming arm and turn that engagement into a
throw. The defensive and offensive functions are narratively integrated,
supporting the user's compound Expression proposal. Grappling may supply one
practiced contribution to both functions. Do not invent a second required Task
or count the same Task twice merely because the Expression has two functions.

Where distinct contributions are genuinely required, apply the existing
compound limitation and dependence rules. This test does not allocate full
independent Effect totals to both functions or authorize a free attack in
response to every defense. Timing, action cost, and any unresolved division of
Effects remain outside this scope comparison.

### Two-Way Parent Control

Use a diagnostic core of 7.5 for each attempt and fourteen local Training
Points:

```text
Striking 4
Guarding/interception 4
Swordsmanship Parent 4
Sword Parrying Branch 2
```

Resulting partial stacks:

```text
Sword strike:    7.5 x 1.4 x 1.4       = 14.70
Sword parry:     7.5 x 1.4 x 1.4 x 1.2 = 17.64
Unrelated strike, no Parent: 7.5 x 1.4 = 10.50
Unrelated dodge, no Training: 7.5      =  7.50
```

The Swordsmanship Parent contributes to both an attack and a defense. The
parrying Branch does not become an offensive bonus merely because the same
sword is used. Losing the sword removes those contributions from a subsequent
unarmed strike without erasing the Striking Task. Unranked evasion remains
possible at the unchanged core in its otherwise permissible circumstances.

This calculation shows meaningful distinction and fallback without universal
Attack or Defend multipliers. It is not a comparison of actual hit rates,
injury output, or complete equal-budget Characters.

### Paired-Test Finding

Striking and Grappling are strong next-stage candidates. Shooting and Throwing
are separate Tasks under the user's subsequent working decision. Targeting is
not an additional mandatory layer. Evasion, interception, and Withstanding
remain available defensive activities without owning all defenses.

The important result is that a Task identifies the practiced activity, not a
side of combat. Attack, defense, protection, and counterattack describe what
the resulting Expression is doing. Protect's eventual purchase status remains
open, and compound counterattack remains subject to the shared rules.
