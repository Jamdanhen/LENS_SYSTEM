# Preparation Action Investment Test

Status: User-approved working action accounting, adopted 2026-09-08.
Date: 2026-09-08.

## Objective and Authority

Compare aiming, a separate setup activity, and preparation for an open defensive
reaction. Recommend action accounting and interaction with Delay without extra
actions or changes to adopted preparation persistence.

Sources:
- `../system/lens-system-baseline-draft-v0.1.md`, Preparation.
- `../system/lens-terminology-quick-reference-v0.1.md`, Initiative and Multiple Actions.
- `lens-preparation-persistence-test-v0.1.md`.

The initiative reference labels persistent order, action passes, and open
reactions as working candidates. This test uses them as controls; it does not
promote the whole initiative system to final rules.

Established controls: preparation supplies assessed, specifically applicable
Resonance; Delay alone does not. Actual uncertain activities resolve normally.
Preparation persists while its supporting facts remain. Ordinary execution and
extraordinary Access are not purchased by declaring preparation.

## Alternatives

1. Treat any declared preparation during Delay as a free numerical benefit.
2. Charge an action for everything described as preparing, including ordinary
   execution already inside another action.
3. Account for the actual activity: ordinary execution stays inside its action;
   a distinct preparatory activity uses an action when action timing matters.
   Apply its established benefit to later eligible actions or reactions.

Alternative 1 makes even a brief delay a potential repeatable source of free
advantage. Waiting has timing risks, but no agreed minimum investment or benefit
scale makes this an established tradeoff. A declaration alone is insufficient.

Alternative 2 charges again for ordinary aiming, adopting a usable stance, or
bringing existing knowledge to bear. It also risks charging an in-combat action
for preparation already completed before combat. Those are not justified taxes.

The user adopted alternative 3. This is an accounting distinction, not a new Prepare
Task, a fixed preparation duration, or a guaranteed bonus for spending an action.

## Case 1: Aiming and Waiting

Ordinary aiming necessary to deliver a shot is included in the shot. It neither
costs an additional action nor supplies an extra preparation bonus by itself.

For additional deliberate preparation, such as establishing a specifically
advantageous supported sight picture, the proposed in-conflict accounting is:

- Spend an action on the distinct setup. A routine setup need not require a roll.
- Assess the advantage actually secured; payment of an action guarantees no value.
- The shot still uses an available action. Setup does not also reserve a free shot.
- That available shooting action can be delayed under the existing Delay model.
- A useful position already established does not require a new setup payment
  merely because combat started or because another shot is attempted.

One-action-per-round fixture, initial order A, B, C:

```text
Round 1: A establishes the setup; B acts; C acts.
Round 2: A delays the available shot; B acts; A shoots; C acts.
Later order: B, A, C, under persistent Delay.
```

A has used two actions across those rounds: setup and shot. No additional action
remains at A's old initiative position. B's intervening activity can alter the
advantage. If the shot is not possible or the desired opportunity never arrives,
no attack is owed merely because A previously prepared.

Existing preparation can also benefit a delayed shot without a new setup action.
That is persistence of an already established advantage, not free preparation
created by Delay. Time actually needed to establish an advantage remains part
of the fiction; this test does not make every setup achievable in one action.

## Case 2: Separate Distraction, Then Shot

Suppose the Character first creates a diversion and later shoots through the
opportunity it establishes. These are independently resolved activities, not
two names for part of ordinary shooting.

The diversion uses an action. Resolve genuine uncertainty or opposition normally.
Success may create an opportunity; assess its actual relevance to the shot.
There is no guaranteed Effect-to-Resonance payment. The shot uses another action.

With two declared actions and ordinary action passes, the diversion resolves in
the first pass and the shot in the second. Each rolled action uses its own Rating
and the existing two-action divisor. An automatic setup still occupies an action
if it is a distinct action-consuming activity; absence of a roll is not absence
of time. Opposition can respond before the later shot.

Delay can change when an available action occurs; it does not undo the divisor,
erase the setup expenditure, or turn a later shot into a free reaction. A setup
performed by an ally uses the ally's action. The beneficiary does not pay the
ally's setup cost again, and benefits only if the resulting facts help.

## Case 3: Preparation for an Open Defensive Reaction

Suppose a Character spends an action establishing a supported guard against an
anticipated frontal impact. Ordinary positioning needed merely to attempt a
block remains part of the block; the additional preparation must establish a
distinct advantage.

The setup uses the normal action. When the anticipated attack arrives, the
Character uses the existing open defensive reaction. Do not charge another
normal action for that defense, and do not create a second reaction alongside it.

Preparation changes the applicable defensive Expression, not the number of
reactions or the Character's initiative position. If several incoming attacks
meet the preparation's actual scope and its supporting facts remain, more than
one reaction can benefit. No one-use exception is added to persistence.

An attack from an unprepared direction may receive no preparation contribution;
an ordinary defensive attempt can remain available. Turning, moving, or being
displaced may change the setup under the adopted persistence rule. If the
anticipated attack never arrives, the setup action has still been used.

Preparing to shoot someone does not make shooting an open defensive reaction.
Counterattack or interrupt permissions cannot be obtained by renaming an attack.

## Rating Illustrations

These are deterministic illustrations, not probabilities, damage forecasts, or
proof that setup is always worth its action. Before-context Rating is 60; an
actually secured preparation contribution of +0.20 is a fixture, not a fixed price.

| Case | Resulting Rating | Action account |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary shot, one action | 60 | One shooting action |
| Free setup declared during Delay, then shot | 72 | One shooting action; illustrates the unpriced-benefit concern, not the recommendation |
| Distinct setup this round, prepared shot next round | 72 | Two actions across two rounds; setup replaced the earlier opportunity to shoot |
| Setup and shot as two declared actions this round | 36 | Prepared shot: 60 x 1.20 / 2; setup occupies the other action |
| Two ordinary shots this round | 30 each | Two actions, each divided by two |
| One setup action followed by an eligible open block | 72 | One normal action plus the existing defensive reaction |

A rolled setup with its own pre-divisor Rating of 60 becomes 30 in the two-action
case. The +0.20 in the later shot is conditional on actually establishing the
illustrated benefit, not guaranteed by that setup Rating.

The defensive example introduces no new multiple-action penalty for open
reactions. Preparation does not change any other applicable defensive modifiers.
How other multi-action configurations affect reactions is not resolved here.

## Findings and Decision Boundary

Adopted: charge the actual distinct setup, not the word preparation. Ordinary
execution is included in its Expression. Separate setup and later attack consume
separate actions; a prepared open defense remains an open reaction. Existing
preparation need not be repurchased to benefit from it again.

Delay relocates an available action. It does not refund spent setup, bank an
additional attack, grant Resonance by itself, or change initiative when merely
making an open defensive reaction. Keeping the benefit and keeping an unused
action are different facts.

This comparison supports coherent accounting, not a universal tactical payoff.
Value depends on the assessed advantage, its applicability and persistence,
the opportunity sacrificed, and what opposition does before it is used.

Precise trigger timing, whether a held attack can preempt an announced action,
and action-pass or round-boundary bookkeeping need their own timing decision.
Do not silently grant those permissions through preparation persistence.
The action-accounting decision is resolved without settling those timing questions.
