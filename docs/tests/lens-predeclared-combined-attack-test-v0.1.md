# Predeclared Combined Attack Test v0.1

Status: User-authorized candidate test, 2026-09-12. No new one-defense limit or
combined-exchange eligibility rule is adopted. The user's stated aim includes
greater output against advanced enemies; overkill against weaker opponents is
allowed and is not by itself a balance failure.

## Candidate

Participants predeclare one coordinated attack, target, intended shared effect,
and each contribution before their rolls. Each commits an available action;
actual timing and Delay remain necessary. Each resolves their full applicable
Expression. Compatible Effects pool, and a feasible whole-attack defense is
resolved once against the combined attack.

This is contrasted with separate attacks, each with its own feasible defensive
Expression. No retroactive pooling after inspecting outcomes is allowed in the
candidate. Swords and beams receive identical treatment at matching Ratings and
fictionally comparable scope. No automatic coordination roll is introduced.

Declaring a combined attack does not supply missing Access or automatically make
a defense that reaches one component capable of reaching all components. The
test does not settle every partial-defense opportunity or timing interaction.

## Probability Controls

Use complete Final Ratings, not Attributes or pre-Difficulty Expressions. All
ordinary equipment, applicable context, and Difficulty are already represented.
No armor mitigation is included in these probability rows; protection is tested
separately below. Ratings 60 / 30 / 120 / 200 / 250 are sensitivity controls, not
new character builds, tier definitions, or canonical superhero statistics.

Separate defense rolls are independent. All Ratings remain fixed through the
comparison, even when earlier harm might change later actions in a real
encounter. This excludes that sequential advantage and other feedback effects.

Natural 01/00 are included. The repeated-01 distribution is expanded through
five continuation depths. The remaining 1e-12 probability is retained, with only
its further output censored. Across at most ten rolls, event-probability error
is bounded by 1e-11 and mean-output error by 6e-11, apart from floating-point
arithmetic. Complication consequences are not simulated or canceled by teamwork.

## Results

Chance of at least one uncancelled offensive Effect, before mitigation:

| Attackers' Final Ratings | Defender Rating | Separate | Combined |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Two at 60 | 30 | 78.60% | 78.38% |
| Two at 60 | 120 | 13.10% | 20.29% |
| Three at 60 | 120 | 18.99% | 36.64% |
| Five at 60 | 120 | 29.60% | 66.26% |
| Three at 60 | 200 | 2.22% | 4.14% |
| Three at 120 | 250 | 3.53% | 59.69% |
| One at 40, one at 80 | 120 | 17.23% | 23.07% |

Combining does not universally increase the chance of any harm. A whole-attack
defense can stop the shared result on one strong defensive roll, whereas
separate defenses create multiple opportunities for a defensive failure.
Against the weaker defender, that difference is small, and expected output is
3.66 separate versus 3.82 combined.

Against Rating 120, two Rating-60 attackers produce expected output of 0.30
separately versus 0.66 combined; three produce 0.46 versus 1.55; five produce
0.76 versus 4.32. This is a deliberate pooling benefit, not merely an accounting
restatement. Against sufficiently superior defense, a small team may still
produce little: three Rating-60 attackers versus Rating 200 remain near 4%
for any uncancelled output even when combined.

The high-output beam fixture illustrates substantial teamwork leverage against
a defender who individually suppresses almost all of each contributor's attack.
It does not establish that particular fictional characters have these Ratings.

## Overkill and Flexibility

Suppose this particular target's remaining participation ends after three more
harm Effects. This is a scenario condition, not a new universal defeat threshold.
An initial separate attack producing four against defense one supplies three,
enough to resolve that target. A later actor whose action is not yet committed
retains their ordinary choices, including another target if available.

The jointly committed four-plus-three attack against defense one supplies six,
but both actions were spent on that target. Excess harm is not transferred to
another enemy. No prohibition on such overkill is needed. The tactical cost is
actual commitment and lost flexibility, not an invented damage ceiling.

This does not quantify how often spare targets, useful alternate actions, or
timing opportunities exist. On one durable target with no reason to act sooner,
combining may be the better choice; that supports the user's intended use rather
than automatically invalidating it.

## Counter-Effects

The existing one-counter-effect limit is per resolved exchange, when the fiction
supports a counter. Moving from several exchanges to one changes this benefit
as well as offensive cancellation.

For two Rating-60 attacks against Rating 120, the expected number of exchanges
with eligible defensive surplus falls from approximately 1.78 separately to
0.73 combined. These are possible counter-effects, not automatic damage or free
retaliatory attacks. The combined exchange does not award one counter per
contributor or copy its counter onto everyone.

This reduction in counter exposure is part of the candidate's actual advantage
and should not be hidden when assessing the value of coordinated commitment.

## Interruption

Four plus three Effects against five defense leave two. If a legitimate
intervention prevents the second contribution, four against five leave none.
If an actual earlier intervention cancels two of that contribution's three,
four plus one against five also leave none. These are distinct already-permitted
interventions, not free extra responses granted by this test.

Failure does not inherently break every shared attack: four plus zero against
one defense still leave three. Actions that actually began are not refunded.
Unbegun actions retain the established ordinary timing treatment. Existing
independently continuing projectiles are not retroactively erased by disabling
their Source after release.

## Defensive Scope

A feasible defense answering the whole four-plus-three attack with five Effects
leaves two. A defense that can answer only the first sword cancels at most its
four; the second sword's three remain. The surplus one cancellation cannot
silently move to a component the method cannot reach.

The main probability comparison assumes a whole-attack response is feasible.
Whether a limited response is followed by another eligible response inside the
proposed combined exchange is not settled by those probability numbers. A
universal one-defense restriction must not be presented as already adopted.

## Protection and Allocation

Pooling opposition must not automatically merge physically distinct sword hits
for per-hit protection, nor make genuinely merged beams separate merely because
several Sources participated. Preserve actual form, application, and protection
scope. Ordinary equipment modifiers are not added again after resolution.

The test exposes an allocation question. Four-plus-three Effects, after three
whole-attack cancellation Effects, leave four. If two actual hits each encounter
one point of matching mitigation:

- Remaining hits 2 and 2 yield 1 and 1: two harm.
- Remaining hits 4 and 0 yield 3 and 0: three harm.

Both allocations conserve output, but they do not produce the same result after
per-hit protection. The defender's actual method may identify the affected
component; an undifferentiated pool does not always do so. This record supplies
no silent allocation preference, universal armor bypass, or extra mitigation.

## Recommendation and Next Boundary

Retain predeclared combined attacks as a promising candidate for concentrated
output against stronger enemies. Do not reject them because weaker enemies can
be overkilled, and do not add an arbitrary coordination surcharge from these
results. Declaration, action commitment, timing, and genuine shared purpose must
be established before the rolls, not selected afterward for advantage.

Before adopting a universal single-defense exchange, resolve limited defensive
coverage and traceable cancellation across distinct protected hits. The test
supports the intended payoff, but does not erase those implementation decisions.
This is an encounter-opening probability comparison plus scenario fixtures,
not a full tactical simulation, playtest, or proof that commitment alone balances
every situation or group size.
