## Purpose

This package consolidates the current LENS discussion, existing project
records, and mathematical tests concerning character capability above Rank 10.

It explains:

- why Rank 10 remains the character-creation ceiling;
- why capability above Rank 10 is limited rather than eliminated;
- how higher Rank Horizons may support long-form heroic and epic advancement;
- what must be true before a higher Horizon becomes available;
- what unlocking a Horizon does and does not provide;
- how Ranks 12, 14, 16, 18, and 20 interact with Difficulty and Scale;
- which parts of the proposal are established, proposed, or still deferred.

This package does not adopt a completed advancement economy. Exact advancement
currency, awards, threshold prices, and pacing remain unresolved.

---

## Executive Proposal

```text
Rank 10 remains the absolute character-creation ceiling.

No character begins with an Attribute Rating, Derived Attribute Rank, Skill
Rank, Specialization Rank, HOLT Rank, or Axis Rank above 10.

Higher Ranks are not eliminated.

Campaign Profiles designed for prolonged heroic, mythic, superhuman, or epic
development may permit later Rank Horizons at:

Rank 12
Rank 14
Rank 16
Rank 18
Rank 20

A higher Horizon is unavailable until its unlock criteria are satisfied.

Unlocking a Horizon raises the maximum Rank that may subsequently be
purchased. It grants no free Ranks, does not change Scale, and does not create
new permissions by itself.
```

The proposed sequence creates five two-Rank advancement bands beyond the
ordinary Rank 10 Horizon.

## Current Authority Boundary

### Locked For The Current System Baseline

```text
Rank 10 is available during character creation.

LENS does not impose a universal starting ceiling below Rank 10.

Rank 10 is the current character-creation ceiling.

The existing character-creation architecture remains 25 Attribute Points,
50 global Derived Attribute Points, and 75 shared Training Points.

Increasing an Attribute after character creation does not grant free Derived
Attribute Points.

A Derived Attribute cannot exceed its parent Attribute.

A Specialization cannot exceed its parent Skill.

Higher Rank does not independently grant permission or change Scale.
```

### Existing Cost Principle

The current baseline does not make an individual Rank more expensive merely
because its numerical value is higher.

This principle remains the starting control for the higher-Horizon proposal:

```text
Rank cost remains consistent.

The major additional cost belongs to qualifying for and unlocking the next
Rank Horizon, not to a hidden surcharge placed on every Rank inside it.
```

This remains subject to testing against the eventual advancement economy.

### Proposed, Not Adopted

```text
Rank 12, 14, 16, 18, and 20 as sequential higher Horizons.

A character-level Horizon unlock rather than a separate unlock for every
Attribute, Skill, HOLT, or other ranked capability.

Campaign permission plus an experiential, narrative, or hybrid unlock gate.

The continued x0.1-per-Rank progression above Rank 10.
```

### Deferred

```text
Exact advancement currency.

Exact advancement awards and expected pacing.

Exact numerical price of each Horizon unlock.

Whether all Campaign Profiles allow every proposed Horizon.

Whether Rank 20 is the permanent system maximum.

Whether a particular setting may allow only selected Rank-bearing layers to
cross Rank 10.
```

## Terminology Control

The current construction language distinguishes:

```text
Attribute Rating
The broad numerical base of an Expression.

Stable Lens Rank
The invested Rank of a Derived Attribute, Skill, Specialization, HOLT Axis,
or another ranked Lens.
```

This package uses `Rank Horizon` as a convenient umbrella for the highest
Attribute Rating or stable Lens Rank currently available to the character.
It does not settle whether final publication should use `Rating Horizon` for
Attributes and `Rank Horizon` for all other layers.

## Why Rank 10 Is Limited At Character Creation

Rank 10 is not restricted because beginning characters must be inexperienced.
A starting character may already be one of the best people in the setting at
a chosen field.

The restriction exists because Rank 10 already represents complete development
inside the ordinary construction horizon.

A fully aligned Rank 10 Expression produces:

```text
Attribute 10
x Derived Attribute Rank 10: x2.0
x Skill Rank 10:             x2.0
x Specialization Rank 10:    x2.0
= Base Expression 80
```

The current point economy permits focused characters to begin with one or more
Rank 10 construction lines. That concentration is paid for through reduced
breadth elsewhere.

Opening Rank 11-20 during character creation would be materially different.
Because the complete Expression stack is multiplicative, continued linear
Rank increases compound into rapidly accelerating final values.

Under the proposed continuation:

```text
Rank 10 = x2.0 for each ranked Lens
Rank 12 = x2.2
Rank 14 = x2.4
Rank 16 = x2.6
Rank 18 = x2.8
Rank 20 = x3.0
```

Rank 20 is therefore not merely twice Rank 10 in a fully aligned Expression.
It produces 6.75 times the Rank 10 output.

The character-creation ceiling protects:

- the distinction between ordinary mastery and post-baseline development;
- room for meaningful vertical advancement in long campaigns;
- the significance of Difficulty and opposition at the beginning of play;
- the mechanical distinction between focused Rank and natural Scale;
- Campaign Profile control over heroic and epic power;
- the setting's decision about whether such development is possible at all.

---

## Why Ranks Above 10 Are Not Eliminated

Removing all development above Rank 10 would solve the inflation risk, but it
would also remove several valuable LENS functions.

Higher Rank Horizons may allow:

- a completed specialist to continue developing vertically;
- long-running campaigns to depict genuine transformation rather than only
  horizontal accumulation;
- heroic characters to challenge higher-Scale opposition through exceptional
  focus without inheriting the opposition's full Scale;
- superhuman, mythic, or Herald-like campaigns to remain inside the common
  LENS Rank grammar;
- ratings above 100 to retain capability against Difficulty, Dissonance, and
  opposition;
- a character to become extraordinary in one capability without making every
  capability extraordinary;
- the system to avoid introducing a separate Paragon multiplier or another
  exceptional-power subsystem.

The design objective is therefore controlled availability, not prohibition.

## Proposed Rank Horizons

| Horizon | Maximum Available Rank | Function |
|---|---:|---|
| Baseline | 10 | Character creation and ordinary mastery |
| Horizon I | 12 | First exceptional development band |
| Horizon II | 14 | Established heroic development |
| Horizon III | 16 | Major superhuman or mythic development |
| Horizon IV | 18 | Advanced epic development |
| Horizon V | 20 | Extreme campaign-end or cosmically relevant focus |

The labels are operational placeholders. They are not setting-facing titles,
character levels, or adopted published terminology.

Campaign Profiles may stop at any Horizon.

Examples:

```text
A grounded campaign may never exceed Rank 10.

A long heroic campaign may permit Rank 12 or Rank 14.

A high-powered superhuman campaign may permit Rank 16.

An epic or cosmically engaged campaign may eventually permit Rank 18 or
Rank 20.
```

The existence of Horizon V does not require every campaign to reach it.

## Proposed Unlock Criteria

A higher Rank Horizon should open only when all criteria selected by the
Campaign Profile have been satisfied.

### 1. Campaign Permission

The Campaign Profile must state that development beyond the current Horizon
is possible.

If the Campaign Profile ends at Rank 10, accumulated advancement alone cannot
force the campaign into an extraordinary Rank range.

### 2. Sequential Access

Horizons open in order.

```text
Rank 12 must be opened before Rank 14.
Rank 14 must be opened before Rank 16.
Rank 16 must be opened before Rank 18.
Rank 18 must be opened before Rank 20.
```

A character cannot skip directly from the Rank 10 Horizon to Rank 20.

### 3. Demonstrated Development

The character must have reached the current Horizon in at least one capability
that makes the next Horizon relevant.

This prevents a character from paying to unlock a ceiling that has not yet
constrained any actual development.

### 4. Major Unlock Requirement

The Campaign Profile selects one of three procedures:

```text
Experiential Unlock
The character commits a large, bounded amount of earned advancement toward
opening the next Horizon.

Narrative Unlock
A major event changes what the character can become. Examples may include
transformation, revelation, extraordinary training, contact with a source of
power, irreversible sacrifice, or another setting-appropriate development.

Hybrid Unlock
The character must satisfy both a major advancement threshold and a narrative
requirement.
```

The choice belongs to the Campaign Profile because grounded training,
superhuman evolution, cybernetic reconstruction, magical ascension, and
Herald-like development do not need identical fictional explanations.

### 5. Purchase After Unlock

Unlocking a Horizon grants no Attribute Ratings or stable Lens Ranks.

It only raises the ceiling that future advancement may purchase.

Example:

```text
A character unlocks Horizon I.

The character may now purchase Rank 11 and Rank 12.

The character does not automatically receive either Rank.
```

### 6. Existing Parent Limits

All existing relationships remain in force above Rank 10.

```text
A Derived Attribute cannot exceed its parent Attribute.

A Specialization cannot exceed its parent Skill.

Increasing an Attribute does not grant free Derived Attribute Points.

Unlocking a Horizon does not open Closed Access or satisfy a Conditional
Access requirement.
```

### 7. Fictional Applicability

Higher Rank never overrides the Applicability Rule.

The fiction must honor what the character's Lenses can do, and the character
must honor what the fiction says those Lenses cannot currently do.

Rank increases effectiveness when the ranked Lens applies. Rank does not make
the Lens universally applicable.

## Recommended Unlock Structure

The cleanest current proposal is a character-level Rank Horizon.

```text
The character has one current maximum Rank Horizon.

The Campaign Profile establishes the highest Horizon the campaign can
eventually support.

The character unlocks each permitted Horizon sequentially.

Every Attribute, Derived Attribute, Skill, Specialization, HOLT, or Axis must
still be advanced separately within that Horizon.
```

This avoids separate Horizon bookkeeping for every capability while ensuring
that the unlock itself provides no free numerical development.

A setting-specific rule may later test localized over-Horizon Access, but that
is not part of this proposal. The common procedure should be tested before
exceptions are introduced.

## Advancement Cost Structure

The proposal separates two costs.

### Horizon Unlock Cost

This is the large, bounded requirement that permits access to the next
two-Rank band.

Its exact value cannot be determined responsibly until LENS establishes:

- the advancement currency;
- the expected award per session or milestone;
- the cost of an ordinary Rank;
- the expected campaign length;
- whether the Campaign Profile uses experiential, narrative, or hybrid
  unlocking.

### Individual Rank Cost

After the Horizon opens, the character must purchase each desired Rank.

The current control keeps the ordinary Rank price consistent rather than
adding a surcharge solely because the Rank is 11 or higher.

Moving one completely aligned Expression from Rank 10 to Rank 12 requires:

```text
Attribute:          +2 Ratings
Derived Attribute:  +2 Ranks
Skill:              +2 Ranks
Specialization:     +2 Ranks

Total:
8 individual advances
```

Moving that complete line from Rank 10 to Rank 20 requires 40 individual
advances, in addition to unlocking all five higher Horizons.

The complete cost is therefore substantial even before exact advancement
prices are assigned.

## Mathematical Test Assumptions

The current test extends the existing progression without creating a second
curve:

```text
Rank N modifier = x(1.0 + 0.1N)
```

For a fully aligned line:

```text
Fully Aligned Expression =
Attribute Rating
x Derived Attribute modifier
x Skill modifier
x Specialization modifier
```

The test excludes HOLT, equipment, active Resonance, Dissonance, and other
Lenses so the Rank progression can be seen directly.

## Scale Interaction

The current active Scale test model is exponential x1.6.

Rank 10 fully aligned at increasing Scale Magnitudes produces:

| Scale Magnitude | Scale Modifier | Expression |
|---|---:|---:|
| M1 | x1.000000 | 80.000 |
| M2 | x1.600000 | 128.000 |
| M3 | x2.560000 | 204.800 |
| M4 | x4.096000 | 327.680 |
| M5 | x6.553600 | 524.288 |

Comparison:

```text
Fully aligned Rank 12 at Scale 1 = 127.776
Fully aligned Rank 10 at Scale 2 = 128.000

Fully aligned Rank 20 at Scale 1 = 540.000
Fully aligned Rank 10 at Scale 5 = 524.288
```

The numerical output is similar, but the capabilities are not identical.

```text
Higher Rank
= exceptional focus and developed effectiveness inside the character's
existing Scale frame.

Higher Scale
= a different natural scope, frame, perspective, reach, and order of
operation.
```

A Rank 20, Scale 1 character may generate enough focused output to affect or
resist something operating at a higher Scale. The character does not thereby
receive that being's natural scope or routine capability.

This is the principal reason not to eliminate higher Ranks: they may provide
the desired path for heroic cross-Scale action without redefining the
character's entire Scale.

## Rank And Scale Compounding Warning

Higher Rank and higher Scale multiply together.

Rank 20 produces:

| Scale Magnitude | Expression | Difficulty 10 Final Value |
|---|---:|---:|
| M1 | 540.000 | 5 |
| M2 | 864.000 | 8 |
| M3 | 1,382.400 | 13 |
| M4 | 2,211.840 | 21 |
| M5 | 3,538.944 | 34 |

These figures exclude HOLT, equipment, active Resonance, and other supporting
Lenses.

The combination is intentionally powerful, but it confirms why higher Rank
Horizons cannot be ordinary character-creation options and why Campaign
Profiles must control their availability.

## Relationship To Permission And Access

Higher Rank is not permission.

```text
Classification determines possibility.

HOLD establishes the setting's extraordinary-capability framework.

HOLT grants thematic permission.

Access determines whether a particular expression is Open, Conditional, or
Closed.

Scale determines the natural frame.

Rank determines developed effectiveness when the Lens applies.
```

Rank 20 Fire does not function underwater when the fiction and Access state say
the Fire Expression is unavailable there.

Rank 20 Strength does not grant flight merely because the rating is enormous.

Rank 20 Skill does not create an extraordinary permission absent the Lens that
permits the attempted Expression.

The Horizon changes how far development may proceed. It does not erase the
rest of the LENS architecture.

## Relationship To The Current Character-Creation Spine

The current baseline character-creation budgets remain:

```text
25 Attribute Points
50 global Derived Attribute Points
75 shared Training Points
```

All character-creation allocations remain subject to Rank 10.

The higher-Horizon proposal does not add over-cap points to these pools and
does not permit unused starting points to purchase Rank 11 or higher.

Higher Horizons belong to post-creation development only.

## Why This Is Not A Character-Level System

A Rank Horizon resembles a tier boundary, but it does not provide the normal
functions of a character level.

Unlocking a Horizon does not automatically grant:

- Attribute increases;
- Derived Attribute increases;
- Skill increases;
- Specialization increases;
- HOLT or Axis increases;
- additional actions;
- Capacity increases;
- Scale increases;
- equipment;
- new permissions;
- predefined features.

It changes one thing:

```text
The maximum Rank the character may purchase.
```

All actual development remains selected and paid for separately through the
normal LENS construction language.

## Risks Requiring Further Testing

### Multiplicative Acceleration

Several over-Rank-10 layers compound rapidly. The full stack may become too hot
when HOLT, Scale, equipment, and Resonance are added.

### Rank And Scale Encroachment

High Rank is intended to challenge higher Scale through focused effort. It must
not grant higher Scale's ordinary scope or make Scale irrelevant.

### Broad Skill Scope

An over-cap Skill with overly broad applicability may affect too many
Expressions. Skill boundaries must remain fictionally meaningful.

### Global Horizon Breadth

A character-level Horizon is simpler than capability-specific unlocks, but it
permits the character to advance any eligible Rank after the unlock. Testing
must determine whether the additional purchase costs provide enough control.

### Advancement Pace

The unlock cannot be priced until the project knows how much advancement an
ordinary session, story, or campaign phase provides.

### High Rank Plus HOLT Axis

HOLT and Axis values above 10 were already retained as a setting-permitted
possibility. They require direct testing against ordinary opposition,
cross-Scale opposition, compound Expressions, and limitations.

---

## Required Advancement Tests

Before this proposal can become working doctrine, test:

1. The ordinary cost and expected pace of purchasing one Rank.
2. The amount of advancement representing a major bounded Horizon unlock.
3. Experiential, narrative, and hybrid unlock procedures.
4. Whether the character-level Horizon is sufficient or too permissive.
5. Rank 12, 14, 16, 18, and 20 against the complete Difficulty Curve.
6. Each Horizon against same-Scale and cross-Scale opposition.
7. Over-cap Attributes with parent-capped Derived Attributes.
8. Over-cap Skills with parent-capped Specializations.
9. Over-cap HOLT Axes and compound HOLT Expressions.
10. High Rank combined with Scale, equipment, Resonance, and Dissonance.
11. Whether ratings above 100 produce useful resistance capacity without
    trivializing the resolution procedure.
12. Whether Rank 20 should remain the final available Horizon.

## Proposed Adoption Test

The higher-Horizon architecture should advance only if it satisfies all of the
following:

```text
Rank 10 remains a complete and viable ordinary mastery ceiling.

Higher Horizons provide meaningful vertical development.

Campaigns can exclude higher Horizons without breaking the engine.

Unlocks are rare and consequential without requiring a full character-level
subsystem.

Ranks remain purchased individually after an unlock.

Parent limits remain intact.

Higher Rank permits focused cross-Scale relevance without conferring higher
Scale itself.

The same Rank grammar continues to function without a bespoke epic-power
system.
```

## Resume Point

The next responsible development step is not choosing arbitrary experience
prices.

The advancement system must first establish:

```text
What advancement currency exists?

How is it earned?

What does one ordinary Rank cost?

How quickly should an ordinary character improve?

How much campaign time should separate Rank Horizons?
```

Once those controls exist, the proposal can assign and test bounded Horizon
unlock costs without inventing a disguised level system or an unsupported cost
curve.
