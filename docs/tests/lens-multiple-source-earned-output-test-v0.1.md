# Multiple-Source Earned Output Comparison

Date: 2026-09-11
Status: Multiple-Source output procedure adopted as a working rule, 2026-09-12.

Adoption note: the user directed continuation of the current recommended
procedure after parking the later pricing/award review. The baseline and
terminology now record separate contributing Expressions, compatible earned-
Effect addition, and distinct complementary functions. Previously approved
targeted opposition, connection interference, and mitigation scope apply.
Specific complementary-output valuations remain unresolved. Candidate labels
and proposed next tests below preserve the original comparison's chronology;
they do not override these subsequent decisions.

## Objective and Authority

Compare two separately performed actions with the same two committed actions
combined. Preserve separately earned contributions and the user's reaffirmed
premise that the second action should produce meaningful additional output.
This does not presume a bonus exceeding the sum of both separate actions.

Sources:

- `../examples/lens-system-example-library-v0.1.md`, Part V, Examples 11-13,
  and Part VI, Example 14. These preserve the earlier discussion: separate
  Source rolls, declared functions, dependencies, and independent residuals.
  The library is not independent rule authority; exact valuation is unresolved.
- `../system/lens-system-baseline-draft-v0.1.md`, Independent Opposed Effects,
  natural results, Effect handling, and Compound Expressions.

Whole-participant averaging and the lead-helper default are withdrawn in the

one Source's Expression is not a rule for averaging two characters' actions.

## Controlled Numeric Test

Each Source commits one action and resolves its own complete Expression.
Ratings below are final test inputs, not character builds. Equipment and
context are already included; neither is added after the rolls. All attempts
have permission and operate within a compatible frame. Uncertain action is
stipulated; these fixtures do not make ordinary routine activities require rolls.

The candidate sums earned Effects only when they reinforce the same declared
result in compatible terms. It does not sum Final Ratings, roll again for the
team, award another baseline Effect, or copy output onto additional targets.

| Case | Source A: Rating / completed roll | Source B: Rating / completed roll | Separate Effects | Combined compatible output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unequal contributors | 80 / 50 | 40 / 20 | 4 and 3 | 7 |
| Equal contributors | 80 / 50 | 80 / 50 | 4 and 4 | 8 |
| Marginal successes | 80 / 79 | 40 / 39 | 1 and 1 | 2 |
| One failure | 80 / 50 | 40 / 60 | 4 and 0 | 4 |
| Both fail | 80 / 90 | 40 / 60 | 0 and 0 | 0 |
| A rolls 01, then 50 | 80 / -49 | 40 / 20 | 13 and 3 | 16 |
| B rolls 00, then 50 | 80 / 50 | 40 / 150 | 4 and 0 | 4 |
| A permitted at Rating zero, rolls 01 then 00 | 0 / 1 | 40 / 20 | 1 and 3 | 4 |

The 00 fixture also creates eleven complication-severity steps for B, handled
separately with fictional applicability. They neither become successful team
Effects nor automatically subtract from A's four. Actual consequences may
disrupt a necessary connection; ordinary timing and consequence rules still
apply. Generated output is not a guarantee of its eventual delivery.

Two independently earned baseline Effects remain two Effects. They are not
duplicate copies of one success. No second baseline is added to their sum.

## Fictional Application Tests

### Shared Lift: Compatible Reinforcement

Two characters simultaneously supply lifting force to the same load. Four
earned lift Effects and three earned lift Effects can provisionally supply
seven toward that shared lift. Each committed action contributes output.

Two people lifting separately would each be limited to their own result at
that moment; simultaneous support can accomplish something neither can alone.
If independent earlier work can persist, it must also be counted fairly in the
separate-action comparison. Cooperation is not inherently superior to every
sequential approach.

No gate-to-Effect threshold, kilograms per Effect, or automatic Scale increase
is established by this arithmetic. Feasibility and the existing frame still
matter. If one Source fails, the other's earned support remains only to the
extent physically viable; an insufficient lift does not open the gate merely
because an individual contributing roll succeeded.

### Fire and Wind: Complementary Output

Fire earns four Burning Effects. Wind earns three Effects appropriate to its
declared delivery or area function. Together they can produce a directed,
more widely delivered fire rather than a localized fire and unrelated wind.

This is not automatically seven Burning Effects, four Burning Effects on each
of several targets, or simultaneous free improvements to every dimension.
Wind's earned contribution remains delivery/area output. Exact area valuation
is unresolved; this test does not assign meters or targets per Wind Effect.
Existing distribution and conservation rules remain in force.

Fire-only success leaves localized fire if viable. Wind-only success leaves
wind without Burning. A failed combination need not erase both actions.

### Delivery and Attack: Necessary Connection

One Source launches a partner; the partner executes a declared attack.
The launch provides an actual route, reach, trajectory, or other supported
contribution. The attack provides its own successful output. Two actions can
reach and affect a target the attacker could not reach alone.

Launch Effects are not automatically additional Wounded Effects. A declaration
that the launch supplies actual impact may justify reinforcement, but requires
its own applicable output rather than relabeling all delivery as damage after
the rolls. This introduces no arbitrary delivery toll or extra attack roll.

Failed launch can leave a successful attack unable to reach its target. Failed
attack does not retroactively erase successful travel. Disrupting the launch
before release differs from disabling the launcher after the partner is airborne.

## Opposition Sensitivity

Use four and three offensive Effects. Hold defensive output constant at the
listed value for each applicable resolved exchange. These are alternative
assembly boundaries, not a settled team-defense procedure.

| Defensive Effects per exchange | Two separate attacks, each opposed | One combined seven-Effect attack, opposed once |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 4 + 3 = 7 remain | 7 remain |
| 3 | 1 + 0 = 1 remains | 4 remain |
| 7 | 0 remain | 0 remain |
| 8 | 0 remain | 0 remain |

Combining can concentrate output against one defense without generating any
additional offensive Effects. That is a genuine benefit beyond presenting
two unrelated attacks. It also makes the exchange boundary consequential.

With eight defensive Effects, two separate exchanges could each support one
counter-effect; one combined exchange could support at most one. Actual
counter applicability is still required. There is no free counter per attacker
inside a single exchange under the existing per-exchange limit.

These controls hold output fixed. They do not predict a full fight: separate
attacks can have different defenses, intermediate Conditions and Function
Bands, initiative opportunities, interruptions, targets, and mitigation.
Mitigation frequency against a combined result is not decided here.

Merely declaring teamwork cannot select the favorable column. The fiction
must establish one combined result. Conversely, a defense that stops a
necessary delivery component can prevent the whole result without opposing
all offensive output directly. The recovered connecting-Lens counter example
already identifies that possibility; its numeric resistance remains unresolved.

## Findings and Next Decision

The candidate preserves greater output relative to one contributor alone,
without assuming that cooperation must exceed the total output of both separate
actions. Equal, unequal, marginal, failed, and special-result contributions
retain their independently earned amounts. Complementary functions remain
useful without being converted into the same effect type.

Recommendation for further development:

1. Add compatible earned Effects when they genuinely reinforce one shared result.
2. Retain distinct output where contributions change different aspects of it.
3. Preserve dependencies and viable residuals; do not average whole characters.

The next substantive test is opposition: distinguish defending against the
combined result from countering a necessary contribution or connecting Lens.
Use the same two-action investment and existing cancellation/counter rules.
Do not adopt universal one-defense-per-team, one-defense-per-person, automatic
synergy, cross-type conversion, or mitigation-frequency rules from these fixtures.

## Follow-On Test: Successful Declared Delivery

Date: 2026-09-12. Tested separately, then explicitly adopted by the user:
one surviving delivery Effect accomplishes the declared, permitted delivery
while its necessary means remain. Preserve normal Difficulty, applicable
cancellation, and actual disruption without a universal Effects-to-distance
conversion. Recorded in the baseline and terminology. The discussion below
preserves the test and its rationale.

Existing references keep objective range as Difficulty, means as permission
and applicable support, and the declared function as the source of outcome
meaning. See `lens-concrete-equipment-placement-opposed-play-test-v0.1.md`,
Range Difficulty Test, and `lens-opposed-leverage-expression-test-v0.1.md`,
Outcome Effect Versus Condition Effect. Neither provides a universal Wind
Effects-to-meters or area table.

Test an alternative to inventing such a conversion: a successful delivery
Expression accomplishes the declared, permitted delivery whose resistance was
already assessed. At least one surviving delivery Effect maintains that success,
provided the actual necessary route and means still exist. Additional delivery
Effects can withstand more applicable cancellation; they do not automatically
increase range, area, duration, and damage together.

Fixture: six generated Burning Effects and three generated Wind delivery
Effects. The declared Wind route carries the Fire to the selected destination;
the range, load, and means are accounted for before rolling. This does not set
a particular distance, Difficulty band, or per-target cost. If delivery ends,
the fixture explicitly permits the Fire to remain locally, without a free
substitute attack or retroactive application at the distant target.

| Applicable cancellation against delivery | Delivery remaining | Burning delivered remotely | Local residual Burning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 3 | 6 | 0 |
| 1 | 2 | 6 | 0 |
| 2 | 1 | 6 | 0 |
| 3 | 0 | 0 | 6 |
| 4 | 0 | 0 | 6 |

The arithmetic preserves six total Burning Effects, not six local plus six
remote. It illustrates the proposed success threshold; it does not establish
the threshold merely by checking its arithmetic.

Distinct actual disruption can still close the route regardless of the numeric
remainder. If the intended task was producing an amount of Wind, moving a
variable load, or expanding area according to an established output rule,
honor that rule instead of assuming a fixed delivery objective. A declaration
does not make arbitrarily large requested output available at a token Difficulty.

The adopted boundary uses ordinary baseline success for the declared delivery
in this fixed-objective case, rather than treating each delivery Effect as an
unspecified additional distance unit. General area, variable magnitude, and
duration valuations remain separate unresolved questions.
