# LENS Assets Whole-Provision Test

Date: 2026-09-05
Status: Approved provisional construction references; not a final price ladder.

## Scope and Controls

The user approved returning to Assets after retaining the Reputation references.
Compare extra personal equipment, mounts, a substantial professional setup,
and a major holding. Test concentrated and distributed starting allocations,
including meaningful limitations. Background provisions remain deferred.

Sources: the current Assets definition, the three-shop-arrangements test,
the fantasy equipment package comparison, and the current starting-package
discussion. This is a deterministic construction comparison, not a dice test
or numerical calibration of acquisition Difficulty.

The current candidate grants one starting allocation point per purchased
Assets Rank. Spending that allocation does not reduce persistent Assets Rank.
The earlier starting-roll allowance is not used here.

Everyone retains the Campaign Profile's guaranteed package: one weapon or
similar, protection, one auxiliary choice, adventurer's gear, and one professional
kit appropriate to one selected Field, with necessary ordinary accessories.
The selections below are extras; none charges again for that package.

Use a grounded fantasy Profile with ordinary goods and established routes to
the described holdings. Costs value the whole stated provision. They are not
universal prices attached to item names, equipment multipliers, or Scale numbers.
The proposed equivalence between provision cost and acquisition Difficulty
remains uncalibrated.

## Approved Provisional References

| Cost | Concrete starting provision | What it adds |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | One complete ordinary starting-package equivalent, additional to the guaranteed package; comparable alternative contents are defined by the Campaign Profile | A substantial ordinary equipment reserve, another person's equipment, or a coherent alternative provision of comparable material value |
| 2 | One sound ordinary riding horse with tack; alternatively, one sound pack animal with load gear | Personal transport or carrying capacity, according to the selected animal's actual training and capability |
| 3 | One sound war-trained riding horse with tack, without special barding or extraordinary abilities | A mount accustomed to the specified combat use as well as ordinary riding |
| 5 | An established one-person workshop: controlled tools, installed equipment for a specified trade, storage, usable rented workspace, and materials for an initial ordinary job | Work that needs a fixed professional facility rather than the portable kit already supplied |
| 10 | An owned, habitable small fortified manor with functioning ordinary defenses, accommodation, storage, a well, stable space, and its immediate grounds | A substantial defensible base that can house the party and its supplies |

The one-point reference replaces the former single ordinary extra weapon.
It does not require an identical duplicate loadout or grant an automatic
equipment-modifier increase. Ordinary eligible contents and equivalent alternatives
are Profile-defined; receiving the baseline free does not consume this allocation.

The manor has no assumed garrison, stocked armory, productive estate income,
trained mounts, or governing jurisdiction. Those are additional facts, not
automatic consequences of owning this described property. Actual included
facilities remain usable; do not impose extra purchases merely to make the
larger provision less useful. Ordinary upkeep is acknowledged, but no starting
debt, Load, or recurring payment schedule is invented.

## Equal-Allocation Comparisons

All examples below are in addition to the guaranteed package.

```text
ASSETS RANK 5: FIVE POINTS AVAILABLE

Travel provision:
  Riding horse 2 + pack animal 2 + ordinary package equivalent 1 = 5

Mounted combat provision:
  War-trained horse 3 + pack animal 2 = 5

Professional provision:
  Established workshop 5 = 5

ASSETS RANK 10: TEN POINTS AVAILABLE

Concentrated holding:
  Fortified manor 10 = 10

Distributed provision:
  Workshop 5 + war-trained horse 3 + pack animal 2 = 10
```

The travel provision supplies mounts and an additional ordinary package equivalent;
the mounted combat provision trades that additional package for relevant mount training. Neither
grants the Character riding or combat expertise.

The workshop supports work unavailable with portable equipment alone but does
not transport itself or supply a mount. The manor provides accommodation,
storage, and defensive possibilities the distributed provision lacks. The
distributed provision supplies trade facilities and trained animals absent
from the manor package. Neither automatically dominates the other, although
the campaign's actual activities may strongly favor one.

Two workshops at five each are another legitimate ten-point allocation when
their trades, locations, or independent access have value. Two descriptions
of the same already-included facility do not create another contribution.

## Application Probes

| Situation | Result |
|---|---|
| The Character loses their primary weapon | The purchased spare is available if actually carried or accessible. Its provision cost adds no attack multiplier. |
| The Character uses two purchased weapons | Ownership grants no extra action. Apply the existing action and Expression rules. |
| A war-trained horse enters combat | Its training matters to the mount's behavior; the rider still uses their own applicable capability and expertise. No numerical mount benefit is invented here. |
| The professional undertakes work needing a forge or fixed bench | The workshop supplies the established facility and relevant tools. It does not supply unpurchased expertise or unlimited materials. |
| The party shelters at the manor | The established accommodation is usable without purchasing a separate lodging permission or rolling to prove ownership. |
| The party defends the manor | Actual defenses and available participants matter. Ownership does not supply an unseen garrison or automatically raise every Character Expression's Scale. |
| The owner wants to fund an expedition by selling their holding | Sale is a possible route when a buyer and transfer are available. The holding is relinquished, not kept alongside its full proceeds. Construction points are not renewable cash or a conversion rate. |
| An official already has use of an issued mount or workshop | Honor the established Authority provision. Do not charge for private ownership unless private ownership is also being selected. Issued use and ownership have different permissions. |

## Limitation Comparisons

### Restricted Workshop

Compare the five-point workshop with access to another person's equivalent
facility on agreed evenings, without exclusive storage or ownership of the
installed equipment. The latter supports some of the same work but cannot be
used on demand, relocated, or sold by the Character.

Result: a credible lower-value provision when those restrictions matter in the
campaign. No fixed refund is assigned. If all relevant work occurs during those
evenings and neither storage nor control matters, the restriction may have
little practical value. Ordinary tenancy was already included in the five-point
package and is not a second discount.

### Damaged Holding

Compare the ten-point habitable manor with the same property having a breached
wall, unsafe upper rooms, and an unusable well. The grounds, remaining storage,
and surviving shelter still exist, but several promised functions do not.

Result: a credible lower-value starting holding, with restoration as future
play. This is not the intact package for fewer points with cosmetic damage.
Do not assign automatic Framework Load, price repairs, or create equipment
Condition tracks in this test; those procedures remain separate unfinished work.

### Service-Dependent Mount

A war-trained mount available only while carrying out a patron's assignments
does not provide the same freedom as the personally controlled three-point
mount. Its whole value depends on whether personal travel or independent work
will actually occur. If the accepted patron relationship already promises this
support, honor it without purchasing the same permission again under Assets.

## Findings and Decision Boundary

The provision architecture passes these conceptual checks: extras do not replace
the guaranteed package; a single substantial asset and several smaller assets
support distinct play; access restrictions and lost functions can change whole
value without a new subsystem; material provision does not automatically add
Training, Authority, personnel, or extra Expression multipliers.

This comparison establishes readable differences, not equal utility in every
campaign or a validated Difficulty price curve. The described 1 / 2 / 3 / 5 / 10
packages are retained for provisional construction use under the approval below.
Limitation discounts remain unresolved.
