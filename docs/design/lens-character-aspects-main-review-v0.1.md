# Character Aspects: Main Review

Date: 2026-09-16.
Status: Historical requested critical review, followed by the revisions and
2026-09-17 core adoption recorded below. Original findings are preserved as
review history, not competing current rules.

## Core Adoption - 2026-09-17

The user adopted the revised lasting-Aspect package into core, with optional
character selection and the expressly deferred extensions preserved. Baseline,
terminology, and protected construction references now carry the rules. No
prices, session awards, protected point allocations, or existing character
records changed. Temporary spending and GM-offer enhancement remain expansion
candidates, not adopted procedures. The revisions below explain why the
original spending findings do not describe the adopted package.

Live-play consistency of applicability and growth remains an observation need,
not an unresolved core-inclusion decision or permission to recalibrate awards.

## Subsequent Candidate Revision - 2026-09-16

After this review, the user approved removal of temporary Aspect spending and
placed temporary enhancement, including a GM-offer variant, in optional
advanced-expansion work. The working package now reflects
that decision; the review below retains its original evidence and line references
to the pre-revision package. Its recommendation to retain temporary spending is
superseded. That revision alone did not adopt Aspects; the later explicit
core adoption is recorded above.

The repeated-spending concern, potential-Access peak-purchase advantage, and
need for Aspect-specific NPC spending rules no longer apply to the revised
candidate. Spending-conversion controls are historical, not an active
development exchange. Ordinary boosts, rerolls, and meaningful narrative
sacrifice remain under their existing guidance.

The user subsequently approved NPCs using the same lasting Aspect factors and
applicability standards, with ranks grounded in established characterization
and history. The package records that treatment and adds application/development
examples. NPC Aspect funding is not an unresolved requirement.

On 2026-09-17, the user approved recognizing new Aspect Access and Rank 1
together when narratively justified. The revised candidate requires no mandatory
delay and awards no automatic rank for a declaration. Access alone remains
possible under the same story-based judgment.

Remaining review considerations: fiction-based applicability, narrative growth
and GM communication, and lasting multiplier impact. Existing no-spending
fixtures remain useful; full-push fixtures
do not describe the adopted package. Core inclusion was subsequently approved
with optional character selection, as recorded above.

Reviewed source: [Aspect package](lens-character-aspects-working-package-v0.1.md).
That package, rather than the original handoff or the separate sixth-Attribute
experiment, supplies the candidate being evaluated. Its branch decisions are
treated as intentional proposals, not questions accidentally left unanswered.

## Findings, In Priority Order

### 1. High Impact: This Adds A Major Spending Option, Not Just Identity Development

Source: package lines 156-201; baseline Working Boost and Reroll Sequence.

The branch intentionally allows repeated Aspect purchases up to x2.0 alongside
the ordinary one-point boost. An Access-only Aspect can reach that ceiling for
five points. This materially changes the core's current one-boost spending
boundary. It is a proposed amendment, not a discovery that existing rules already
allow unlimited boosts. If adopted, the baseline must explicitly distinguish the
one ordinary Resonance boost from bounded temporary Aspect expenditure.

The change has real output consequences. In the review's complete Expression
fixture, an attacker and defender each have Final Rating 39 without Aspects.
The probability of positive offensive remainder is 29.57%. Giving only the
attacker a full Aspect push plus ordinary boost produces Rating 95 against 39
and raises that probability to 84.99%, before mitigation. The mean offensive
remainder rises from 0.73 to 4.22 Effects. This is not a survival or victory
probability; target Conditions, mitigation, duration, and further actions are
outside the fixture.

When both actors push to Rating 95, offensive remainder occurs 45.29% of the
time and its mean is 1.72 Effects. Equal multipliers do not cancel out of the
independent-Effects procedure. Equal-Effect cancellation becomes less frequent,
and successful offensive remainders can be larger. Defensive surplus still
obeys the adopted one-counter-effect limit; nothing here grants extra retaliation.

This is not evidence of a broken mechanic. It can support exactly the stronger,
identity-driven actions the user wants. It is evidence that the adoption decision
must include encounter impact and spending pressure, not just a small starting
bonus. Do not automatically increase all Difficulties to cancel the new benefit.

Recommendation: retain this as an explicit, substantial part of the playtest
candidate. Do not quietly weaken it, raise its price, or prohibit coexistence
with the ordinary boost. Confirm that its actual influence on play is wanted.

### 2. High Impact: GM Adjudication Controls Both Immediate Power And Lasting Growth

Source: package lines 73-82, 93-130.

The same GM decides which Aspect applies, whether a choice merits development,
and when that development becomes effective. This supports fiction-first play,
but variation in judgment affects both today's Expression and future capability.
The package explicitly protects quiet choices, unsuccessful commitments, and
player-authored identity. Those safeguards are strengths; their success at the
table is not established by numerical assertions.

Broad applicability is not itself a defect. A protector can build, bargain,
investigate, or fight without needing separate Aspects for each action. However,
two equally sincere concepts can differ greatly in practical opportunity:
protecting traveling companions may arise frequently, while loyalty to an absent
relative may seldom be directly involved. The same multiplier and development
reference do not establish equal practical value.

Recommendation: retain player recommendation and GM adjudication, including ties.
Do not replace them with automatic highest-factor selection. Give examples of
clear, debatable, and unrelated applications, with the reason for each. Helping
dependents earn a living can genuinely express family devotion; the phrase
"everything eventually helps my family" should not settle every application by
itself. Judge the actual character, action, and circumstances rather than imposing
an immediate-danger requirement or a fixed list of permitted verbs.

The important playtest observation is whether adjudication stays brief and
understandable, not whether every Aspect fires equally often. Disagreements about
fit should not become judgments about the player's morality or sincerity.

### 3. High Impact: Equivalent-Value Recognition Is Not A Proven Advancement Exchange

Source: package lines 203-227; spending test's recognition controls.

The source rightly distinguishes recognizing a meaningful sacrifice from making
temporarily purchased ranks permanent. It also correctly notes that points are
not refunded. There is no demonstrated cash-generation loop.

However, the tests assign development using proxies of 10, 20, or 60 points per
rank and assumed GM recognition. Aspects have no adopted purchase price, and
their practical applicability differs from a single HOLT Axis. Consequently,
the 20-point proxy cannot establish that actual awards are economically
equivalent. The test verifies consequences of its assumptions, not the fairness
of those assumptions.

At the 20-point proxy, full recognition of twelve sessions' eligible expenditure
produces Rank 3 from Rank 1, with further analytical progress. A different
stipulated policy awarding a full rank after every push reaches Rank 10 at
session 9. These are different assumed GM policies, not predictions of player
behavior and not proof that the proposed guidance inevitably causes either.

Recommendation: preserve the possibility of strong narrative recognition for
meaningful expenditure. Keep equivalent-value arithmetic in the explanatory
test material, not as an implied exchange rate on the player-facing page.
Describe the actual choice first. Money spent is evidence, not a receipt
entitling the player to a particular rank. Assess the whole choice once rather
than automatically adding a second spending award to a whole-scene award that
already considered the expenditure. Non-spending choices remain fully eligible.

If a table regularly awards nearly equivalent development, describe the outcome
honestly: some development is redirected into the Aspect rather than wholly
forfeited. The player still sacrifices freedom to spend it elsewhere.

### 4. Medium Impact: Potential Aspects Have Immediate Mechanical Value

Source: package lines 50-66 and 160-163.

An undeveloped Aspect has no permanent bonus, but it grants access to the full
temporary spending range when applicable. It is not merely a future story hook.
Leaving potential slots empty is mechanically different from filling them,
although a player may reasonably prefer to leave identity open for later play.

| Permanent Rank | Normal factor | Aspect points needed for x2.0 |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | x1.0 | 5 |
| 1 | x1.1 | 5 |
| 2 | x1.2 | 4 |
| 5 | x1.5 | 3 |
| 8 | x1.8 | 1 |
| 9 | x1.9 | 1 |
| 10 | x2.0 | 0 |

Permanent development therefore improves ordinary availability of the benefit
and reduces peak-use cost; it does not raise the peak above what potential
Access already permits. That is coherent, particularly for a character's first
defining act, but should be stated plainly. The equal maximum-push cost at odd
and preceding even ranks follows the agreed whole-point final increment. It is
not a rounding defect to repair.

Recommendation: retain the proposed allocation for playtesting. Explain what
potential Access does. Do not invent compensation for unused slots, a lifetime
Aspect cap, or a requirement to fill every slot. Before a published default,
clarify recognizing genuinely new Aspects in play and whether/how NPCs receive
Aspects and funds for temporary spending. The candidate does not justify an
unlimited GM spending budget or compulsory mirrored spending by opponents.

### 5. Medium Impact: Timing Needs Explicit Integration, Not New Restrictions

Source: package lines 116-120 and 194-201; core spending sequence.

The candidate says temporary enhancement is for one roll but does not expressly
state its purchase deadline. The core already buys a numerical boost before the
initial roll. Integration should state that temporary Aspect expenditure is
committed before that roll too, rather than silently creating a post-roll repair
option. This is recommended inherited timing, not an adopted change here.

Paid reroll retention is already settled: keep the enhanced Expression and replace
the complete dice result. Do not charge again, permit another paid reroll, or
reopen that branch decision.

Narratively earned development can occur during a scene. It does not authorize
rewriting a settled result. The package already preserves that boundary; no
mandatory between-scenes rule or training montage is needed. The same Aspect
contribution must not also be counted as contextual Resonance. Independent
equipment, circumstances, Function, Difficulty, Scale, and Access retain their
existing jobs.

### 6. Medium Impact: Optional Participation Is Not Numerically Neutral

Source: package lines 17-20, 54-57, 70-89.

A player who takes no Aspects lacks benefits that another player receives.
That need not be forbidden, just as not every character must choose the same
tools. It should not be presented as numerically equivalent. A campaign that
omits the module also has a different output profile from one using it.

At full permanent or temporary development, Aspects add a factor of two to an
applicable Expression. Ordinary six-layer raw output can go from 320 to 640;
with one Rank-10 Axis, raw output can reach 1,280 before other factors. This is
not a breach of the newly adopted Rank-10 ceiling, which does not cap Final
Ratings. It does mean that old capability examples without Aspects are no
longer the complete enabled-module examples.

Recommendation: introduce the package as an optional core playtest module first,
available to ordinary and extraordinary characters. Do not limit it to Heralds,
or describe it as necessary for every campaign. Promotion to a default core
layer should follow evidence that its extra decisions improve actual play.

## What The Package Gets Right

- Identity is neither a sixth Attribute nor a new source of extraordinary
  permission. Its numerical layer has a clear, separate role.
- Narrative alignment can occur without a roll. The proposal does not require
  manufacturing tests to award character development.
- Failure can reveal commitment as well as success. Quiet consistency can count
  without speechmaking, suffering, or compliance with a prescribed plot.
- Only one Aspect contributes to the Expression. Multiple legitimate Aspects
  can develop without automatically multiplying their numerical benefits.
- The independent factor is coherent with the existing Lens calculation. It
  amplifies an available Expression; it cannot repair Closed Access or a zero
  contextual multiplier by itself.
- Temporary and permanent development are clearly different. No second currency,
  automatic reimbursement, or hidden permanent purchase price is required.
- Tensions do not automatically punish the Aspect not selected. Transfer and
  fulfillment let characters change rather than retaining a completed motive
  solely to keep their investment.
- The deferred flaw funding and fulfillment payoff ideas are correctly outside
  this initial package. They should not delay or silently enter its adoption.

## Numerical Findings

### Complete Expression Controls

Use A5/D3 and Task, Field, Specialty, Precision all at 5: raw ordinary output
40.5. All layers are assumed applicable, Scale matched, Function complete,
equipment neutral, and no context except the specified ordinary boost.
These are Expression fixtures, not complete repriced characters or recommended
encounter Difficulties. Apply 0.98 x 0.6^(Difficulty - 1) and floor only once.

| Difficulty | No Aspect | Starting Aspect x1.1 | Full push x2.0 | Full push + ordinary boost |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 39 | 43 | 79 | 95 |
| 2 | 23 | 26 | 47 | 57 |
| 3 | 14 | 15 | 28 | 34 |

With an applicable Rank-5 Axis the same rows become 59/65/119/142,
35/39/71/85, and 21/23/42/51. Higher numbers above 99 still improve Effects;
they do not remove natural-00 failure. An extra x2.0 is substantial but does
not remove accumulated Difficulty: one added Difficulty band and x2.0 together
give x1.2 relative to the former result before flooring; two bands give x0.72.

### Opposed Controls

Both actors use the same ordinary fixture and D1 unless a modifier is shown.
Natural-01 success output and repeat continuations are included to a residual
probability of 1e-14 per actor. Natural 00 supplies no successful Effects;
its separate complications are not simulated. No paid rerolls or mitigation.

| Attacker / defender | Final Ratings | Positive offensive remainder | Mean offensive remainder |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Neither has an Aspect | 39 / 39 | 29.57% | 0.73 |
| Only attacker has starting Rank 1 | 43 / 39 | 33.38% | 0.88 |
| Both have starting Rank 1 | 43 / 43 | 31.73% | 0.83 |
| Attacker ordinary boost only | 47 / 39 | 37.34% | 1.04 |
| Attacker full Aspect push plus boost | 95 / 39 | 84.99% | 4.22 |
| Same attacker against ordinary boosted defense | 95 / 47 | 81.15% | 3.93 |
| Both use full Aspect push plus boost | 95 / 95 | 45.29% | 1.72 |
| Permanent Aspect 8 against permanent Aspect 2 | 71 / 47 | 57.31% | 2.13 |

These show output sensitivity, not an encounter balance verdict. Paid rerolls,
different complete builds, mitigation, actual stakes, and action sequences can
change the play result. The symmetric full-push case is a sensitivity control,
not permission for the GM to match every player expenditure for free.

### Spending And Growth

At Access-only or Rank 1, a full push plus ordinary boost costs six points.
Against the reference award of 19, one such use leaves 13, two leave 7, and
three leave 1 for lasting purchases or savings. A paid reroll costs another
point when used. Three full pushes each followed by a paid reroll cost 21,
requiring a draw on prior savings even at the reference award. Actual funds
must exist before expenditure; these totals do not allow award borrowing.

This can make a satisfying choice, but numerical affordability cannot establish
whether players feel excited or pressured to spend. One full push a session
does not halt ordinary advancement. Repeated offense and defense pushes can
consume almost all or more than that session's earnings.

The three-to-four-relevant-session development reference implies approximately
27-36 relevant sessions for nine ordinary awards from Rank 1 to 10 if that
reference alone is followed. It is not a guarantee or mandatory delay. Defining
events may accelerate it and several Aspects may develop together. Eight-wedge
circles and sliders remain memory aids, not eight required accomplishments.
With four players and all six starting entries filled, there could be 24 Aspect
entries to remember; this is a workload illustration, not a requirement to
update every entry every session.

## Integration Recommendations

If the user approves a playtest module, make the smallest necessary changes:

1. Explicitly introduce one independent Aspect factor without reclassifying it
   as a sixth Attribute, HOLT permission, or pooled contextual bonus.
2. Name temporary Aspect spending separately from the one ordinary boost, state
   pre-roll commitment, preserve the existing reroll, and retain the x2.0 limit.
3. Explain potential Access's immediate spending value and lasting ranks' effect
   on the baseline benefit and cost of reaching the same peak.
4. Provide a short applicability and narrative-development example set, including
   a quiet choice, a failed attempt, a broad legitimate application, an unrelated
   action, and a costly action whose entire significance is judged together.
5. State how the module is offered to player characters and NPCs before treating
   enabled-module opposition as a calibrated default. Do not infer an NPC bank.
6. Clarify that normal session recognition can coexist with story-delivered
   Aspect development, consistently with existing story-award guidance. Do not
   automatically subtract session points or pay spendable points for an Aspect
   rank. Avoid duplicate recognition of the same evidence within one award.

Do not change prices, awards, Difficulty, or protected creation allocations to
offset an unproven imbalance. Do not add an automatic spending-to-rank exchange,
mandatory suffering, progress quotas, forced moral conduct, or penalties for
choosing between legitimate commitments. Leave flaw-funded additions, valued
fulfillment payoffs, and arithmetic for already-developed successors deferred.

## Recommendation And First Decision

The concept is strong enough for an optional core playtest module. The most
important concerns are magnitude, adjudication, and incentive communication,
not mathematical inconsistency or a demonstrated infinite-growth exploit.

Recommend retaining the branch's main design, including the independent factor
and temporary spending, with the integration clarifications above. Do not yet
declare the complete package a mandatory/default core layer or claim balanced
encounter pacing. No new generic arithmetic sweep is needed before a bounded
table test; observe one scene with genuine alignment, one without it, and one
with competing commitments, including a non-spending character choice.

The first substantive decision is whether to accept the whole package's larger
identity-linked spending opportunity as an optional core playtest feature. That
is different from merely agreeing that narrative identity deserves recognition.
Approval would not itself decide unresolved NPC funding or deferred extensions.
