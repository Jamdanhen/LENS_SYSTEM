Source count does not itself define mitigation frequency. Neither the same
target nor simultaneous timing alone proves that two effects have become one.

## Matching and Distribution Controls

### Different Forms in One Delivery

Four Burning and three Impact Effects arrive together. A Lens mitigating two
Burning Effects leaves two Burning and three Impact. It neither mitigates
Impact nor receives a second use against the Fire merely because there were
multiple Sources.

The broader question of allocating a single shared mitigation allowance that
matches several distinct forms is not settled by this typed fixture. Do not
silently apply one allowance in full to every component.

### Delivery Is Not Additional Harm

Four Burning Effects delivered by three Wind Effects encounter mitigation two
against Burning. Two Burning remain. The Wind output does not become extra
Burning to overwhelm the protection; its delivery function remains separate.

### Different Targets

A stipulated valid distribution assigns four Effects to one target and three
to another. Each target has its own mitigation two. Two and one remain.
One shared origin does not combine the targets' protection into a single
deduction, and the original seven Effects are not copied onto each target.
The test does not establish target-count or area pricing.

### Defined Limits Override the Fixture

A protection with a finite reserve, a per-round limit, a particular interception
window, or another established restriction follows that definition. Do not
reset it per hit merely because the unrestricted test Lens did. Likewise, do
not invent repeated mitigation from the number of contributing characters.

## Verification and Recommendation

`lens-multiple-source-mitigation-test-v0.1.cjs` passed all assertions: five
mitigation-strength comparisons, cancellation order, mixed-form applicability,
delivery-versus-harm separation, and independent target protection.
This is an arithmetic and applicability check, not a full combat simulation.

Recommendation: determine mitigation application from the actual incoming
effect and the protection's defined scope, not from the number of contributing
Sources. A single reinforced matching effect encounters the applicable
mitigation once; genuinely separate applications remain separate. Preserve
typed matching, individual targets, and explicit limits.

No universal team bonus, mitigation bypass, new armor value, or automatic
permission to merge unrelated attacks is proposed. The mitigation-scope
boundary is now adopted; compatible earned-output addition remains a tested
assembly candidate unless separately adopted.
