# Opposed Stakes and Accomplishment Test

Date: 2026-09-10
Status: Diagnostic supporting the user-approved ordinary opposed procedure,
2026-09-10. Baseline and terminology updated after approval. Movement unchanged.

## Adoption Note

The user approved signed Net Margin with declared stakes distinguishing
comparative victory from successful accomplishment. Lesser failure may win a
comparison but cannot manufacture successful action Effects. Subsequently,
on 2026-09-10, the user approved equal Margins as no comparative winner or
successful contested Effects, preserving independently accomplished results
without automatic rerolls or free actions. Special-result opposition remains
unresolved. The test and recommendation below preserve the reasoning presented
before approval.

## Question and Authority

The user distinguished winning a comparison by failing less badly from
successfully accomplishing an action: a less inaccurate throw can win a target
contest, but an unsuccessful attack remains unsuccessful.

This test applies that distinction to racing, competing persuasion and attack
versus defense. It follows the signed-margin test, without repeating its full
probability enumeration or treating that test's fully relative Effects as an
adopted rule.

## Shared Calculation

```text
Margin = Final Rating - roll
Net Margin = acting Margin - opposing Margin

A successful action that wins opposition generates:
1 baseline Effect + floor(Net Margin / 10)
```

Comparison alone can identify better performance even when both Margins are
negative. It does not establish the successful accomplishment represented by
an individual attempt. A prize or declared comparative victory may follow the
comparison; this is not a retroactive successful hit or automatic damaging
Effect.

Ordinary rolls only. Natural 01, 99, 00 and their continuation interactions are
not decided. Equal margins have no decisive comparative winner as a retained
test control, not an adopted tie rule. Equality to one's own Rating counts as
individual success for these tests, preserving the earlier executable convention.

## Six Arithmetic Fixtures

| Acting Margin | Opposing Margin | Net Margin | Acting outcome |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| +50 | +20 | +30 | Successful opposed accomplishment, 4 Effects |
| +50 | -30 | +80 | Successful opposed accomplishment, 9 Effects |
| -50 | -80 | +30 | Better performance, no successful action Effects |
| -30 | +50 | -80 | Failed action, opposing side performs better |
| +20 | +20 | 0 | No decisive comparative victory, tie control |
| -30 | -30 | 0 | Both fail, no decisive comparative victory |

All six arithmetic fixtures were checked with PowerShell. The same fixtures
were then reviewed qualitatively against each of the three cases below: eighteen
scenario applications, not eighteen statistical simulations.

## Case 1: Racing Toward a Destination

Separate the questions the fiction actually poses:

- Who runs better during this contested interval?
- Does either runner accomplish a difficult push needed to arrive before the
  gate shuts?

If both fail that push, -50 beats -80 as comparative performance, but neither
has established timely arrival. Ordinary movement already available is not
erased. This does not introduce a distance formula, new movement allowance,
second automatic roll, or reopen the parked movement lane.

If one succeeds and one fails, the successful runner wins this comparison.
If both succeed, compare Margins. Success at an independent deadline and relative
finishing order remain different facts: both can arrive in time while one arrives
first. Do not let the comparison retroactively make the other miss the deadline.

Effects have no new seconds, meters, or universal lead conversion in this test.
An entire race and a bounded interval must not be confused; a lead during an
interval does not automatically establish the eventual race winner.

Result: passes with the resolution scope and actual accomplishment stated.

## Case 2: Competing to Persuade an Audience

Two advocates seek support for different proposals. The audience may reject
both. At -50 versus -80, one case was less unsuccessful, but neither advocate
has secured the requested support. No successful pressure Condition is created
solely by subtracting two failures.

If the declared event is instead a judged debate asking who presented the
better case, the same relative comparison can establish a debate winner. The
winner has not thereby made every listener believe the claim or support the
proposal. These are different stakes, not different Task arithmetic.

At +50 versus +20, the leading advocate has Net Margin 30 and four generated
Effects for the successful contested outcome. At +50 versus -30, Net Margin
80 gives nine. Neither supplies unrelated concessions, a mandatory voting rule,
unlimited control or automatic votes-per-Effect. Existing player agency and
GM-adjudicated consequence rules continue to govern valid social Effects.

Only establish a relative winner if the two efforts meaningfully compete over
the declared outcome. Two compatible proposals do not become mutually exclusive
merely because both advocates made rolls. Do not add a generic Difficulty for
resistance already represented by active opposition.

Result: passes; it prevents lesser failure from becoming compulsory persuasion.

## Case 3: Attack Through Defense

At -50 versus -80, the attack fails and inflicts no successful attack Effect.
The failed defense cannot manufacture contact. No successful defensive maneuver
is retrospectively claimed merely because the attack missed either.

At +50 versus +20, Net Margin 30 yields four attack Effects.
At +50 versus -30, Net Margin 80 yields nine attack Effects.
The latter retains the user's consistent subtraction: failed opposition can
amplify an already successful action.

At -30 versus +50, the attack fails; defense does not gain a free counterattack.
A defensive win establishes only the outcome that the defensive Expression
actually carries. A compound counter retains its existing distinct contributions
and action accounting.

Equal margins produce no decisive attack victory under the tie test control.
Generated Effects still precede matching mitigation and do not necessarily
translate one-for-one into Capacity Load.

Result: passes; no miss-to-hit conversion and no failed-defense arithmetic branch.

## Findings

The same subtraction supports all three cases. What differs is the meaning of
the established stakes, not the calculation or the Attribute family.

The declared stakes must precede the roll. A failed attempt cannot be relabeled
afterward as a comparison to obtain the original successful effect.

Comparison can award a genuine comparative outcome. It need not award successful
action Effects. Conversely, comparative loss does not erase an independently
achieved fact, such as both runners meeting a deadline.

The earlier signed-margin severity increase remains real: these tests do not
remove the Rating-20 attack's potential ten Effects against a badly failed
defense. They address eligibility, not that separate output tradeoff.

## Recommendation

Retain one signed Net Margin calculation. Let stated comparative stakes recognize
the better result even when both attempts fail; require individual success and
victory over opposition for successful contested action Effects. Preserve Access,
actual scope, ordinary action costs, and social agency.

The test supports this as a working ordinary-roll procedure. Full adoption needs
user agreement. Equal margins and special-result opposition remain unresolved;
do not add topic-specific contest subsystems or another universal roll.
