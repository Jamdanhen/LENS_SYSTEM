# LENS Background and Starting Package Peer Survey

Version: 0.1  
Status: Research Record  
Decision Status: No LENS background architecture adopted  
Purpose: Examine how narrative-focused role-playing games establish starting
backgrounds, identity packages, social resources, reputation, relationships,
obligations, and related character truths, using GURPS as a control.

---

## Research Question

```text
How do existing role-playing games turn a Character's history and social place
into starting game material without allowing one broad phrase to provide
unlimited mechanical value?
```

Related LENS questions:

```text
What belongs inside a background?

Which background elements are only narrative truths?

Which elements grant Access or permission?

Which elements deserve numerical expression?

Should wealth, reputation, authority, relationships, obligations, equipment,
and learned competence share one construction currency?
```

This survey does not assume that LENS needs a universal Background Lens.

---

## Systems Examined

```text
Fate Core
Blades in the Dark
13th Age
QuestWorlds
City of Mist
Cortex Prime
Daggerheart

```


## Fate Core

### Starting Structure

Fate Core begins a Character with:

```text
five Aspects

one High Concept
one Trouble
three Aspects produced through the Phase Trio

a Skill pyramid
three free Stunts
Refresh 3
```

Aspects may describe:

```text
identity
personality
beliefs
relationships
problems
history
social standing
reputation
other significant truths
```

### How Background Matters

An Aspect is true in the fiction without being permanently added to every
roll.

To gain a major mechanical benefit from a helpful Aspect, a player normally
spends a Fate Point to invoke it.

When an Aspect complicates the Character's life, accepting the compel returns
a Fate Point.

Stunts are separated from Aspects. They provide defined mechanical exceptions
or bonuses. Three are free; additional Stunts reduce Refresh.

Fate Extras handle powers, important equipment, vehicles, organizations, and
other elements that require more structure than an Aspect, Skill, or Stunt can
provide. Extras use existing character currencies such as Aspect slots, Skill
slots, Stunt slots, or Refresh.

### Wealth and Reputation

```text
Wealth may appear as a Resources Skill, an Aspect, or an Extra depending on
what work it performs.

Reputation usually fits naturally as an Aspect.
```

The sentence alone does not supply a permanent universal bonus.

### Control Method

```text
fixed number of Aspect slots
activation through Fate Points
complications replenish the activation currency
defined mechanical benefits placed in Stunts or Extras
```

### Design Lesson

Fate balances broad narrative truths through limited slots and a bidirectional
currency. It does not attempt to make every phrase equally broad through point
pricing.

Official sources:

- [Fate Core Character Creation](https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/character-creation)
- [Fate Core Character Sheet](https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/character-sheet)
- [Fate Core Stunts and Refresh](https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/stunts-refresh)
- [Creating an Extra](https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/creating-extra)

## Blades in the Dark

### Starting Structure

Blades distributes background information across several choices:

```text
Playbook
Heritage and a specific family detail
Background and a specific personal detail
Action ratings
one Special Ability
one close friend
one rival
one Vice and Vice purveyor
Playbook equipment access
```

The Playbook is an area of focus and preference. It supplies starting Action
ratings, available Special Abilities, advancement triggers, contacts, and
equipment access without making other types of action unavailable.

### How Background Matters

Heritage and Background do not become broad ratings.

Instead:

```text
one starting Action dot must reflect Heritage
one starting Action dot must reflect Background
two additional Action dots are freely assigned
```

The Character receives a total of seven starting Action dots, including the
three already placed by the Playbook. No Action may begin above 2 without a
special exception.

### Wealth, Reputation, and Relationships

Blades separates these functions rather than packing them into Background:

```text
Playbook represents part of reputation and approach.

Close friend and rival establish two explicit relationships.

Vice establishes a recurring need and a relationship to a purveyor.

Equipment is available through the Playbook and declared through Load.

Coin, Stash, crew status, and faction relationships operate elsewhere in the
system rather than becoming one Background bonus.
```

### Control Method

```text
Background directs one unit of an existing competence currency.
Heritage directs one unit of the same currency.
Playbooks constrain available special options.
Relationships are selected in both helpful and adversarial forms.
Equipment access is bounded by Load during an operation.
```

### Design Lesson

Blades makes background mechanically visible without creating a second Skill
system. History influences the allocation of existing Action ratings rather
than supplying a new broad bonus on top of them.

Official source:

- [Blades in the Dark Character Creation](https://bladesinthedark.com/character-creation)

## 13th Age

### Starting Structure

Each Character receives:

```text
8 Background Points
minimum Background rating 1
maximum Background rating 5
as many Backgrounds as the point budget permits
```

A Background is a broad category of prior experience, such as:

```text
Cat Burglar
Imperial Soldier
Resentful Barkeep
Hunted Outlaw
Temple Acolyte
```

### How Background Matters

Backgrounds replace a conventional noncombat Skill list.

The player explains which Background applies to the attempted task. The roll
uses:

```text
d20
+ relevant Ability modifier
+ Character level
+ one relevant Background rating
```

Only one Background applies to a check. Multiple Backgrounds do not stack.

### Wealth, Reputation, and Relationships

Official design discussion permits Backgrounds to represent more than jobs:

```text
items
relationships
personal history
temporary story resources
```

That flexibility works because the Background contribution remains capped and
only one Background applies to a roll.

### Control Method

```text
fixed total point budget
hard per-Background cap
only one Background per check
Backgrounds affect noncombat Skill checks rather than all mechanics
```

### Design Lesson

This is the clearest example of the straight Background Point model.

It is also the clearest duplication warning for LENS. Because LENS already has
Skills and Specializations, importing rated backgrounds in this form would
risk purchasing learned competence twice.

Official sources:

- [13th Age Archmage Engine SRD Backgrounds and Skill Checks](https://pelgranepress.com/media/SRD/13thAgeArchmageEngineSRD.pdf)
- [13th Age Backgrounds Design Discussion](https://pelgranepress.com/2013/05/24/13th-age-backgrounds/)

## QuestWorlds

### Starting Structure

QuestWorlds begins with a brief Character concept combining:

```text
an occupational Keyword or area of expertise
a distinguishing characteristic
```

A series may also grant Keywords such as culture or religion.

The As-You-Go method allows abilities to be created when they first become
relevant until the Character reaches the starting allowance. Advanced list and
prose methods are also provided.

### Keyword Models

QuestWorlds offers two treatments of Keywords.

```text
PACKAGE MODEL

The Keyword is shorthand for a package of abilities.

The abilities may be increased together during creation, but are too unrelated
to improve as one unit later.
```

```text
UMBRELLA MODEL

The Keyword is both a usable rated ability and a collection of narrower
abilities.

Breakout abilities improve a specific part of the Keyword.

Example:

Detective 17
Forensics +2
Handgun +1
```

### Wealth, Reputation, and Relationships

QuestWorlds can treat wealth as:

```text
an explicit ability

or

an implication of background, profession, or status
```

The rating does not objectively count money. It measures how effectively the
Character overcomes story obstacles with wealth and resources.

Relationships may be rated abilities. Allies, Patrons, Contacts, Followers,
Sidekicks, and Retainers receive distinct treatment according to the kind of
relationship involved.

Relationships may also operate as Flaws when they create obligations or
recurring story obstacles.

### Control Method

```text
finite starting ability allowance
common starting ratings
limited improvement points
Keyword scope defined by occupation, culture, religion, or setting
Breakout abilities distinguish narrow expertise
Flaws create rated resistance or penalties
```

### Design Lesson

QuestWorlds is the closest peer to the idea of a broad background package that
can remain mechanically usable. It survives because the entire game is built
around freeform rated abilities and because it explicitly defines the
relationship between a broad Keyword and narrower breakout abilities.

Official source:

- [QuestWorlds System Reference Document](https://www.chaosium.com/content/FreePDFs/QuestWorlds/QuestWorlds%20SRD.pdf)

## City of Mist

### Starting Structure

A Character is built from four Themes.

Each Theme normally contains:

```text
a Theme title
three Power Tags
one Weakness Tag
one Identity or Mystery
```

Logos Themes cover ordinary life, including categories such as:

```text
Defining Event
Defining Relationship
Mission
Personality
Possessions
Routine
Training
```

### How Background Matters

Power Tags can describe:

```text
abilities
equipment
allies
resources
traits
story elements
catchphrases
```

Each relevant Power Tag normally contributes one point of Power. Relevant
Weakness Tags subtract and also drive Theme development.

The Theme groups several related statements into one identity structure. It
does not treat the Theme title alone as an unlimited bonus.

Official design guidance also limits overly broad tags and emphasizes that
specific tags are more reliable in their stated circumstances.

### Wealth and Reputation

```text
Wealth may be represented through a Possessions Theme and specific tags such
as rich, a business, property, or financial access.

Reputation may appear as a tag, Identity, relationship, or situational Story
Tag depending on what is actually established.
```

### Control Method

```text
fixed number of Themes
fixed starting Power and Weakness Tags per Theme
each relevant Tag contributes the same unit
Themebooks use questions to control conceptual scope
Weakness Tags create direct mechanical exposure and advancement
broad-tag guidance prevents a sheet of universal descriptors
```

### Design Lesson

City of Mist balances freeform character truths by decomposing each broad
Theme into several specific usable and vulnerable statements. The Theme is a
container for related Lenses, not a universal multiplier by itself.

Official sources:

- [City of Mist Tags and Statuses](https://sonofoak.com/en-gb/blogs/news/tags-and-statuses-in-city-of-mist-ttrpg)
- [City of Mist Power Tag Scope Guidance](https://sonofoak.com/en-eu/blogs/news/choosing-power-tags-in-city-of-mist-part-3)
- [City of Mist Mysteries and Identities](https://sonofoak.com/en-ca/blogs/news/mysteries-identities-for-players)

## Cortex Prime

### Starting Structure

Cortex Prime is modular. A game selects the Trait Sets that express what
matters in that setting, such as:

```text
Attributes
Roles
Skills
Values
Relationships
Affiliations
Distinctions
Powers
Resources
```

Distinctions are commonly a Prime Set.

A typical Character possesses three Distinctions rated d8 that describe:

```text
background
personality
role in the story
```

### How Background Matters

A relevant Distinction adds its die to the pool.

Every Distinction includes Hinder. The player may replace its d8 with a d4 and
gain a Plot Point.

A game's selected Trait Sets determine whether wealth, relationships,
reputation, or roles remain inside Distinctions or receive their own rated
category.

Some implementations highlight other traits through a Distinction or use
preconstructed pathways and archetypes to assemble starting packages.

### Control Method

```text
fixed number of Distinctions
equal starting die size
usually one trait from each Trait Set enters the pool without added cost
Hinder gives every Distinction a disadvantageous use
the game definition decides which concepts deserve separate Trait Sets
```

### Design Lesson

Cortex treats the campaign's choice of categories as part of game design.
Background, wealth, relationships, and role do not have to be forced into one
container. The system chooses separate Trait Sets only when that distinction
matters to the campaign.

Official sources:

- [Cortex Prime Rules Compendium](https://www.cortexrpg.com/compendium/explore-the-rules)
- [Cortex Prime Codex](https://prod-images.cortexrpg.com/compendium/cortex-prime-game-handbook/CortexCodex.pdf)

## Daggerheart

### Starting Structure

Daggerheart deliberately separates several pieces that other games might call
background:

```text
Class and Subclass
Ancestry
Community
Background questions
two Experiences
Connections to other player Characters
```

### How Background Matters

The answers to Background questions have no explicit mechanical effect. They
shape the Character and the GM's preparation.

Mechanical expression is moved into Experiences:

```text
two starting Experiences
each begins at +2
a relevant Experience may be added by spending Hope
```

An Experience cannot be universally broad and cannot grant spells, powers, or
other specific extraordinary permissions.

Ancestry grants two defined features. Community grants one defined feature.
Class and Domain Cards own extraordinary capability.

### Wealth and Reputation

```text
Wealth or reputation may appear in Background fiction or an Experience when
properly scoped.

Neither Background nor Experience is allowed to replace the defined mechanical
features that grant extraordinary capability.
```

### Control Method

```text
Background fiction is mechanically unranked.
Only two Experiences begin play.
Both use the same starting modifier.
Hope gates their direct numerical contribution.
Breadth and extraordinary permissions are explicitly restricted.
Ancestry, Community, Class, and powers remain separate packages.
```

### Design Lesson

Daggerheart offers the clearest separation between narrative history and its
selective mechanical echoes. A Character's full past is not a bonus. Two
chosen Experiences are the limited parts of that past that can enhance rolls.

Official source:

- [Daggerheart SRD 2.0](https://www.daggerheart.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/DH_SRD_2_2026_08_25.pdf)

## GURPS Control

### Starting Structure

GURPS does not generally use one broad narrative Background rating.

It decomposes background and social position into separately priced traits,
including:

```text
Wealth
Status
Rank
Reputation
Independent Income
Debt
Legal Enforcement Powers
Security Clearance
Claim to Hospitality
Alternate Identity
Allies
Patrons
Contacts
Contact Groups
Dependents
Enemies
Duty
Social Stigma
Cultural Familiarity
Languages
```

### Templates

Character Templates package the Attributes, secondary characteristics,
advantages, disadvantages, and Skills required to fill a role believably.

The Character pays the full total cost of the included traits. Templates are
not discounted package deals and can be customized.

When templates are combined:

```text
duplicate leveled traits use the highest required level
required traits from all templates are retained
conflicting traits identify an incompatible combination
```

### Wealth and Reputation

GURPS treats them as different quantities.

```text
Wealth measures material starting position and economic capacity relative to
the setting.

Reputation specifies the reaction modifier, affected population, and frequency
of recognition.

Status measures social standing.

Rank measures formal authority inside an organization.
```

### Control Method

```text
common Character Point currency
separate pricing formulas for distinct functions
Campaign limits and prerequisites
templates priced as the exact sum of their parts
advantages and disadvantages both affect the same budget
```

### Design Lesson

GURPS achieves precision by refusing to let one background phrase carry all of
its mechanical content. That provides the control case for functional
decomposition.

It also demonstrates the complexity cost of pricing every relationship,
privilege, resource, and social condition independently.


Relevant sections:

## Five Established Background Models

### Model 1: Pure Narrative Background

```text
The Character's history is true and guides play.

It does not directly add a number.
```

Primary example:

```text
Daggerheart Background questions
```

### Model 2: Bounded Narrative Trigger

```text
A small number of freeform truths can modify play.

Their use is limited by slots, activation currency, or one-contribution rules.
```

Primary examples:

```text
Fate Aspects
Daggerheart Experiences
Cortex Distinctions
```

### Model 3: Rated Broad Background

```text
The Background is itself a broad competency rating.

The system caps the rating and prevents several Backgrounds from stacking.
```

Primary example:

```text
13th Age Backgrounds
```

### Model 4: Background Keyword or Theme Package

```text
A broad identity contains or organizes several narrower expressions.

The system defines how the parent and narrower parts relate.
```

Primary examples:

```text
QuestWorlds Keywords and breakout abilities
City of Mist Themes and Tags
```

### Model 5: Functional Decomposition and Point Pricing

```text
Wealth, reputation, authority, relationships, obligations, and other elements
are separately defined and separately priced.
```

Primary example:

```text
GURPS
```

## Common Control Mechanisms

The surveyed games control freeform background value through one or more of the
following:

```text
fixed number of narrative slots
fixed point budget
hard maximum rating
one Background or one Trait per roll
activation currency
separate permission-granting mechanics
paired helpful and harmful expressions
mandatory relationships or obligations
questionnaires that constrain scope
Campaign-defined categories
decomposition into distinct functions
prebuilt packages using existing currencies
```

No surveyed game relies only on:

```text
Write any background phrase and apply it whenever the player can make an
argument.
```

The more freeform the wording becomes, the more visible the limiting structure
becomes elsewhere.

## Background Content Taxonomy

The research shows that the word background commonly contains several
mechanically different things.

| Background Content | Common Treatment in Peer Systems | Existing or Candidate LENS Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Personal history | Aspect, question, concept, Theme | Character concept or stable narrative truth |
| Occupation | Skill package, Keyword, Background rating | Skills and Specializations; possibly identity or Access |
| Learned competence | Skill, Action, Experience, Tag | Skill and Specialization |
| Culture | Heritage, Keyword, package | Classification, Campaign Profile, Language, or stable Lens |
| Social class or Status | Advantage, Aspect, Keyword | Stable social Lens or Campaign relationship |
| Formal office or Rank | Advantage, relationship, package | Access plus institutional relationship |
| Wealth | Skill, ability, Tag, Advantage | Resource or Infrastructure relationship; unresolved |
| Reputation | Aspect, Tag, Advantage | Contextual social Lens; unresolved |
| Affiliation | Relationship, Keyword, Playbook element | Relationship or Infrastructure Lens |
| Contacts and Allies | Separate relationship traits | Relationship Lens |
| Duty or obligation | Trouble, Vice, Flaw, disadvantage | Limitation, Access condition, or relevant Dissonance |
| Signature possession | Extra, Tag, Advantage, equipment access | Equipment or Artifact Lens |
| Extraordinary capability | Stunt, Extra, class feature, power Theme | Classification, HOLD, HOLT, Axis, and Access |
| Character belief or value | Aspect, Identity, Distinction | Stable narrative Lens or Character concept |
| Adversary or rival | Rival, Flaw, disadvantage | Relationship and recurring opposition |

## Immediate LENS Findings

```text
1. Background is not one coherent mechanical category.

2. Wealth, reputation, authority, learned competence, relationships,
   obligations, and extraordinary permission perform different work.

3. A broad Background rating would duplicate Skills whenever it represents
   training or occupational competence.

4. An entirely unpriced narrative Background becomes too loose unless its
   mechanical use is bounded by another structure.

5. The strongest narrative systems do not solve this problem with exact point
   equivalence alone.

6. They instead limit slots, contributions, activation, scope, or the kinds of
   mechanical work a background element may perform.

7. The Campaign Profile is a natural place to define which background
   categories matter, but Campaign Profile approval alone is not a complete
   acquisition or balance rule.

8. GURPS proves that full functional decomposition can be balanced precisely,
   while also demonstrating how quickly that approach becomes a large
   catalogue.

9. QuestWorlds and City of Mist provide the strongest examples of broad
   identity containers that are decomposed into narrower expressions.

10. Fate, Cortex, and Daggerheart provide the strongest examples of broad
    narrative truths whose numerical use is limited by a common currency or
    fixed contribution rule.
```

## Strongest Implication for the Current LENS Question

The next question should not be:

```text
How many Background Points does a Character receive?
```

The next question should be:

```text
Which parts of a Character's background belong to existing LENS layers, and
which important persistent truths currently have no owner?
```

Only after that classification should LENS decide whether the remaining
unowned truths need:

```text
a fixed number of selections
a shared construction budget
an activation resource
a paired Resonance and Dissonance structure
a Campaign Profile package
or no new mechanic at all
```

## Recommended Next Research Test

```text
BACKGROUND FUNCTIONAL CLASSIFICATION TEST

Take the following candidate background elements:

profession
wealth
reputation
social status
formal authority
institutional affiliation
contacts
allies
dependents
enemies
obligations
oaths
cultural identity
languages
signature possessions
ordinary equipment access
extraordinary permissions

For each element, determine:

1. What fictional truth does it establish?

2. Does an existing LENS layer already own it?

3. Does it grant permission or Access?

4. Does it provide stable capability or only contextual Resonance or
   Dissonance?

5. Can it be independently lost, transferred, damaged, opposed, or advanced?

6. Does it require a number during ordinary play?

7. If numerical, is the number competence, magnitude, availability,
   reliability, resource depth, or social effect?

8. What prevents it from duplicating Skills, Equipment, Infrastructure,
   Classification, HOLD, HOLT, Axis, or Access?
```

This classification should precede any LENS Background Point model.
