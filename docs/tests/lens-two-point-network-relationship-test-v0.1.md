# LENS Two-Point Relationship Versus Two One-Point Contacts

Date: 2026-09-05
Status: Conceptual equal-cost test; two-point package proposed, not final-locked.

## Controls

The user requested the two-point relationship test, not a repricing of the
accepted one-point reference. Retain the one-point contact from
`lens-one-point-network-contact-test-v0.1.md` unchanged.

Both portfolios commit two points from a starting Network allocation. They
can use the same Character with Network Rank 2: the Rank remains 2 in either
case. Its ordinary Derived multiplier remains x1.2 when applicable; contact
cost creates no additional automatic multiplier. No Attribute, Training,
Scale, Capacity, or equipment difference is introduced.

Candidate whole-package construction costs are 1 and 2, corresponding to
candidate acquisition Difficulty 1 and 2. These costs are not recurring use
Difficulties. This test does not mathematically calibrate acquiring either
relationship during play or change the Difficulty curve.

## The Two Choices

### Breadth: Two One-Point Contacts

One innkeeper in each of two towns that the campaign visits. Each is normally
reachable during business, holds messages, and shares useful local information
they actually possess. Material support is not included as a dependable
entitlement. An innkeeper may still choose to offer more in the fiction.

### Depth: One Two-Point Relationship

One of those innkeepers, with the same local knowledge and ordinary resources,
is now an established dependable ally. In addition to the one-point support,
they offer temporary lodging, ordinary meals, and safekeeping of a modest
personal load while the Character handles an ordinary setback. They accept
ordinary inconvenience to help rather than treating the Character as another
paying guest. These are stipulated details of this candidate relationship.

The support is personal and local, not free rooms for an unlimited party,
permanent housing, an unrestricted warehouse, major funding, or guaranteed
protection from serious retaliation. A longer or more demanding request may
still succeed through the fiction and existing rules. No universal night count,
favor quota, automatic debt, or refusal is added.

## Scenario Results

| Need | Two one-point contacts | One two-point relationship |
|---|---|---|
| Obtain information in either town | Established source in each place | Established source in one place; no invented remote reach |
| Leave and collect ordinary messages along the route | Both towns covered | One town covered |
| Stay somewhere while replacing lost travel supplies | Lodging can be sought, but free support is not part of either package | Temporary lodging and meals are ordinary agreed support, subject to actual circumstances |
| Leave a pack safely while resolving a local problem | May ask, but this additional service is not established by the packages | Modest safekeeping is included; ownership and liability details follow the fiction, not a guarantee against every event |
| One innkeeper becomes genuinely unavailable | Another contact remains, useful within that other contact's reach | This support route is disrupted; no substitute contact appears automatically |
| Hide from pursuers whose retaliation threatens the innkeeper's life | Not included | Not included merely because the relationship costs two points |
| Obtain an answer neither contact could know | Neither supplies knowledge they lack | Stronger commitment does not create expertise or information |

Support within the established relationship normally needs no roll to win
cooperation again. Resolving an actual obstacle to providing that help can
still require an Expression. Dependable support must remain dependable in
ordinary play; the GM should not negate the purchased benefit through invented
obstacles every time it is used.

## Boundary Checks

The two-point relationship gives access to aid from someone else; it does not
give ownership of the inn or increase personal Assets Rank. This is meaningful
cross-support already permitted by Network, not a reason to forbid material
help. Record who supplies it and the actual scope instead of applying an extra
Assets bonus for the same contribution.

Ordinary help does not automatically consume Framework Capacity or create debt.
Extraordinary strain and specific obligations remain possible through existing
Conditions, not a new contact cost-per-use system.

Depth is not defined as 'exactly twice as much help.' Two points is a proposed
whole-relationship value. The test shows distinct benefits at equal expenditure,
not a proven universal exchange rate between information and lodging.

## Finding and Recommendation

Conditional conceptual pass: neither portfolio subsumes the other in a campaign
that uses both towns and occasional local setbacks. Breadth supplies coverage
and another route; depth supplies a dependable local fallback with material aid.

In a campaign confined to one town, the distant contact may have little practical
value. Conversely, travel that never returns to the ally can make depth less
useful. Starting choices should be evaluated against the Campaign Profile's
actual scope, not nominal geographic reach alone.

Recommend retaining this two-point package as a reference candidate beside the
one-point contact. Do not construct a full ten-rank catalogue from one comparison.
The next substantive step is to apply whole-provision valuation to Authority,
where the provision grants recognized decision-making power rather than another
person's cooperation. Background provisions remain deferred.
