# Combined Defense Allocation Test v0.1

Date: 2026-09-12.

Status: Candidate test and recommendation only. No main-rule adoption.

Subsequent decision, 2026-09-12: the user's "Go ahead" accepted the bounded
cancellation-allocation recommendation and authorized limited-defense testing.
That narrow rule is now recorded in the baseline and terminology reference.
The original test below remains the evidence; its other open questions and the
predeclared-combined-attack eligibility candidate were not adopted by this step.

## Question and Authority

The user requested testing with a thorough explanation of the proposed
defender-directed allocation of cancellation within a combined attack.

The preceding `lens-predeclared-combined-attack-test-v0.1.md` found that
predeclared coordinated attacks can produce the intended concentrated output,
but pooling does not settle which actual hits survive a defense. That matters
when protection addresses particular hits or forms.

Existing authority is the baseline's Multiple-Source Targeted Opposition and
Effect Resolution and Conservation guidance. It requires declared opposition
targets, traceable cancellation, actual protection scope, and ordinary action
accounting. A response cannot switch targets after seeing the rolls. One
whole-result defense is not copied across contributors. Coordination alone does
not establish a universal defensive-response limit.

The candidate being tested adds a choice of numerical allocation within a
declared defense where the fiction leaves a genuine choice. It does not turn
the previously adopted prohibition on retargeting into an after-roll exception.
The broader predeclared-combined-attack eligibility proposal remains a candidate.

## Candidate Procedure

1. Establish the combined attack and its distinct contributions before rolls.
2. Declare the defensive method, target, and actual reach before rolls.
3. Finish individual Expressions, continuations, and any paid replacement rolls.
4. Apply each defensive Effect once to attacking output it can actually oppose.
5. Follow a distribution already determined by the fiction. Otherwise, where
   multiple distributions are genuinely feasible, let the defender choose.
6. Preserve the identities of surviving hits, forms, and targets wherever those
   differences matter to actual protection or consequences.
7. Apply only protection's already-defined function and scope. Do not add an
   ordinary equipment multiplier again, create new mitigation, or manufacture
   defensive surplus from mitigation.

Numerical allocation happens during resolution, not as another action or roll.
It cannot turn a declared parry of one weapon into an evasion of a second.
Being able to reach two contributions does not necessarily permit every
conceivable distribution: the particular response can impose further limits.
The enumeration below deliberately tests the most flexible allocation case;
it does not prescribe anatomical or tactical micro-accounting at the table.

## 1. The Ordinary Shared-Harm Case

Suppose the completed attack Expressions produce four and three Effects, and
the completed defensive Expression produces three. For example, Final Rating
80 with roll 45 generates four; Rating 70 with roll 45 generates three. These
are illustrative completed Ratings, with all applicable prior calculation
already included, not new character benchmarks or fresh equipment bonuses.

The combined attack has seven offensive Effects. The defense has three
cancellation Effects, leaving four. It does not cancel three from each attacker.

If all four surviving Effects have interchangeable consequences and identical
protection treatment, there is no reason to choose a distribution. Seven minus
three is the entire opposition calculation. This remains true if three people
rather than two produced those seven Effects.

Combined opposition does not inherently turn two sword contacts into one
physical contact, nor make several contributors' genuinely merged manifestation
separate hits. Preserve only distinctions that actually remain relevant.

## 2. The Case That Exposed the Gap

For this fixture only, suppose a defined protection mitigates one Effect from
each actual sword hit. This is an explicit Effect-Mitigation function, not an
assumption that ordinary armor or equipment always gets a post-roll subtraction.
Actual equipment placements and protection values are not adopted here.

| Cancel from first / second | Surviving first / second | After per-hit mitigation | Total harm Effects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 / 0 | 1 / 3 | 0 / 2 | 2 |
| 2 / 1 | 2 / 2 | 1 / 1 | 2 |
| 1 / 2 | 3 / 1 | 2 / 0 | 2 |
| 0 / 3 | 4 / 0 | 3 / 0 | 3 |

Every row spends exactly three defensive Effects and leaves exactly four
offensive Effects before protection. The last row completely stops the weaker
attack, so protection encounters only the stronger remaining hit. In the other
rows it encounters two weakened hits. No protection is being copied onto the
same hit, and the fully cancelled hit supplies no mitigation to another hit.

Fictionally, a defender might partially deflect both blows and trust their
protection to handle the weakened contacts, rather than avoid one blow entirely
and take the other at full force. This is a possible account of the numbers,
not a requirement to narrate a new maneuver for every cancelled Effect.

With mitigation two per actual hit, the difference becomes larger: distributing
cancellation 2 / 1 leaves 2 / 2, both absorbed, for zero harm. Distributing it
0 / 3 leaves 4 / 0, for two harm. The choice is therefore not always cosmetic.

## 3. A Defense That Cannot Reach Everything

The attacks still generate four and three Effects. The defender produces five,
but the declared method can oppose only the first attack.

- Four defensive Effects cancel the first contribution.
- The second contribution's three Effects remain.
- The unused defensive Effect cannot move to the unreachable contribution.

This is not seven minus five equals two. That arithmetic would silently broaden
the defense's permission. Numerical success cannot provide missing reach.

The zero-reach script fixture similarly confirms that no amount of generated
output can cancel an unreachable attack. It is an allocation boundary check,
not permission to roll an impossible defense in play.

This test does NOT decide whether another distinct response is feasible and
available under ordinary action and reaction rules. It neither grants a second
free defense nor declares a new once-per-round or once-per-combination cap.
The whole-attack one-roll probability benefits in the preceding record must not
be applied indiscriminately to this limited-coverage case.

## 4. Different Forms and Consequences

An impact contribution generates four Effects, and an electrical contribution
generates three. Defined protection mitigates two impact Effects and no
electrical Effects. A feasible whole-attack response generates three cancellation.

| Cancellation choice | Final surviving output |
| --- | --- |
| All three against electricity | Two impact Effects |
| Two against impact, one against electricity | Two electrical Effects |
| All three against impact | Three electrical Effects |

The first two choices have equal total output, but need not have equivalent
fictional consequences. The defender may reasonably prioritize a form relevant
to their vulnerabilities, ongoing Conditions, or immediate objective. Minimizing
the arithmetic sum is not necessarily their best or only sensible choice.

If the defensive method can oppose only impact, the last row is the only legal
distribution here. The better electrical-defense choices do not become available
merely because the player sees them in the arithmetic.

No universal electrical Condition, automatic additional status effect, or
particular vulnerability value is created by this example.

## 5. Genuine Merging and Limited Protection

If the established manifestation is genuinely one matching effect application,
the same seven attack Effects minus three defense leave one four-Effect
application. Protection defined to mitigate one per application reduces that
to three, once. There is no need for source-level allocation in this fixture.

Conversely, if protection has a single shared reserve of two against the whole
matching result, it removes at most two in total, not two per contributor or
per hit. Four remaining Effects become two irrespective of their distribution
in the tested identical-form fixture.

This difference follows the actual protection rule, not a weapon-versus-power
privilege. Calling attacks a combined beam does not automatically earn merged-hit
treatment. Nor does keeping two sword hits distinct require separate opposition
rolls by itself. Opposition scope and protection scope are related but not
identical questions.

## 6. Failure, Interruption, Specials, and Counters

- Four plus zero can still leave three against one defense where the first
  contribution is independently viable. Failure does not supply negative output.
- An actual intervention that prevents the second contribution changes four
  plus three against five into four against five. Do not refund begun actions or
  erase independently continuing effects merely because their Source is struck.
- Natural 01 at Rating zero followed by continuation 50 completes at -49 and
  generates five Effects. A natural-00 contribution generates none. Against
  three defense, their compatible five-plus-zero output leaves two. Separate
  complications are not erased; this fixture assumes none prevents that output.
- Nine attack Effects against twelve whole-attack defensive Effects leave no
  offensive remainder and at most one fictionally eligible counter-effect, not
  three counters and not one per Source. No applicable counter means none.
- Protection reducing surviving harm to zero does not retroactively create
  defensive surplus. Three defensive Effects against seven attack Effects remain
  insufficient for a counter even when later matching mitigation absorbs all harm.

The script checks completed special-result fixtures, not a new probability
distribution or a complete complication simulation. Partial-scope counter
eligibility while another contribution succeeds is not settled here.

## 7. Numerical Verification

Run `lens-combined-defense-allocation-test-v0.1.cjs` with Node.

The bounded sweep covers:

- Two contributions, each 0-8 Effects; each mitigation 0-3; all four reach masks;
  defense 0-16.
- Three contributions, each 0-4 Effects; mitigation vectors 0/0/0, 1/1/1,
  2/2/2, and 0/1/2; all eight reach masks; defense 0-12.

Result: 140,128 input states and 176,864 legal allocations, plus named fixtures.
All assertions passed. Each state was also checked with contribution order
reversed. There were 7,712 states with different harm totals under different
allocations; the largest spread in this bounded set was three Effects. These
counts are test coverage, not estimates of how often those situations occur in
play, and three is not a universal upper bound.

Checks establish conservation, no out-of-scope cancellation, no cancellation
beyond generated output, invariance under relabeling/order, unchanged total
without mitigation, and non-increasing best achievable harm as defense grows.
Each extra defensive Effect improves the minimum harm total by at most one
under the fixed additive mitigation fixtures.

For independent verification only, the enumerated minimum equals:

```text
Unreachable surviving harm
+ max(0, reachable harm after fixed per-hit mitigation - defensive Effects)
```

This identity verifies these numeric fixtures. It is not a proposal to reorder
mitigation in the rules, reveal secret protection, or derive counters from an
already mitigated attack. Further fictional restrictions on allocation can
remove the mathematically optimal choice.

The first run exposed an incorrect hand-estimated maximum in the three-hit
fixture: 4/3/2 minus four cancellation leaves five across at least two surviving
hits, so one mitigation per hit allows at most three harm, not four. The fixture
expectation was corrected; the independently verified final range is two-three.

## Assessment and Next Decision

The candidate resolves traceability without new dice, extra Effects, or an
automatic armor bypass. It preserves why coordinated output can matter against
a strong defense. Overkill against weak opposition remains permissible; action
commitment and loss of opportunities to redirect those actions still matter.

The cost is a genuine allocation choice for mixed consequences or per-hit
protection, potentially slowing large combinations. Do not pretend it is free
complexity, and do not require allocation when all outcomes are interchangeable.
Do not make players enumerate every distribution or calculate a global optimum.
Ask which reachable contribution the defense prioritizes only when it matters.

Recommendation: where the declared defensive method does not already determine
the distribution, let the defender assign earned cancellation among contributions
that method can actually oppose. Resolve interchangeable output as an ordinary
total. Keep differing protection and consequence identities visible only as
needed. This is a proposed limited allocation rule, not a universal single-defense
rule for anything labeled teamwork.

The substantive adoption decision is whether that bounded defensive choice is
worth its occasional table work. If accepted, the next unresolved test is a
coordinated attack where no single defensive method covers all contributions:
compare feasible whole-attack evasion with limited responses under existing
action accounting, including partial-scope counter eligibility. Do not silently
decide that remaining reaction question through this allocation test.

No baseline, terminology, character, setting, or pricing rules were changed.
