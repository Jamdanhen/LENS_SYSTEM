# Severe HOLT Limitation: Consequence of Use

Status: Authorized scenario and arithmetic test. The 75% Severe adjustment is
an approved test candidate, not a final compensation rate. The six-Load cost
and timing below are test fixtures only, not adopted activation rules.

## Defined Fixture

A four-area HOLT permits extraordinary physical force, physical durability,
movement, and stability, routed through Strength, Toughness, Movement, and
Stability. All four are physically carried in this fixture. This is not a
replacement definition of the Strength HOLT or its other tested packages.

The HOLT has no additional calendar or artifact Access restriction. Each
Expression invoking it adds six levels of a named physical depletion Condition
after that Expression resolves, whether successful or unsuccessful. The cost
is once per invoking Expression, not once per participating Axis. No sustained
effect, stored manifestation, or multiple-use trigger rule is adopted here.

For this scenario, the depletion requires meaningful rest and recuperation,
or a permitted matching recovery Lens. It does not clear automatically at
the end of a turn, scene, or session. Exact recovery duration is not set.
There is no automatic generic healing or immunity bypass in the fixture.
Existing matching recovery remains valid; this is not an unhealable cost.

Hold ordinary ratings, Axis development, and Scale constant. Do not reinvest
the estimate adjustment as points in this test.

## Existing Mechanics Used

Baseline Function Bands:

```text
Load 0-2: 100%
Load 3-5: 75%
Load 6-7: 50%
Load 8-9: 25%
Load 10+: loss or exhaustion of the affected function
```

Physical Load affects applicable Might- and Mobility-carried Expressions.
It does not automatically penalize Mental or Framework Expressions. The same
Load is not charged again as separate numerical Dissonance.

The consequence after resolution changes later Expressions, not the already
resolved result. Function loss at 10+ is not automatically death or
unconsciousness. Specific Condition fiction and the Campaign Profile govern
what the loss means and any Accepted Consequence options.

## Calculated Progression

| Situation | Starting Load | Band for invoking Expression | Load added afterward | Resulting Load | Following physical function |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| First use while fresh | 0 | 100% | 6 | 6 | 50% |
| Second use without recovery | 6 | 50% | 6 | 12 | Functional loss, 10+ |
| First use while already carrying four Physical Load | 4 | 75% | 6 | 10 | Functional loss, 10+ |
| After matching recovery removes three of the first six levels | 3 | 75% | 6 | 9 | 25% |
| After complete matching recovery | 0 | 100% | 6 | 6 | 50% |

The value 12 is the arithmetic total crossing the 10+ threshold, not adoption
of an extended condition track or additional overflow punishment. Stop this
unassisted physical-use sequence at functional loss; do not assume another
physical activation is possible just because the HOLT has no separate Access
restriction. Conversely, do not close every unrelated route or Capacity.

JavaScript evaluation checked all rows and ten Function Band boundary values:
0, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, and 12. Boundary checks passed. No opposed-roll,
win-rate, or encounter-length simulation is claimed.

## Fictional Consequence

A fresh Character invokes the HOLT to force a blocked exit open. The attempt
resolves at the existing 100% Function Band, then depletion raises Physical
Load to six. Subsequent physical climbing, running, ordinary defense, and
other applicable physical actions use the 50% band, even without the HOLT.

If another obstacle requires a second invocation before recovery, it resolves
at 50% and then takes the total past functional loss. The Character can choose
to make that costly effort while still capable, but the resulting loss matters
for what happens afterward. The fixture does not guarantee either attempt
succeeds, nor award success in exchange for taking the Load.

This is a broader consequence than merely losing HOLT Access: ordinary
physical capability also suffers. It is not global incapacity. For example,
an independently permitted Psyche- or Influence-carried action is not assigned
the Physical Function Band just because the same Character is depleted.

## Recovery and Campaign Counterchecks

1. Ongoing pursuit or several consequential encounters without safe recovery:
   the depletion changes subsequent options and risks. Severe is defensible.
2. One decisive activation followed by guaranteed safe, complete recovery:
   the Character repeatedly receives the full-strength use without meaningful
   later exposure. The six-Load label alone does not justify Severe.
3. Rapid matching recovery with meaningful action, resource, or other costs:
   evaluate the actual burden including those costs. Recovery is not cheating.
4. A practical matching counter-Lens routinely neutralizes the consequence:
   reassess the combined package's burden rather than retaining an unchanged
   severe estimate or inventing a prohibition on recovery.

An available Accepted Consequence does not automatically erase the burden:
it trades immediate pressure for the lasting restriction actually accepted.
No new conversion amount or duration is assigned here.

## Estimate and Finding

```text
Four affected areas x 5 = 20 raw scope reference
Candidate Severe adjustment: 20 x 75% = 15
Adjusted reference: 20 - 15 = 5
```

The 75% candidate remains plausible for the ongoing-pressure fixture. The
test does not establish equal value to full-moon Access or validate an actual
purchase price. In particular, cost timing lets a fresh Character use full
capability once, so scenarios ending immediately afterward are a real
counterexample to automatic Severe classification.

The existing Condition, Load, Function Band, and matching recovery procedures
carry the consequence without a new exhaustion subsystem. Six Load is not a
universal threshold that automatically earns a 75% adjustment.

The subsequent overlapping-limitations comparison is recorded in
`lens-holt-overlapping-limitations-test-v0.1.md`. It tests suit dependence plus
depletion, duplicate descriptions, and disjoint or partially overlapping
Access restrictions. No combination rule is adopted by that test alone.
