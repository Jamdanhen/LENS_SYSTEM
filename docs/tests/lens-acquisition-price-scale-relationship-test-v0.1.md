# Acquisition Price, Scale, and Assets Relationship Test

Date: 2026-09-06
Status: Exploratory deterministic and conceptual tests; no new construction rule adopted.

## Objective and Correction

The user clarified that acquisition Scale multiplies the price or resource
requirement of the purchase. It does not multiply the buyer's Assets, grant the
buyer higher Scale, or require a newly invented financial-Scale permission.
The user requested identification and execution of the next tests.

The scope is the relationship among Profile starting value, Assets purchasing
provision, acquisition magnitude, outside support, limitations, and upkeep.
No specific fictional structure receives a Magnitude in this test. Starting
provisions, in-play acquisition checks, HOLT purchasing, limitation discounts,
and upkeep procedures are not finalized here.

## Test 1: Fixed Budget, Increasing Acquisition Price

Controls, chosen only to isolate the relationship:

```text
B = Profile starting-value reference
Available acquisition budget = 50 B
Unscaled test acquisition price = 40 B
Scaled acquisition price = 40 B x 1.6^(M - 1)
```

The 50 B budget is illustrative, not an adopted Rank 10 entitlement. It is the
available amount for this test, without an additional free-package allowance.
The exact treatment of free gear versus additional Assets value stays open.
The 40 B price is an arbitrary test fixture, not an item placement.

The existing x1.6 Scale candidate is reused for arithmetic. Applying it to
prices is under test; the numerical candidate is not final doctrine.

| B | Fixed budget | M1 price | M2 price | M3 price |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 200 | 10,000 | 8,000 | 12,800 | 20,480 |
| 50,000 | 2,500,000 | 2,000,000 | 3,200,000 | 5,120,000 |
| 125,000 | 6,250,000 | 5,000,000 | 8,000,000 | 12,800,000 |

All three Profiles produce price/budget ratios of 0.8, 1.28, and 2.048.
The first purchase fits; the other two exceed available resources. No buyer
rating changes. The Profile values are hypothetical, not published starting
wealth for any named peer game.

Result: mathematically invariant across currency baselines when the relative
price is held constant. This proves translation consistency, not that every
setting prices equivalent goods in the same relative proportions.

## Test 2: Three Ways to Address the Same Shortfall

Use the 12,800 price and 10,000 available budget. The shortfall is 2,800.

| Route | Immediate outcome | Continuing distinction |
|---|---|---|
| No additional support | Purchase exceeds the budget | The current allowance does not finance it. |
| Patron contributes 2,800 | Combined funding meets the price | The actual agreement determines ownership, duties, or restrictions; none is invented automatically. |
| Credit supplies 2,800 | Immediate funding meets the price | A liability remains. No interest rate, automatic Condition, or repayment rule is assigned. |
| A genuinely reduced provision costs 9,600 | It fits with 400 remaining | Lost functions or restricted rights remain real. The illustrative 25% reduction is not a discount schedule. |

Owning a reduced provision and borrowing to own the complete provision are
not the same bargain. Neither changes persistent Assets Rank simply because
funding or the purchase arrangement changes.

A HOLT can affect this test only through a defined contribution. Funding,
permission, or replenishment cannot be inferred from the label Wealth alone.
No HOLT price or bonus is invented. If a future HOLT only improves an
acquisition Expression, it does not thereby establish an unlimited cash grant.

Result: support and limitations can bridge a resource gap without changing
the buyer's Scale. Their exchange rates and construction costs remain open.

## Test 3: Acquisition Is Not Upkeep

- Two physically comparable holdings can have different ongoing support:
  one includes productive resources and personnel, the other does not.
- Purchase by savings or credit does not establish recurring income equal
  to that purchase price.
- Taking control of a holding can establish possession without proving the
  ability to maintain every function indefinitely.
- Stable, adequately supported upkeep can be automatic under existing
  routine-action principles. Disrupted supply, lost income, or overextension
  can establish meaningful uncertainty.

Result: acquisition Rank or price cannot by itself establish a monthly
Difficulty of the same number. A monthly procedure remains a candidate; its
resistance must represent the upkeep situation. No new upkeep schedule,
automatic Framework Load, or separate ledger is adopted.

## Test 4: Count Acquisition Magnitude Once

When a final catalogue price already includes the holding's magnitude, applying
the acquisition Scale multiplier again for the same reason counts it twice.
Use either a within-frame price with an explicit Scale adjustment or a final
price containing that adjustment. Do not import a peer's fully priced castle
and assume it is an unscaled price.

If monetary prices are later translated into acquisition Difficulty, test
that translation against the existing resolution rules. This record does
not remove or modify Scale in ordinary Expression resolution.

## Findings and Next Decision

The corrected candidate passes the fixed-budget and currency-normalization
checks. Larger acquisition requirements can exceed starting means without
requiring the buyer to purchase higher Scale. Support, credit, reduced
provisions, and later acquisition can produce distinct fictional outcomes.

This does not calibrate item Magnitudes, prove that x1.6 matches every intended
magnitude jump, establish a Rank curve, or price limitations or HOLTs.

The next bounded test is Assets Rank progression in starting-package units,
holding acquisition Scale at M1. Compare low, middle, and high Ranks, preserving
the distinction between the free starting package and additional Assets value.
The illustrative 50 B endpoint can be a test control, not a predetermined lock.
