# Classification Package Working Note

Date: 2026-09-06
Status: Discussion and illustrative construction cases. No species package,
purchase price, or new HOLD/HOLT mapping adopted.

## Active Question

Define what choosing a Classification supplies before pricing its package.
Classification can establish innate permission, assumptions, limitations, and
vulnerabilities. A HOLD can define granted Access as part of a package as well
as Access available for further acquisition. Not every permission requires a
separate purchase or a ranked HOLT. Access does not automatically grant training
Ranks or an Expression bonus.

The user corrected the Domain discussion: a Domain may be one type of HOLT,
not a replacement for HOLT or proof that every higher-order capability is a
Domain. Species/type package placement remains under discussion.

Distinguish Access to use an application now from eligibility to acquire a
capability later. Granting the latter does not automatically grant the former.

## Setting-Specific Species HOLDs: User Clarification

The user explicitly notes that a HOLD defining high-fantasy elves may differ
from one defining Tolkien-esque elves. The same principle applies to dwarves,
orcs, Uruk-hai, humans, and other species or entity types.

A shared species name does not establish a universal package of permissions,
development options, limitations, or vulnerabilities. The applicable setting's
HOLD defines that implementation. A difference may concern what is possible,
not merely how large its numerical modifiers are.

LENS supplies the common mechanical grammar; the setting supplies the concrete
definition. These comparisons do not adopt particular fictional traits, new
HOLD/HOLT mappings, or prices. The dwarf candidates below remain examples to
test, not features required of every setting's dwarves.

## Human Package: User Direction To Preserve

The user wants humans considered for a meaningful HOLD-related package granting
Access and possibly carrying a limitation. Humans must not be left as the empty
mechanical choice while other Classifications receive valuable provisions.

This is a recorded design concern, not an adopted Human HOLD, HOLT, bonus,
limitation, or pricing rule. The user explicitly does not want the contents
determined now. Resume human package design later; do not invent compensatory
benefits, mandatory weaknesses, or presumed universal human traits in this note.

## Existing Elf Illustrations

Low-light sight and passage over loose snow were used as illustrative granted
permissions without automatic Awareness, Movement, or Stability increases.
They are setting-dependent examples, not an adopted or complete Elf package.
Cultural languages and traditions require an appropriate established background;
their package treatment is not settled by identifying a species.

## Dwarf Comparison: Original Assistant Candidates

Continue the user's requested dwarf comparison with three distinct questions:

1. Darkvision could grant sensory Access under conditions where ordinary sight
   is unavailable. Its actual limits belong to the setting. It does not supply
   free Observation or Awareness Ranks.
2. A defined stone-sensing faculty could grant perception of otherwise
   inaccessible geological information. That would not by itself grant practiced
   masonry, engineering, or crafting expertise. Ordinary familiarity with stone
   must not be relabeled as a supernatural sense without defining that permission.
3. Resistance to specified poisons tests something different: ordinary people
   may already attempt to resist poison. A genuinely superior resistance needs
   an actual defined mechanical benefit, not merely permission to attempt an
   already-available Expression. Applicable Resonance, mitigation, or a specified
   immunity are alternatives to examine, not cumulative or adopted benefits.

No universal dwarf anatomy, temperament, numerical bonus, immunity, cost, or
required drawback is assigned. The number of examples is not a package quota.

## Construction Implication To Examine

A Classification package may need both permissions and other applicable Lens
functions. Access alone cannot describe every benefit. Use the existing grammar
before inventing a species-specific subsystem; determine which contributions
are innate, cultural, conditional, or independently developed in the chosen
setting. HOLD/HOLT structure and valuation remain open.

## Starting Allowance: Construction Comparison

Status: The user approved testing setting-defined starting packages with
approved variants, without adding a mandatory player-facing provision pool.
Compare complete packages against a common intended starting provision;
values and extra-purchase funding remain unresolved. This approves a test
model, not final provisioning rules or specific species packages.

Purpose: determine where starting-provision balance is handled without
declaring unpriced permissions free or changing the 25 / 50 / 100 pools.

Two approaches were compared:

1. The Campaign Profile supplies setting-defined baseline packages and
   approved variants, evaluated against a common intended provision level.
   The applicable HOLD describes what is granted, what can be developed,
   and the package's limitations.
2. Every Character receives a shared provision budget and selects individual
   features. This needs usable costs for Access, Resonance, and restrictions
   before player allocation is meaningful. The pool's source and size would
   also need decisions; none are adopted here.

| Construction case | Setting-defined packages | Player-allocated provision budget |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Choose the baseline dwarf | Receive its specified provisions; no individual trait shopping needed | Assemble or purchase those provisions using costs not yet established |
| Add extraordinary stone sense without removing anything | Explicit expanded variant; must be evaluated against the common intended provision | Requires a price before determining whether the budget covers it |
| Exchange poison Resonance for stone sense | Compare the resulting complete variant; a one-for-one item exchange is not proof of equal value | Requires comparative costs; number of features alone is insufficient |
| Choose a human | Needs a meaningful package in the same comparison; contents remain parked at the user's request | Receives the same proposed allocation opportunity, but eligible features and prices remain unresolved |

The comparison favors setting-defined baseline packages for the current
development lane because player construction can remain simple while design
work evaluates complete packages. It does not prove any pair of packages
balanced, eliminate the need for estimates, or require every setting to use
the same dwarf or human provisions.

Division of responsibility for the approved test model:

```text
Campaign Profile: intended starting provision and available choices.
HOLD: the concrete grants, development options, and restrictions.
Player: selects the package and any approved variant.
```

An optional expansion is not automatically free, a new HOLT, or a purchase
from Training Points. Its inclusion, exchange, or additional acquisition
remains an explicit setting construction choice. Existing ordinary creation
and advancement budgets remain unchanged.

Decision: use setting-defined baseline packages with approved variants as the
next construction test model, without a mandatory player-facing provision
pool. Common provision level, actual feature values, cross-package equivalence,
and extra-purchase funding remain unresolved. The immediate gain is locating
the balance work in package design, not claiming it has been completed.

## User Qualification: Keep Package Negatives and Limitations

The user explicitly retained negative features and limitations as part of
package development. The package model must not become a comparison of free
benefits alone. It must examine meaningful Dissonance, vulnerabilities, Access
restrictions, dependencies, and other applicable burdens alongside provisions.

This does not yet establish a mandatory number of drawbacks, a matching
penalty for every benefit, or a universal dwarf weakness. An existing package
burden must not be counted again as separate compensation. Additional benefits
can also change how burdens function, so review the whole proposed variant.

### First Negative-Feature Comparison

Assistant test candidate only: this setting's dwarf physiology experiences
visual interference in ordinary bright daylight that does not impede an
otherwise matched ordinary observer. Represent it as relevant Dissonance to
sight-dependent Expressions, not a global Attribute penalty or automatic Load.
No amount, severity band, or species rule is adopted.

| Context | Consequence for package comparison |
| --- | --- |
| Unlit underground exploration | Darkvision helps; the daylight drawback is inactive |
| Daylight travel requiring visual scouting | The candidate drawback creates an actual disadvantage, not merely loss of darkvision's benefit |
| Campaign almost entirely underground | The same drawback may carry little practical balancing value |
| Package B touches stone to use its nonvisual sense in daylight | That route may bypass the visual interference; it does not cure the underlying visual sensitivity or reveal unrelated information |

The expanded sense can therefore add permission and provide an alternative
approach around a particular weakness. It should not inherit an unchanged
assessment merely because the written drawback is identical. Do not invent
an extra penalty for using a legitimate alternate sense.

Darkvision failing to reveal room contents through a wall is a scope boundary,
not by itself a negative relative to the ordinary observer. Similarly, a
contact requirement is part of the defined stone-sense permission; it does
not automatically earn a second, separately valued limitation adjustment.

Result: package negatives remain useful and must be represented concretely.
This example demonstrates their assessment, not equivalence between daylight
Dissonance and the benefits, nor adoption of daylight sensitivity for dwarves.
The user subsequently selected Dense Build as the leading negative candidate,
instead of adopting the daylight-sensitivity example. Human-package contents
remain parked.

### Selected Negative Candidate: Dense Build

Working scope, including the user's added jumping application: relevant
Dissonance when bodily density hinders jumping, buoyancy, or crossing
weight-sensitive surfaces. Jumping can qualify even with solid takeoff and
landing surfaces. It is not a global
Mobility penalty, automatic loss of swimming Access, or a fixed movement-rate
reduction. No magnitude or final species package is adopted.

The authorized underground, surface, and water comparison is recorded in
`../tests/lens-dwarf-dense-build-context-test-v0.1.md`. It identifies active
hazards and inactive controls, preserves genuine countermeasures, and shows
that stone-observation benefits may help identify a safer route without
removing the weight constraint.

The candidate survives that applicability review. The subsequent authorized
numerical comparison is recorded in
`../tests/lens-dwarf-package-modifier-comparison-v0.1.md`. The user then approved
x1.10 for both applicable Resonance traits and x0.90 for Dense Build as working
test values. The x1.20/x0.80 comparison remains unselected. Final values and
whole-package balance remain unresolved; a meaningful drawback does not
automatically offset all package benefits.

The assembled package is in
`lens-dwarf-starting-package-working-reference-v0.1.md`. The follow-on exchange
comparison in the stone-sense test record replaces stone Observation Resonance
with sensory Access as an assistant-proposed variant, not an approved equal
exchange or automatic added provision.
