# Return to Area Growth and Complementary Output v0.1

Date: 2026-09-12.
Status: Comparison only. No area rate, measurement, band ladder, rounding rule,
or additional Difficulty-composition procedure is adopted.

## Return Point

The user approved feasible coordinated execution as combined-assault entry
guidance and explicitly directed a return to complementary output and area
growth. The entry guidance is now in the baseline and terminology reference.
No further approval of already-resolved defensive allocation is needed.

The prior proportional-area test left two leading candidates: each additional
Effect Level assigned to growth adds 10% or 20% of the ORIGINAL declared area.
In both candidates, growth is additive against that original amount, not
percentage compounding. Area growth has not been abandoned or confined to a
universal within-band ceiling.

The prior single-Source scan found no full-anchor shortcut at 10% within its
tested domain, while 20% retained advantages up to ten percentage points.
Both retain immediate Difficulty-edge advantages. Those results are bounded
single-Source findings, not a proof about all combinations. See
`lens-area-proportional-growth-test-v0.1.md`.

## Controls

- Use coverage, not radius, for these numerical examples. The exact measurement
  and connection from one ordinary unit to an area ladder remain unresolved.
- One surviving area Effect establishes the declared area in the candidate.
  Only additional output allocated to growth enlarges it.
- Each participating Source has actual Access, means, shared execution, and
  action commitment. Complete area-capable Effects are supplied as fixtures.
- The full assessment must include whatever range, area, and other actual
  burdens apply before output is generated. This comparison does not grant
  a distant enhancer a range-free roll or adopt a new sum of Difficulty ranks.
- All shared area contributors reinforce the SAME declared coverage. This is
  not adding independent area footprints, choosing the largest contributor's
  declaration after rolling, or counting overlapping footprints twice.
- A declared separate harm contribution retains its function. No harm is
  copied per newly covered unit or automatically increased by area growth.
- Cancellation examples assume an actual permitted response directed at area
  output. Neither mandatory separate delivery rolls nor extra defense rolls are
  created here.

## Example 1: Shared Area Output

Two contributors reinforce declared coverage 100. They generate three and two
compatible area Effects: five total. One establishes the shared declared area;
four are available for expansion if assigned there.

| Candidate | Added coverage per additional Level | Four additional Levels | Final coverage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10% | 10 | 40 | 140 |
| 20% | 20 | 80 | 180 |

Do not subtract one baseline from each contributor before adding their output.
Both earned successes are real Effects under the adopted multiple-Source rule.
Three plus two equals five, not four. There is only one shared declared area
to establish in this fixture; it does not award another baseline after pooling.

If actual opposition cancels two area Effects, three remain. One sustains the
declared area and two can enlarge it: coverage 120 or 140 respectively. If all
five are cancelled, this area contribution supplies no successful coverage;
do not give its baseline back after cancellation.

Zero area output does not by itself erase an independently viable manifestation
supplied elsewhere. Follow actual dependencies and residual-effect rules rather
than inventing a universal collapse or a fallback radius.

## Example 2: Area Enhancement Plus Harm

One contributor supplies three area Effects; another supplies four harm Effects.
The declared coverage is again 100.

The area contribution establishes 100 and can spend its two additional Levels
to reach 120 at 10%, or 140 at 20%. The distinct harm contribution remains four
Effects before applicable opposition and protection.

It is not seven area Effects, nor seven harm Effects. It is also not four harm
automatically applied at full strength to every unit of coverage. Actual harm
allocation and target application must preserve existing conservation. This
comparison establishes no new per-target area-damage rule.

Additional area output could instead support a separately permitted refinement
when an actual valuation exists. No free precision around allies, persistence,
or automatic conversion to damage is supplied by these growth candidates.

## Example 3: Declared Ambition With Two Contributors

For a controlled stress fixture, each Source has pre-Difficulty Rating 160 and
rolls 40. Each faces the same area-only comparison ladder used previously:
25/50/100/200 coverage at D1/D2/D3/D4. These are illustrative band assignments,
not adopted values or a complete ranged-attack assessment. All remaining
shared area output is assigned to growth.

| Declared coverage | Final Rating each | Effects each | Final area at 10% | Final area at 20% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 25 | 156 | 12 | 82.5 | 140 |
| 50 | 94 | 6 | 105 | 160 |
| 100 | 56 | 2 | 130 | 160 |
| 200 | 33 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

At 10%, more ambitious successful declarations retain greater final coverage in
this fixture. At 20%, declaring 50 and declaring 100 both reach 160 despite
different Difficulty. That is a pressure on declaration incentives, not proof
that the two choices have equal success probability. These are fixed rolls.

Teamwork's extra output is not inherently a loophole: both Sources committed
their actions. Nonetheless, declarations and surplus conversion still need to
preserve a meaningful reason to attempt the larger challenge. Do not generalize
the old single-Source no-full-anchor-shortcut result to multiple Sources without
a dedicated comparison of both cooperative routes.

The fractional 82.5 is intentionally shown. This test does not quietly floor
each contribution or decide how partial coverage appears on a hex map. At the
25 anchor, 10% adds 2.5 per Level; 20% adds five. Simpler arithmetic at that
anchor is a real advantage of 20%, not universal freedom from fractions.

## What Remains Unresolved

- Selecting a working growth percentage.
- Final area measurement and Difficulty bands, including the ordinary one-unit
  starting reference.
- Grid rounding and fractional coverage.
- A cooperative declaration-shortcut probability comparison if the candidate
  is taken into broader testing. The fixed-roll table does not answer it.
- Exact complementary refinements such as additional shaping or persistence.

None of these questions reopens adopted Effect addition, defensive allocation,
counter handling, or coordinated-assault entry. The pricing and award review
remains queued after current output work.

## Recommendation

Use 10% of original declared coverage per additional area Effect as the next
working candidate. It is the stronger supported choice for preserving declared
ambition, while still producing meaningful cooperative growth. Keep 20% as the
more generous, simpler-at-current-anchors alternative, with its known tradeoff.

This is a recommendation for continued testing, not a claim that ten percent
has solved every boundary or establishes a universal conversion for other
functions. No band cap or automatic Difficulty reduction is proposed.
