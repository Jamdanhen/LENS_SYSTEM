## Candidate Retail Provisions

### Two Points: Modest Trading Counter

- Established use of one counter in a functioning shared market during normal
  trading hours, with ordinary display space and a small secure storage space.
- Personally controlled, modest stock in one stated inexpensive line of everyday
  goods, such as thread, buttons, and ordinary sewing notions.
- Ordinary transaction money and working resources supporting that stock's
  normal turnover; no substantial surplus reserve.
- A normal existing arrangement for obtaining replacement stock when the
  Character can pay for it. This is commercial availability, not a free loyal
  supplier contact or guaranteed credit.

This can be a longstanding livelihood run by an experienced person. It is not
necessarily a beginner's business. It supplies neither ownership of the market
building nor a separate shop, staff, expensive stock, or private transport.

### Five Points: Established Small Rented Shop

- Established use of a separate small shop with its own sales area and storage.
- Ordinary fixtures and personally controlled stock across a defined everyday
  product range, broader and deeper than the counter's sewing-notions stock.
- Working resources for ordinary transactions and replenishment of that stock.
- No building ownership, employees, luxury inventory, or substantial surplus
  investment fund implied.

The retained five-point workshop is the comparator: both establish a usable
fixed business facility beyond a portable kit. The workshop concentrates value
in installed trade equipment; the retail shop concentrates it in inventory,
fixtures, and working resources. Equal cost is a proposed whole-value judgment,
not proof that every workshop and every shop have equal value.

The two-point counter is a smaller positive provision, not the five-point shop
with an invented three-point limitation refund. Limitations pricing stays open.

## Scenario Tests

| Probe | Two-point counter | Five-point shop | Finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open for ordinary trade | Has the site, kit, stock, and working resources to do so | Has the corresponding resources at the larger stated scope | Both are operational starting arrangements, not kit-only descriptions. |
| Complete an ordinary sale within the established trade | Routine sale remains automatic absent real uncertainty | Same | Neither requires a Difficulty roll simply to earn an ordinary livelihood. |
| Replace normally sold stock | Uses receipts and established working resources through the existing supply route | Same principle for its larger actual stock | Replenishment is not free merchandise. Distinct disruption or insufficient means can create an actual problem. |
| Meet a large order exceeding inventory and available funds | Needs another acquisition or financing route | May meet more of the order from established stock, but still has finite means | No automatic new cash or credit from calling either package a business. |
| Divert goods to personal use or equip companions | May use the goods actually controlled; those goods are then unavailable for sale | Same, with a broader stated inventory | No blanket ban on useful stock, but also no duplicate business and expedition supplies. |
| Sell a valuable weapon requested by an adventurer | A sewing-notions counter has no such weapon merely because it is a shop | Only if the specified shop's actual product range contains it | Retail competence is not universal inventory. |
| Leave the premises arrangement | Retains personally controlled stock, portable tools, and funds; loses use of the market site | Retains personally controlled stock, tools, funds, and removable fixtures; loses rented premises access | Material ownership and premises access have distinct consequences. |
| Leave on a long expedition | Goods do not sell themselves; someone must actually run the counter if trade is to continue | The larger shop also has no automatically supplied employee | Neither package grants passive income merely because it exists. |
| Face a contested bargain | Existing appropriate capability and Training apply | Same with the same Character, goods, and circumstances | The package price is not a bargaining multiplier; actual material leverage can matter when the scene changes. |

No numerical ongoing revenue, upkeep, Load, or asset-condition procedure is
created. Ordinary turnover describes the starting situation, not immunity from
later financial pressure or a perpetual income guarantee.

## Comparison with the Accepted Two-Point Mount

The counter supplies local earning activity, controlled saleable goods, and
working resources. The mount supplies travel or carrying capability and is
itself a controlled asset. Both can be sold or lost through the fiction.
Neither replaces the other's function. A traveling campaign may favor the
mount; a settlement-centered campaign may favor the counter.

Their difference does not require a prohibition on profitable trade or on
selling starting possessions. It requires an honest package description and
Profile judgment about expected usefulness. The present scenarios support
trying the counter at two, but do not establish economic or currency parity.
