## Limitation Negotiation Guidance - 2026-09-15

The user retained the comparison examples as provisional GM-negotiation
guidance. Duration, Area, Distance, Intensity, and Selectivity help describe
lost capability; they are not an automatic discount grid. The earlier
25-step sketch below is exploratory history, not a requirement to define
or tally every step.

Use 20% as a starting comparison for losing one substantial dimension of
useful capability, not an entitlement or a claim of equal practical value.
Compare the unrestricted capability, actual restriction, and campaign context.
Several smaller restrictions may justify a similar estimate through their
combined burden; trimming unused extremes across five dimensions does not
automatically earn 20%.

Retained examples:

| Restriction | Provisional comparison |
|---|---|
| Single target only; no area effects | Strong 20% reference candidate when area was otherwise available. |
| Contact only | Strong 20% reference candidate when meaningful reach was otherwise available. |
| Range reduced from six miles to five | Usually minor in close-range play; potentially meaningful in distant engagements. |
| No independently persisting manifestations | Potentially substantial for barriers, illusions, or constructs; less so for immediate results. |
| Cannot spare eligible targets within an area | Meaningful where companions or protected targets are exposed. |
| Only one eligible target type | Depends on what it excludes in the campaign, not the number of target labels. |
| Restricted intensity | Meaningful when expected challenges become unavailable; unused extremes alone do not establish equivalent loss. |

Preferring the restricted style does not erase the lost options. A close-combat
specialist may still accept a real contact-only limitation. Judge overlapping
restrictions together: inability to spare targets within an area adds no burden
when that same capability cannot produce an area at all. These examples do not
set final prices, advancement caps, a universal ceiling, or automatic stacking.
