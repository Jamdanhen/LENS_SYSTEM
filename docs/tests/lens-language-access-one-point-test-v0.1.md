# One-Point Language Fluency Test

Status: Tested candidate subsequently adopted by the user, 2026-09-07.

Adoption: one unrestricted point buys one additional, narratively justified
ordinary language, without expertise ranks or automatic languages per Background
Rank. Preserve existing free grants and exclude protected Training funding.
The comparison below preserves the test reasoning; the baseline and terminology
reference now carry the working provision.

## Objective

Test ordinary language fluency as a specific provision without making
Background Rank a language count or replacing purchased expertise.

Authority: current baseline construction and carryover rules, unified pricing
adoption record, and the user's approval to run this test. This record does not
change the baseline or the separate experimental branch.

## Candidate

One unrestricted point purchases ordinary fluency in one narratively justified
language not already granted by the Character's established history or Campaign
Profile. The proposed creation funding is the ordinary flexible allowance, not
protected Training. This is a proposed addition to eligible purchases.

The provision supplies ordinary communication, not Task, Field, Specialty, or
Precision ranks. Background facts can supply particular languages; Background
Rank does not automatically supply that many languages.

## Case Comparison

| Case | Candidate result | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Background 4, deeply rooted in one monolingual community | No automatic four-language grant | Rank need not represent varied linguistic history. |
| Background 1, established multilingual upbringing | Retain the particular languages established in construction | Low Rank does not revoke coherent existing fluency. |
| Profile already grants a common language | No additional charge | No duplicate purchase of an existing provision. |
| Character adds a plausible language learned before play | Spend one eligible point; ordinary conversation is normally automatic | Specific fluency has a tangible construction result. |
| Fluent Character negotiates with an unwilling seller | Resolve the relevant Expression if uncertainty remains | Shared language permits communication, not agreement or a free expertise multiplier. |
| Fluent Character encounters ambiguous ancient writing | Apply existing applicability, Access, Difficulty, and expertise rules | Ordinary fluency does not guarantee specialist interpretation. |
| Character requests an entirely inaccessible secret language | Point expenditure alone cannot establish the missing learning route | Price does not override Closed Access or narrative eligibility. |
| Character buys several justified languages | Separate purchases; no new numerical language cap | This does not buy universal translation or unknown languages. |
| Character loses Background function under Framework pressure | Previously established language knowledge is not erased | Loss of Background leverage is distinct from losing learned competence. |
| Character retains unused creation funds | Only eligible unrestricted funds carry; unused Training is lost | No route from protected Training leftovers to language provisions or HOLTs. |

These are qualitative architecture checks, not probability simulations or
evidence that all languages have equal value in every campaign.

## Budget Check

Using the current 125 ordinary flexible points as the proposed eligible source:

| Additional languages | Candidate cost | Flexible points remaining | Protected Training |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | 0 | 125 | 750 |
| 1 | 1 | 124 | 750 |
| 4 | 4 | 121 | 750 |
| 10 | 10 | 115 | 750 |

This is deliberately inexpensive provision buying, not a substantial constraint
on multilingual characters. A single Specialty Rank costs five points; five
languages at the candidate price would cost the same. Their benefits differ:
ordinary communicative coverage versus a ranked contribution in applicable
Expressions. Neither automatically substitutes for the other.

## Boundaries and Recommendation

The candidate fits the architecture without a Background entitlement formula,
new resolution procedure, or automatic training ranks. Recommend one point as
the working ordinary-language provision, subject to adoption.

Do not infer that a small numerical price means small narrative value. A
language-centered campaign may make fluency central to access and discovery.
Campaign Profiles should identify relevant language scope and existing grants;
this test does not establish a universal price for extraordinary translation.

Literacy, dialect coverage, and historical variants should be stated where
material to the provision or Profile. No separate universal literacy ladder or
automatic per-dialect charge is proposed here.

Acquisition during play still requires narrative eligibility. Spending a point
does not spontaneously teach a language in the middle of a scene. This test
does not impose a mandatory training subsystem.

Decision resolved: the user adopted the ordinary provision and eligible funding
as stated above. This test does not settle prices for contacts or other lasting
provisions.
