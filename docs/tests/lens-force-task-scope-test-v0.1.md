# LENS Force Task Scope Test v0.1

Date: 2026-09-03

Status: User-requested conceptual test. Force is a naming and scope candidate,
not adopted terminology. The approved shared scope includes lifting,
supporting, pushing, pulling, and dragging. Its name remains open. Social
pressure examples below test a possible extension; they do not silently adopt
it or change the baseline.

## Question

Does Force describe a recognizable practiced activity, including useful
lateral applications, or does it become a universal bonus for overcoming
anything difficult?

This is an applicability, language, and training-scope test. It is not a
simulation, an empirical player test, or proof of equal purchase value.

## Two Meanings Under Comparison

The narrower formulation discussed before this test:

> Apply directed pressure to move or support something against resistance.

The broader candidate tested here:

> Apply directed pressure to cause or prevent a change against resistance.

The second formulation can describe deliberately pressing someone to yield
as well as moving or supporting a load. It is broader than the five approved
applications. Neither formulation means simply "succeed despite Difficulty."

## Controls

- Keep the agreed Attributes, Derived Attributes, and Task / Field / Specialty
  architecture. Force does not become a new Attribute or a universal Lens.
- Existing permission, Access, Scale, Difficulty, opposition, Effect, and
  compound rules apply. No second damage, resistance, or influence contest.
- Force is a candidate for the Task slot, not an extra factor automatically
  added to Strike, Discharge, Command, or other Tasks.
- Differences in direction, duration, technique, medium, or target do not
  alone require separate Tasks. Do not restrict the word to a fixed Attribute
  family or an equipment whitelist to make the examples pass.
- An unranked ordinary attempt remains possible. Task Rank neither creates
  the means of pressure nor supplies a missing HOLT, resource, or authority.
- Only meaningful uncertainty requires resolution. Every lifting, holding,
  opening, request, or moment of effort does not require a roll.
- The examples are stipulated fictional situations. They are not exclusive
  routing rules, mandatory substeps, or rules for all possible uses of an item.

## 1. The Five Approved Applications

| Application | Stipulated Expression | What practice contributes | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lift | Strength x Force: raise a heavy gate. | Arrange and apply upward pressure against the load. | Fits the proposed activity. |
| Support | Strength x Force: keep that gate from falling while others pass. | Maintain effective support against downward pressure. | Fits even when nothing moves; clearer than Displace for this case. |
| Push | Strength x Force: press a crate clear of a blocked passage. | Apply pressure in a useful direction while managing the resisting load. | Fits. |
| Pull | Strength x Force: draw the crate toward oneself with a rope. | Manage the pull and the load's resistance. | Fits without a separate directional purchase. |
| Drag | Strength x Force: draw an incapacitated companion out of danger. | Manage a resisting load during relocation. | Fits without adding Protect or Treat merely because it is a rescue. |

These are abbreviated capability / Task pairings, not complete numerical
stacks. Might is the parent of Strength; applicable Fields, Specialties,
equipment, and other relevant Lenses are omitted for clarity, not removed
from the system. Calling the Task Force does not replace natural Strength.

The practice being purchased concerns applying pressure effectively, not
creating a larger natural force rating or making every technique familiar.

## 2. Lateral Routes Already Supported By The Working Architecture

| Route | Stipulated action | Why it can remain the activity |
|---|---|---|
| Psyche x Cognition x Force | Free a heavy obstruction using a lever and understood mechanical advantage. | The arrangement of applied pressure supplies the solution. This preserves the accepted Cognition-with-Lifting teaching route. |
| Mobility x Coordination x Force | Raise, lower, and position a load through crane controls. | Controlled application through machinery, not personal Strength, supplies the attempt. Task Rank does not determine the crane's lifting Scale. |
| Influence x Control x Force | Pull a resistant object using an already permitted telekinetic grip. | The Character regulates an externally directed pressure. The Task does not grant telekinesis. |

Finding: the label can retain ordinary and extraordinary cross-Attribute
uses without becoming a Strength-only Task. A high Force Rank is not a reason
to use the largest Derived Attribute regardless of what supplies the action.

## 3. The Same Obstacle, Different Activities

Consider a locked door. "Force it open" is not enough information to select
the Task without knowing what the Character actually does.

| Declared activity | Candidate treatment | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Apply pressure through a pry bar until the fastening yields. | Force, through the actual capability supplying the application. | Strong fit. The resulting damage does not require a new breaking Task or a second damage contest. |
| Deliver a blow that breaks the fastening. | Strike remains applicable. | The blow does not automatically acquire a Force multiplier because it involves physical force or is described as forcing entry. |
| Blast the fastening with a directed energy bolt. | Discharge remains applicable. | The output's force and the desired result do not establish another Task contribution. |
| Pick the mechanism so it releases. | Use the actual learned manipulation of the lock; final Task naming for that activity is not settled here. | Success against resistance is not enough to justify Force. Do not invent a lock-picking Task name to complete this test. |
| Present recognized authorization and have someone open it. | Use the actual Authority-based interaction. | Access or cooperation may resolve the situation without applied pressure or even a roll. |

These are clear teaching cases, not a universal rule that every contact is
either an impact or sustained pressure. A brief shove can still fit Force;
duration alone does not decide the Task. Actual ambiguity remains subject to
the existing applicability discussion rather than a mandatory taxonomy.

## 4. Does "Force An Agreement" Have A Real Lateral Use?

### Case A: Actual Personal Pressure

The Character presents a credible adverse consequence and presses for a
specific concession. The attempt concerns inducing a decision through that
pressure, not merely delivering a speech.

Candidate route:

```text
Influence x Manipulation x Force
```

Why this is more than a word substitution: there is an identifiable pressure,
a target able to respond to it, and practiced management of how the demand is
pressed. A successful response might be reluctant compliance, not sincere
agreement or changed belief. Existing resolution determines the outcome;
the Task grants no automatic obedience or extraordinary mental Access.

Assessment: plausible under the broader candidate, not established by the
approved load-handling scope alone.

### Case B: Institutional Pressure

An official brings a credible, applicable sanction to bear to obtain
compliance from an unwilling party. Their recognized position, not personal
magnetism, supplies the pressure in this stipulated scene.

```text
Leverage x Authority x Force
```

Assessment: plausible broader lateral. Rank does not manufacture the office,
jurisdiction, or available sanction. A routine accepted instruction is not
automatically this activity; Command and other interactions retain their uses.

### Case C: Material Pressure

A Character with actual control of necessary financing presses for revised
terms by credibly withholding that financing.

```text
Leverage x Assets x Force
```

Assessment: plausible broader lateral. Money is not an automatic Force
contribution; controlling and applying the pressure is the declared activity.
Simply purchasing an item or offering mutually favorable terms does not
become Force merely because Assets participates.

### Case D: A Compelling Explanation

The Character supplies evidence and reasons that persuade another person.
Calling this "forcing them to see the truth" does not independently show a
Force contribution. Use the actual reasoning or communication activity.

Assessment: the phrase describes the desired result without establishing
the tested pressure activity. This does not permanently prohibit Cognition
from Force: the lever example already shows a clear Cognition route.

Finding: the broader meaning produces credible lateral candidates; it does
not require treating all persuasion, communication, or Authority as Force.
Whether that broader practice is a desired purchase remains a user decision.

## 5. Tests Against Universal Application

| Claim | Finding |
|---|---|
| "I force myself to remember the answer." | Trying harder does not identify a distinct pressure-management activity. Use the relevant recall or reasoning Expression. |
| "I force my body to resist the poison." | Desire to overcome a condition is not a new contribution. Use the applicable physical capability and genuine practiced response. Force Rank does not create immunity. |
| "I force the lock open by picking it." | The method matters; wording does not replace the practiced activity. |
| "I force the opponent backward with my shot." | Discharge can already produce that result. Do not add Force automatically. |
| "I keep holding the load, so nothing happens." | Preventing a change can be the consequential objective. The support application remains meaningful even with no displacement. |
| "There is resistance, so Force applies." | Rejected as a universal argument. Difficulty or opposition establishes a challenge, not every Lens that contributes to meeting it. |

These are tests of insufficient justifications, not permanent prohibitions
on surprising narrative applications. The existing relevance rule remains
the standard; Force does not need a new exceptions list or an extra mandatory
declaration procedure.

## 6. Task, Supporting Lens, And Result

The same scene can contain pressure in several roles:

- The Character actively applies it as the uncertain practiced activity:
  Force may occupy the Task slot under the candidate scope.
- A deadline, threat, or institutional circumstance supports another activity:
  the relevant Lens may affect that Expression without adding Force as a Task.
- The narration describes a result as forced: the word alone adds nothing.

This does not make pressure a new formal Lens classification. It applies the
existing requirement for a distinct, relevant contribution. A legitimate
compound remains possible under existing rules, but incidental pressure does
not automatically produce Force x Strike, Force x Command, or another stack.

## 7. Training Transfer And What The Test Cannot Prove

If Force is adopted with the broader scope, its Task Rank would genuinely
travel across applicable load handling and coercive-pressure uses. A Field
or Specialty can add relevant expertise, but an unranked Field cannot become
a new prohibition invented to prevent that transfer.

For example, Force Rank 8 supplies the existing x1.8 Task multiplier wherever
the adopted activity applies. It does not give the same Final Rating in a
gate-lifting attempt and an institutional confrontation: their Attributes,
Derived Attributes, expertise, Access, Scale, and opposition can differ.
It also does not grant a relevant asset, threat, or power where none exists.

That breadth is a real purchasing consequence. The proposed transferable
practice is managing how pressure is applied and maintained. This test finds
the narrative correspondence plausible; it does not prove that the same
full Task multiplier is priced appropriately across the final catalogue.
No numerical simulation or budget change would settle that naming decision.

For the already approved five applications, substituting Force for the
unnamed Task at identical Ranks leaves the numerical stack unchanged.

## Conclusion And Recommendation

Force passes the conceptual test as a candidate for the approved activity.
It handles stationary support more naturally than Displace and retains the
accepted machinery, mechanical-advantage, and extraordinary examples.

Its natural broader meaning also supports credible personal, institutional,
and material pressure applications. Those are not automatically a mechanical
failure or evidence that the word must be restricted to physical action.
However, they go beyond the previously approved load-handling scope and
cannot be silently adopted as a naming-only change.

The blanket objection "every action overcomes resistance, therefore Force
cannot work" is too strong. The actual risk is failing to distinguish the
practiced application of pressure from trying hard or describing a result.
The shared applicability rule already provides that distinction; this test
does not require another resolution rule.

Recommendation: keep Force as a strong candidate and present the broader
pressure scope for deliberate user consideration. The remaining decision is
whether that broader learned activity is intended, rather than whether Force
is categorically invalid. Displace remains the user's provisional leading
name until a further decision. No final name or extension is adopted here.
