# Package 2: Access-Only HOLT Review

Subsequent decision: adopted into core LENS on 2026-09-13. See
`../design/lens-core-three-package-adoption-v0.1.md`. Historical review language
below does not override adoption; explicitly reserved conversion and compensation
questions remain unresolved.

Date: 2026-09-13
Status: Exploratory review completed. No main adoption, character conversion,
refund, acquisition grant, price change, or award recalibration authorized.

## Recommendation

Advance Access-only HOLTs to a bounded working playtest comparison at acquisition
40 and Axis 15, without adding a replacement purchased ceiling. Review it first
independently, then alongside Package 1. The proposal is architecturally cleaner
for directly developing permitted applications, and the present engine does not
require parent HOLT Rank to calculate an Expression.

This is not a cosmetic deletion. It removes shared purchased headroom and its
economy of developing several Axes within one theme. It also frees substantial
funds for ordinary development or additional themes. Those are design choices
to observe, not reasons to automatically reject the model or restore the removed
expense through a uniform Axis price increase.

The strongest remaining adoption boundaries are limitation compensation,
Rank-based starting provisions, and existing-character conversion. The model
can be tested without compensation credits or converted characters while those
remain unresolved. It should not silently replace them in main.

## 1. What Parent Rank Actually Does

The current baseline gives the parent Rank three related functions:

1. It measures overall potential within a Theme.
2. It supplies one purchased ceiling shared by the Theme's Axes.
3. It bounds some provision and limitation-reinvestment procedures.

It is not an Expression multiplier. Permission, Axis applicability, and the
two-required-Axis mean do not depend numerically on it. No additional universal
resolution use was found in the reviewed baseline and terminology sections.
This does not assert that every possible setting has no Rank-based requirement.

The shared ceiling has a real economic purpose. Once a parent is sufficiently
developed, the next eligible Axis costs only its own ranks. A player developing
one Axis pays all of the headroom cost, while a broad developer distributes it
across several Axes. Removing that arrangement favors early concentrated growth.

Whether separately purchased potential is desirable is a design question, not
a prerequisite of the Lens engine. Under this proposal the HOLT remains the
character's acquired thematic permission, while the HOLD remains the definition
governing that category's construction and setting behavior. Neither must become
a multiplier merely because the parent number is removed.

## 2. Horizon And Access

The Rank-10 baseline horizon is sufficient to bound the proposed numerical Axis
multiplier at x2.0. Do not add a Derived-to-Axis cap without a separate reason.
The ordinary D<=A cap remains unchanged.

An Axis 10 on a weak ordinary foundation is not complete competence. For example,
A2/D1 and unranked Training yield 2.2 x2 = 4.4 before Difficulty under Package 2
alone. The high Axis does not supply missing underlying capability or expertise.
This is arithmetic illustration, not a new starting character.

The distinction between permission and development already exists: at acquisition,
an Open Axis 0 can be used without a bonus under current rules. The candidate does
not newly grant that behavior. Closed Access stays Closed; Condition Access still
requires its stated condition. Neither Axis ranks nor purchased Access grant
every possible Axis, indefinite persistence, additional actions, or automatic Scale.

The horizon bounds the multiplier; it does not prove that every thematic package
or starting allowance is balanced. A Profile may define its actual provisions
and eligibility. No new delay, mandatory training regimen, or hidden gate is
introduced to compensate for cheaper numerical development.

### Important Scope Caution

An Axis is not necessarily one spell, tool, or named technique. It is the HOLT's
relationship through one Derived Attribute. It may improve several permitted
Expressions using different Tasks and Fields. The phrase "one application" must
not be used to price Axis development as though it necessarily covers only one
narrow manifestation. Existing Specialty/Precision scope remains distinct.

## 3. Costs And Pace

All supplied cost and session calculations were verified. These are complete
acquisition-plus-development purchases, not purchases on top of an already-owned
parent Rank. Acquisition includes one charge only.

| Target | Current | Access-only | Savings |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Access, no Axis ranks | 40 | 40 | 0 |
| One Axis 1 | 55 | 55 | 0 |
| One Axis 5 | 275 | 115 | 160 |
| One Axis 10 | 550 | 190 | 360 |
| Three Axes 5 | 425 | 265 | 160 |
| Five Axes 5 | 575 | 415 | 160 |
| Five Axes 10 | 1150 | 790 | 360 |
| Twenty Axes 10, where all are actually permitted | 3400 | 3040 | 360 |

The last row is only an economic breadth control, not a required or automatically
available Axis count. The highest required parent Rank is purchased once, not
once for each Axis. On an owned theme, raising its leading capped Axis costs
55 under current rules versus 15 in the candidate. Raising an Axis beneath an
already-sufficient current parent ceiling costs 15 in either system.

At unchanged 14-point saving sessions, one Axis 5 takes 20 -> 9 sessions,
one Axis 10 takes 40 -> 14, and three Axes 5 take 31 -> 19, including acquisition.
At ten saved points per session, candidate Access plus Axis 10 takes 19 sessions.
These dates illustrate the purchase change; they are not objections based on
retaining a particular award rate. No awards or milestones were adjusted.

### Why Not Immediately Increase Axis Prices?

No single Axis price reconstructs the removed shared expense:

| Target to match at the same total price | Required replacement Axis price |
| --- | ---: |
| One Axis 5 | 47 |
| Three Axes 5 | About 25.67 |
| Five Axes 5 | 21.40 |
| One Axis 10 | 51 |
| Five Axes 10 | 22.20 |

These are diagnostic equivalents, not recommendations. A uniform increase changes
the distribution of costs again, especially for broad developers. Retain 40/15
for the initial playtest rather than silently reinstating the parent charge.

### Limitations Become More Immediately Spendable

At 125 eligible points, acquisition prices 40/30/20/10 permit a highest single
Axis of 2/2/2/2 under the old parent structure versus 5/6/7/7 in the candidate.
Remaining points are retained in the results; the old builds could use them
elsewhere. This does not establish automatic discounts or new compensation.

The existing distinction still works: acquisition reductions value the actual
whole-HOLT limitation, subsequent Axis ranks remain 15, and an eligible buyoff
can use the difference between acquisition estimates. Closing Access or removing
a limitation still requires actual eligibility. Narrow theme wording alone is
not an automatically compensable limitation.

## 4. Concentration, Breadth, And Additional Themes

Concentration becomes proportionally cheaper. The one-Axis-10 package falls by
about 65.5%; five Axes at 10 fall by about 31.3%. The marginal price of another
Axis remains meaningful, but there is less incentive to spread development just
to make previously purchased headroom worthwhile.

At 190 points, the candidate permits these distinct portfolios, assuming actual
thematic eligibility and scope:

- One HOLT with one Axis 10: 40 + 150.
- One HOLT with five Axes 2: 40 + 150.
- Four HOLTs with two purchased Axis ranks distributed among them: 160 + 30.

These are not equal fictional value claims. They demonstrate a real choice
between deep numerical development, several moderately developed routes, and
broad permission with little numerical improvement.

The additional-theme price is unchanged, but saved parent expenditure can buy
more themes. At the old 550-point price of Access plus one Axis 10, the candidate
could buy that same developed Axis for 190 and, if eligible, spend the remaining
360 on nine additional 40-point HOLTs. Their Axes could remain unranked but Open.
That is a significant possible increase in repertoire even without higher output.

Access-only breadth was already available cheaply under the current architecture.
This proposal does not invent it; it makes that breadth easier to combine with
deep Axis investment. Review permission breadth and actual counters, not just
raw multipliers, when evaluating those portfolios.

### Two-Required-Axis Check

Keep the adopted mean, not multiplication or minimum. With one new HOLT and
125 points, current development can buy Axes 2/1 for 125, producing x1.15 when
both are genuinely required. The candidate can buy a total of five ranks for
115: 5/0 or 3/2 each produces x1.25 in that combined Expression, provided both
Axes have Access.

The portfolios are not otherwise identical. A 5/0 character has stronger solo
use of one route and no bonus on the other; 3/2 supports either solo use more
evenly. This equal-sum behavior follows the existing linear modifiers and mean.
Removing the parent ceiling makes extreme allocations easier to reach, so it
is worth testing whether a frequently used solo Axis becomes the obvious sink
for investment when the second Axis is used only in combination.

No required Closed Axis is averaged Open. No second full Effect budget or
new action is created. This does not settle three-or-more-Axis combinations.

## 5. Competition With Ordinary Development

The supplied 125-point example is correct: current Access plus Axis 2 costs
110, leaving 15; Access-only plus Axis 5 costs 115, leaving 10. It is not an
optimized whole purchase plan. The remaining points and ordinary alternatives
can change which allocation yields the best result.

The test therefore exhaustively enumerated legal upgrades on selected existing
routes using exactly the same additional 125-point eligible allowance. It did
not spend the 1200/1000/750 protected allocations, alter existing ranks, or use
the pre-existing ordinary-only allowance again. Ordinary-only options buy no
HOLT. Mixed options must pay acquisition and may distribute the remainder among
ordinary ranks and one Axis. All prices remain 60/20/10/10/5/5 and 40/15.

### Package 2 Alone: Single-Route Numerical Bests

| Starting route | Ordinary upgrades only | Current HOLT plus upgrades | Access-only HOLT plus upgrades |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A5/D5 and all four Training ranks 5 | 102.89 | 84.48 | 84.48 |
| Existing guide signature | 311.04 | 304.13 | 329.93 |
| Existing guide broad firearm | 82.08 | 76.74 | 93.02 |

Numbers are pre-Difficulty Expressions, not percentages. The all-five fixture
is not asserted to be a complete character. For the broad-firearm objective,
Specialty/Precision remain inapplicable: the optimizer is not allowed to turn
the requested broad capability into a new narrow attack to inflate its result.

The all-five route favors ordinary expertise at this budget, and its best mixed
purchase reaches only Axis 1 under either architecture. In contrast, the more
developed guide benefits from cheaper Axis investment. Its strongest candidate
signature allocation is A8/D7/T9/F9/S10/P10 and Axis 4: 25 ordinary points plus
100 for acquisition and Axis development. It spends all 125 and yields 329.93.

The same comparison with Package 1 gives ordinary/current-HOLT/access-only raw
values of 137.18/112.64/112.64 for the all-five route and
326.40/337.92/363.89 for the guide signature. Full ranks, costs, and spare points
are in the results file.

This supports the package's prediction: Axis development becomes particularly
attractive when ordinary Training is already developed or near its ceilings.
It does not dominate every route from the beginning.

These are numerical comparisons, not equal-permission claims. An ordinary upgrade
cannot substitute for HOLT Access when the desired action is otherwise impossible.
The HOLT examples assume a permitted enhancement genuinely supporting the existing
method; they do not automatically transfer firearm or medical expertise to a
different kind of manifestation. No new universal theme or application is adopted.

## 6. Interaction With Package 1

Separate two comparisons:

- At identical ordinary and Axis ranks, Package 2 changes no Expression number.
- At identical funding, it can change the Axis ranks that can be purchased.

The factorial control uses unchanged all-five ordinary ranks and dedicates 125
eligible points to acquisition plus the highest single Axis. It leaves residual
funds unspent to isolate the two changes; the optimized comparison above handles
mixed spending separately.

| Architecture | Axis | D1 success | D2 | D3 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Current | 2 | 44% | 26% | 16% |
| Package 1 only | 2 | 59% | 35% | 21% |
| Package 2 only | 5 | 55% | 33% | 20% |
| Both | 5 | 74% | 44% | 26% |

At A5/D5, Package 1 supplies x4/3 and the affordable Axis change supplies
1.5/1.2 = x5/4. Together they give x5/3, exactly offsetting one x0.6 Difficulty
step in this controlled case. This is a useful relationship, not an adopted
Difficulty discount or a universal equivalence at other Attributes/budgets.

The low all-three route changes D1 13% -> 25% and D3 4% -> 9%. The all-five
broad route without Specialty/Precision changes D1 19% -> 33% and D3 7% -> 11%.
Such low probabilities are not themselves defects when the declared challenge
is genuinely difficult. Evaluate whether the assigned challenge matches the
fiction and intended capability, rather than demanding uniformly high success.

Both maximum ordinary foundations remain 320, and a single Rank-10 Axis makes
the same x2 contribution under either HOLT architecture, giving 640 before other
factors in that expanded stack. This is not a universal Final Rating cap or a
permission to multiply several Axes together.

### Opposed Output Sensitivity

Use the existing guide's signature route with a permitted HOLT contribution,
a distinct D3 shot challenge, and the guide's unchanged allocations on feasible
Field-supported defense. The defender does not receive an invented defensive
HOLT bonus. This is an intentionally asymmetric purchase illustration, not equal
spending by both combatants or a representative complete encounter.

| Model | Attack / defense Ratings | Any offensive remainder | Mean remainder | At least ten remainder |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Current | 76 / 29 | 69.94% | 2.92 | 0.79% |
| Package 1 only | 84 / 31 | 77.28% | 3.51 | 0.86% |
| Package 2 only | 95 / 29 | 88.85% | 4.53 | 3.79% |
| Both | 105 / 31 | 94.06% | 5.41 | 11.03% |

The increased output is the intended benefit of purchasing greater capability;
it is not automatically excessive. It does establish that unchanged maximum
output and faster access to existing ranks can materially alter encounter stakes.
Test defensive investment and actual protection alongside offensive investment,
rather than assuming all characters receive equal proportional benefits.

Function handling also remains intact: the all-five D3 control at
100/75/50/25% Function changes success from 16/12/8/4% to 26/20/13/6% with both
packages. No Capacity increase, relaxed Access, or special-result rewrite occurs.

| Existing characters' purchased parent ranks | Saved construction data and characters using current architecture | Separate conversion decision; neither automatic refunds nor free Axis redistribution are authorized. Previously bought headroom may be partly or wholly unused. |

Its current parent-limited arithmetic is a historical comparison, not universal new compensation doctrine. |

The limitation-return issue is substantive, not just deleting the words "parent
Rank." Do not convert every forfeited hypothetical Axis-10 ceiling into spendable
value. Likewise, an old grant of parent Rank 5 is headroom, not five developed
Axes or a mandate for an automatic point refund. Package-specific replacements
must preserve their intended benefit, not an assumed conversion.

Other parent-cap references in the glossary, teaching summaries, and current
pricing record would require wording updates after adoption. The two-Axis mean
explicitly uses Axis modifiers, so it needs no replacement formula. Limitation
buyoff based on acquisition-price differences can remain structurally intact;
its future calibration is not settled by this review.

## Next Evidence And Decision

The next playtest should retain the proposed prices and include a one-Axis
specialist, a multi-Axis practitioner, a permission-broad character with several
HOLTs, and an ordinary-development alternative. Compare the actual outcomes
each can attempt, not only one attack number. Include a highly trained ordinary
foundation, because that is where Axis purchases became most competitive.

No numerical necessity for another purchased parent or a Derived cap emerged.
No claim of complete package balance emerged either. Recommend advancing this
architecture with the stated legacy-provision decisions reserved. The announced
award package can now be reviewed independently, then the combined system can
be assessed without treating current awards as an obstacle to either proposal.
