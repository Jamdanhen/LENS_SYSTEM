# LENS Starting-Package and Animal Equivalency Test

Date: 2026-09-05
Status: One-point equipment-package benchmark approved provisionally; animal and companion proposals remain candidates.

## Scope

The user requested checking one complete ordinary starting package as a candidate
one-point Assets benchmark, adding a domestic animal and cart, an ordinary pet,
and a trained pet or companion to the comparisons.

Sources: the approved Assets whole-provision references, the two-point
cross-character comparison, and the Network relationship test. Preserve the
existing guaranteed package. The subsequent approval replaces the older one-point
ordinary-weapon reference only. Saved Characters and equipment modifiers are unchanged.

Use a grounded fantasy Profile. The initial package means its ordinary approved
weapon or equivalent, protection, auxiliary choice, adventurer's gear, one
professional kit, and ordinary necessary accessories. It does not mean the most
expensive item imaginable in each slot. Exact eligible contents remain Profile
work. No real-world prices, biological capability estimates, or probability
simulation are used to manufacture numerical equivalence.

## Equipment Benchmark

Approved provisional reference: one purchased Assets allocation point provides an additional complete
ordinary package, or a Profile-defined alternative of comparable material value.
The free baseline is received once under the starting Profile. It establishes
starting readiness; its free provision is not evidence that its contents have
no value. Allocation expenditure does not reduce persistent Assets Rank.

This replaces the ordinary extra-weapon reference; it is not an
additional rule allowing the same ordinary sword to cost one point alone while
the whole package containing it also costs one point. Exceptional items may
have different value, but none is priced here.

| Comparison | Result |
|---|---|
| One additional package versus one extra ordinary weapon | The package includes the weapon and substantially more. At equal cost, the earlier single-item reference is dominated. The approved revision therefore replaces that ordinary-item example. |
| Two additional packages versus one riding mount | Packages can equip two people who already exist or supply an equipment reserve. The mount enables mounted travel. Neither contains the other; the two-point mount remains plausible for testing. |
| Two packages versus the two-point trading counter | Portable equipment versus established premises access, modest inventory, and working resources. Both are substantial ordinary provisions. Actual resale and earning equivalence remains uncalibrated. |
| Five packages versus the five-point workshop | More ordinary gear does not automatically supply installed equipment or usable premises; the workshop does not contain five personal loadouts. Both need actual item scopes before financial parity can be claimed. |
| Equip another person | The equipment may be given or lent to them. Their existence, cooperation, expertise, and relationship are not purchased by the equipment package. |
| Replace one's existing kit with a more suitable package | Relevant alternative contents can be selected where the Profile permits. No automatic equipment-modifier increase follows from spending a point. |

Finding: a materially useful benchmark with clearer breadth than a spare item,
not a verified currency unit or a universal equipment-upgrade entitlement.

## Animal and Cart

Candidate example: one ordinary working animal with established cart-pulling
training, harness, and a serviceable small cart. State its actual riding, pack,
and hauling capabilities in the Profile; 'domestic' alone does not state them.

Tests:

- Road haulage: the combination provides a genuine transport arrangement, not
  merely a decorative possession. Loads still need to exist.
- A route the cart cannot traverse: establish what the animal can do separately.
  Do not assume it loses all usefulness when unhitched or invent a restriction
  merely to preserve the price comparison.
- Rider travel: cart-pulling training alone does not establish all riding
  capability. Conversely, honor riding ability if actually included.
- Cart loss: the surviving animal and harness remain real assets.
- Equal animal control: if the animal has every useful capability of the already
  priced two-point pack animal, adding the cart cannot justify pricing the whole
  combination at one while keeping the former at two.

Recommendation: compare the working-animal-and-cart package in the two-point
band first. If it includes the same pack capability plus meaningful additional
haulage, review the existing pack-animal package alongside it. Same-cost coarse
bands may tolerate small differences; major extra utility needs a real valuation
decision. Do not declare a specific species inferior or cripple its capabilities
to force the package into one point.

## Ordinary Pet

An ordinary household pet fits the earlier distinction between ordinary life
facts and purchased consequential starting provisions. Candidate treatment:
the Profile may include an ordinary pet among personal belongings without
requiring an entire extra Assets point. This is not a universal free-animal rule;
rarity, acquisition value, or unusual capabilities may change the comparison.

Natural behavior remains possible and relevant. A pet can notice, make noise,
or react in the fiction without having every such moment separately purchased.
Not charging a point does not make the animal mechanically nonexistent.

## Trained Pet

Candidate one-point example: an ordinary watchdog with established training to
accompany its handler, respond to ordinary cues, and alert to an approaching
unfamiliar presence it can perceive. Training is dependable in ordinary
circumstances, not certainty against concealment or every dangerous situation.

| Probe | Result |
|---|---|
| An unfamiliar person approaches within perceptible conditions | The established alert behavior is useful; do not require purchasing a second relationship merely to receive normal trained cooperation. |
| An intruder successfully avoids the dog's perception | Alert training does not supply impossible information or guaranteed detection. |
| The party needs a lost person's trail followed | Tracking expertise is not established by watchdog training alone. Evaluate the actual animal, rather than deriving every form of training from 'trained pet.' |
| The animal reacts physically to danger | Its actual capabilities and the ordinary resolution architecture apply; absence of purchased combat training does not prohibit natural action. |
| Someone expects a fully trained scout, tracker, fighter, and tireless guard | That is a broader package than the stated watchdog. Re-evaluate its whole value rather than treating 'companion' as an unlimited inclusion. |

Recommendation: the described watchdog is plausible beside one complete ordinary
equipment package as a one-point candidate. No universal pet modifier, perfect
loyalty, action cap, or new animal-training subsystem is introduced.

## Companion Relationship

'Companion' does not specify the priced benefit. It might describe the ordinary
pet, the trained watchdog, a powerful mount, or a capable independent ally.
Price what is actually promised: capability, availability, and established
cooperation, using the same whole-provision reasoning as other assets and allies.

The Network ally examples provide a comparison for a relationship supplying
dependable independent aid. This does not mean every animal requires Network,
every companion costs two, or that Assets and Network must both be charged for
the same support. Which starting allocation establishes an unusual companion
relationship remains a construction question when a concrete case requires it.
No 'pet class' or companion subsystem is created to settle the label.

## Follow-Through: Pack Animal Versus Animal and Cart

### Fixed Animal Control

Use the same sound ordinary working animal in every case. For this fictional
specimen, establish both pack-carrying and cart-pulling training before the
comparison. Hold its health, temperament, carrying and pulling capabilities,
availability, and ownership constant. Do not attribute differences to breed,
species, or a convenient deficiency. These are stipulated game capabilities,
not quantitative zoological claims.

```text
A: Animal + fitted pack gear.
B: Same animal + harness + serviceable small cart.
C: Same animal + fitted pack gear + harness + small cart.
```

The cart is suitable for local and ordinary road haulage. It is not a freight
wagon, armored vehicle, mobile workshop, or automatically stocked store.

| Situation | A: Pack gear | B: Cart equipment | C: Both |
|---|---|---|---|
| Move a load within the animal's ordinary pack capability | Ready with fitted load gear | Requires an appropriate way to secure the load if not using the cart | Ready with fitted load gear |
| Move a load established as cart-haulable but beyond pack capability on a suitable road | Cannot move that whole load as one pack | Can haul it with the cart | Can haul it with the cart |
| Take a route that admits this animal but not this cart | Use pack gear | Animal can continue; carrying the goods depends on actual available equipment, not loss of its underlying ability | Leave or store the cart and use pack gear |
| Cart breaks or is stolen | No cart to lose | Animal and surviving equipment remain; repair or replacement is a separate action | Same, with pack gear as an available fallback |
| Leave unnecessary equipment behind | Possible | Possible | Possible; ownership of the cart does not require dragging it everywhere |
| Sell the animal or equipment | May seek buyers for actual transferable property | Same | Same; possessing extra equipment is additional material value |

Finding: A and B have different immediate equipment functions. C contains both
and is materially better equipped. Do not claim terrain disadvantages make C
equivalent to A: the Character can leave the cart behind. One point for C while
retaining two points for A would make the older price indefensible on these
controls.

### Compare Two Possible Pricing Treatments

```text
Separate additions:
  A stays at 2.
  C costs more, with the extra equipment separately valued.

Whole transport package:
  Expand the 2-point working-animal reference to C.
  Ordinary suitable pack and cart equipment are included.
```

The first is possible, but the current references do not establish the price
of a cart or its fraction of a complete equipment package. Assigning another
whole point would be a new judgment, not arithmetic derived from prior prices.

Recommend the second for this provisional reference. The one-point equipment
reference now values a complete ordinary kit, so provision-level pricing is
already the adopted direction. This proposal openly increases what the earlier
two-point pack-animal package includes. It does not pretend A and C have equal
contents or establish a universal rule that all accessories are free.

### Cross-Reference Checks

- Against two one-point equipment packages: the transport provision provides
  movement and hauling; the equipment packages outfit people or create reserves.
  The transport provision contains no cargo or additional adventuring kits.
- Against the two-point counter: both are complete modest working arrangements.
  One is mobile hauling equipment; the other has a fixed trading site, stock,
  and working resources. Both can support paid work without guaranteed income.
- Against the ordinary riding mount: include its suitable ordinary riding gear.
  A cart does not automatically confer riding capability or equal travel speed.
  Actual additional capabilities already established for either animal remain
  usable; do not invent exclusive 'riding' and 'hauling' classes.
- Against the three-point war-trained mount: the proposed ordinary working
  package does not add war training. Preserve that reference's distinct benefit
  and leave its exact equipment contents unchanged here.
- Against the five-point workshop: a cart supplies neither installed professional
  machinery nor a working shop merely because tools can be carried on it.

Finding: the expanded package has a plausible place at two alongside the existing
material references. This remains a qualitative construction judgment; exact
resale or income parity has not been demonstrated.

### Concrete Recommendation

Broaden the provisional two-point working-animal reference to one ordinary
working animal with suitable pack gear, harness, and a serviceable small cart.
The cart may be selected when appropriate to the animal and Profile; not choosing
or carrying it does not change what that specific provision permits. The separate
ordinary riding-mount option remains available. This is not permission to add
every transport function or expensive accessory to either option.

The decision now is whether to adopt that expanded two-point provision, not
whether to authorize another comparison. If adopted, update the affected starting
allocations without changing their totals and return to assembling the existing
shopkeeper's remaining provisions. Pet, watchdog, and broader companion prices
remain candidates; Background and limitation discounts remain deferred.

### Equal-Spending Probes

- One dog versus one complete equipment package: the dog adds trained animal
  assistance; the package adds a substantial equipment reserve or equips another
  existing person. Neither supplies the other. One point is a plausible coarse
  provision value, not proof of equal sale prices.
- Two dogs versus one riding horse: two dogs provide more hunting assistance
  but do not supply the horse's personal transport. Their real cooperation remains
  usable; no new action restriction is invented to protect the comparison.
- One hunting bird versus one riding horse: aerial hunting assistance versus
  personal transport. The bird does not automatically transmit its observations
  or become a remote-controlled scout. Two points is defensible for this defined
  trained provision, but is not a universal price for every hawk or falcon.
- Draft transport versus riding horse: a complete hauling arrangement versus
  personal riding transport. Keep all actual animal capabilities. No forced
  species restriction is needed; neither package includes the other's equipment.
- Draft transport versus the currently approved pack animal at two: the extra
  cart makes the former materially better equipped when the animal is otherwise
  identical. Recommend expanding that pack-animal reference to the whole modest
  hauling provision, rather than pretending the contents are equal.

### Peer Calibration and Limits

horse at 8 gp. These support a 1:2:2 comparison as a starting analogy, not a direct
hunting-dog price or a LENS conversion formula. Its guard dog is much cheaper.
