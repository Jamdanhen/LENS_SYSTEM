# Advancement and Reward Trigger Peer Survey

Date: 2026-09-06
Status: Research comparison requested before designing LENS award categories.
No award values, triggers, cost curve, or new reward currency adopted.

## Scope

Identify what published games recognize as worth rewarding, and how that can
support consistent session-end judgment. Separate advancement, character revision,
and expendable play resources. This is a selected cross-system survey, not an
exhaustive inventory or a claim that every edition uses the same rules.

## Published Comparators

### Dungeons & Dragons: SRD 5.2

The advancement text identifies overcoming challenges and completing adventures
as sources of XP awarded by the GM. This is broader than merely encountering
something, and the survey does not equate XP exclusively with killing. It does
not extrapolate this edition's text to every historical edition.

Source: https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/srd/5.2/SRD_CC_v5.2.pdf

### GURPS Fourth Edition

Steve Jackson Games' publisher article describes the Campaigns recommendation
of session awards for good roleplaying and advancing the mission. The article
separately proposes adapting criteria to genre, including humor in a comedy
campaign. Genre variants are advice, not universal mandatory core triggers.

Source: https://www.sjgames.com/ill/archive/July_08_2012/You_Might_Have_A_Point_There
Publisher text was available through indexed search; direct retrieval returned 403.

### Blades in the Dark

Individual XP recognizes desperate action rolls, a playbook's characteristic
approach to challenges, expression of beliefs/drives/heritage/background, and
trouble involving vice or trauma. Addressing the playbook challenge need not
succeed. End-session triggers distinguish occurrence from repeated occurrence;
players assess their own triggers. Downtime training also gives XP.

This combines incentives for risk, character expression, trouble, and practice
rather than evaluating only successful outcomes.

Source: https://bladesinthedark.com/advancement

### Dungeon World: First Edition

End-session advancement recognizes resolving a character bond with the other
player's agreement, fulfilling alignment, important discoveries about the world,
overcoming a significant enemy, and noteworthy treasure. Group questions award
everyone; the individual questions differ. Misses on move rolls also generate
XP during play, so failure need not mean absence of advancement.

Sources:
- https://roll20.net/compendium/dw/End%20of%20Session
- https://www.dwsrd.org/playing/playing-the-game.html

Do not confuse these rules with the newer DW2 designer preview found in the
search, which uses different group paths and currencies.

### Fate Core

Milestones reflect session/scenario/arc transitions and significant fictional
developments. Minor milestones primarily permit revision; significant milestones
add skill development; major milestones accompany larger changes and greater
power. Not every milestone is an XP award or a numerical improvement. Exclude
the SRD page's explicitly unofficial consensus sidebar from rules authority.

Source: https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/advancement-change
SRD endorsement: https://fate-srd.com/

### Ironsworn

The publisher describes vows as the organizing commitments of play and their
fulfillment as the route to experience and new abilities. The reward recognizes
completion of committed quests, not each incidental activity as an independent
XP source. This survey does not import its progression tracks into LENS.

Source: https://tomkinpress.com/pages/ironsworn

### Call of Cthulhu: Official Introductory Rules

Successful rolled use can mark a skill for a later improvement check. This
recognizes exercised capability rather than awarding a general pool for
performance. Improvement becomes harder as the skill value rises. This is a
different reward delivery mechanism, not a proposed replacement for LENS's
shared advancement pool.

Source: https://cthulhuwiki.chaosium.com/rules/rewards-of-success.html

### Burning Wheel: Designer Explanation

Thor Olavsrud distinguishes revealing something true about a character
(Embodiment) from confronting and breaking a character belief (Moldbreaker).
The explanation also discusses contribution to the group's progress. The
source is designer commentary in a one-player adaptation discussion, not a
complete core award table. These Artha rewards are not interchangeable with
ordinary direct skill-advancement points.

Source: https://forums.burningwheel.com/t/earning-artha-for-1-player-and-1-gm-embodiment-workhorse-and-mvp/22248

## Candidate Recognition Categories: Survey Synthesis

These group the evidence for discussion; they are not mandatory or mutually
exclusive LENS awards:

1. Participation or reaching a session/story breakpoint.
2. Advancing or completing an objective or commitment.
3. Overcoming a meaningful challenge.
4. Discovering important information or obtaining a significant reward.
5. Expressing identity, belief, motivation, or background in play.
6. Developing a relationship or changing a conviction.
7. Facing risk, trouble, failure, or consequences.
8. Exercising or training a capability.
9. Supporting group progress or the intended genre experience.

Observation: triggers can assess accomplishment, process, or character change.
They are not all different ways of measuring victory. A session with failure
can still contain risk, meaningful choices, discovery, or relational change.

## Award Administration

The sources show several alternatives: GM assessment, player self-assessment,
shared end-session questions, automatic triggers during action, and story
breakpoint awards. Smaller numerical awards can still have explicit criteria;
their precision and the purchasing economy are separate issues.

For LENS's next design discussion, concrete examples and explicit triggers may
reduce ambiguity compared with an undefined good-roleplaying grade. This is an
inference from the procedures, not measured comparative usability. Do not treat
theatrical performance, airtime, or GM agreement as necessary proof of character
expression. Determine which categories fit every LENS game and which should be
Campaign Profile choices before pricing them.

## Limits

Shadowrun and Vampire were searched but no sufficiently inspected edition-specific
primary award table was added to this comparison. Indexed Shadowrun Third Edition
rulebook text mentions individual portrayal and planning awards, but direct
retrieval timed out; this survey does not endorse the earlier broad suggestion
that those games lack roleplaying recognition. Exact comparisons require the
intended edition. No new scan of unrelated local projects was performed.

No experience amounts, final progressive/flat choice, advancement unlock, or
extra reward track follows from this survey. Next substantive decision: choose
what LENS should recognize universally versus what belongs to a Campaign Profile.
