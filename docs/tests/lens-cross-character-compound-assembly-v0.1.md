# Cross-Character Compound Assembly

Date: 2026-09-11
Status: Exploratory comparison following withdrawal of the lead-helper default.
No cross-character formula, shared-load translation, or new team rule adopted.

Correction, 2026-09-11: The user identifies greater combined output in return

The library is a development reference, not independent rule authority, but

The assistant withdraws the recommendation below to pursue whole-participant
averaging as the leading model. Its arithmetic remains a historical candidate
comparison only. Single-Source Axis combination must not be confused with

same Effects; it does not erase separately earned output. Exact combination
values remain unresolved, and no universal sum, synergy premium, or bonus to
every outcome dimension follows from this correction.

## Starting Principle

Identify the coherent activity and what each character actually contributes.
Multiple actors do not inherently mean multiple independent attempts, one
privileged lead plus bonuses, or an automatic sum of complete Expressions.
Distinct functions, dependencies, partial counters, and conserved Effects
remain governed by the existing compound architecture.

The adopted two-Axis mean averages Axis modifiers inside an Expression. It
does not settle how Attributes, Tasks, and expertise from different characters
are assembled. Applying a mean to complete participant Expressions is an
experimental extension, not a quotation of an adopted rule.

## Fastball Special

Neutral description: one character throws a willing partner, who attacks the
target as part of that delivery. Treat the integrated maneuver as the activity
under examination, not automatically as a throw followed by a separately
purchased attack and an additional team attack.

| Contribution | Supplies | Does not automatically supply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thrower's applicable bodily or extraordinary Throw Expression | Launch, trajectory, delivery of the partner | The partner's trained attack or permission to use their equipment/powers |
| Partner's applicable attack Expression | The directed attack made through that delivery | The thrower's strength, range, or launching capability |

Actual route selection still follows narration: bodily force, timing, or
coordination may carry different performances. No particular Attribute is
made the exclusive owner of Throw or Strike by this example.

The maneuver's gain can be delivery to a target the partner cannot otherwise
reach this way. Cooperation need not automatically increase damage to provide
meaningful capability. Conversely, actual extra impact is not prohibited; it
must have an identified contribution rather than a free duplicate Effect pool.

Dependencies and counter checks:

- Preventing the launch before release can prevent the delivered attack.
- Disabling the thrower after release does not retroactively remove momentum.
- Preventing the partner's intended attack after launch does not erase their
  travel. Any remaining collision follows the actual situation and permissions,
  not an automatic second full attack budget.
- A target's successful defense cancels applicable output through current
  opposition. No defender receives a free counterattack just because the
  offense involved two people.
- Both characters commit their actual participation under normal timing.
  This is not a new free attack, doubled action allowance, or roll per named
  body movement. Exact placement of a combined action across initiative is not
  independently adopted here.

Delivery is not automatically a separately purchased Effect merely because
it is one part of the description. If the declaration seeks separately
allocated effects, preserve the existing shared budget; do not charge arbitrary
new Effect costs to make the compound description work.

## Shared Lift

Neutral description: two people jointly lift a beam. Each supplies genuine
physical support and participates in handling the same load. The contributions
need not have different Task names to be real.

First establish whether the combined people, equipment, positions, and access
can support the intended load. This is an assessment of actual means, not a
claim that high success probability manufactures unlimited lifting force.
If the resulting activity is routine, no coordination roll is required merely
because two people participate.

If uncertainty remains, a compound resolution can represent joint execution.
However, an unchanged mean of two equal Ratings cannot on its own express
both unchanged handling competence and increased aggregate physical support.
Do not treat that mean as a complete lifting-capacity model.

If a necessary lifter releases their end, reassess support and dependencies.
If the other can now support or brace it, an appropriate residual activity may
remain. If not, losing that contributor is more than merely subtracting a
contextual bonus. Completed movement of the beam is not retroactively undone.

Two equally capable people may be able to manage a larger load at comparable
effort, or the same load more easily. Existing feasibility and Difficulty or
context assessment could represent that change; this comparison has not
quantified it. It does not grant a fixed Difficulty reduction per helper,
automatic Scale increase, or predetermined Resonance for every extra body.

## Arithmetic Candidates

Illustrative participant calculations, all relevant to the specified activity
and comparable frame, with shared context neutral. They are not new builds.

| Contributions | Minimum | Mean | Sum |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 80 and 40 | 40 | 60 | 120 |
| 80 and 80 | 80 | 80 | 160 |

At ordinary roll 30, the first row produces respectively 2, 4, and 10 Effects
before opposition. Those numbers demonstrate the size of the design decision;
they do not demonstrate that every candidate fairly models the same fiction.

For the fastball special, a mean is a plausible candidate: it includes both
performances, moderates uneven development, and expands the available activity
without automatically doubling output when the contributors are equal. The
lower result compared with the stronger solo Expression is not by itself a
defect: the solo and compound activities have different capabilities.

For the shared lift, a mean alone is incomplete as a representation of added
physical support. A sum of complete Expressions is not proven to measure that
support either, because those Expressions also include learned execution and
other multipliers. A universal minimum likewise does not capture the added
means. The physical aggregation question must not disappear behind a choice
of arithmetic operator.

## Cross-Layer Mixing Check

Synthetic legal-rank controls, not complete character allocations:

```text
Character A: Attribute 10 x Derived 2 x Task 1 x Field 1 x Specialty 1 x Precision 1 = 20
Character B: Attribute 1 x Derived 1 x Task 2 x Field 2 x Specialty 2 x Precision 2 = 16

Mean of intact calculations: 18
Multiply the mean of each corresponding layer: 41.765625
Take the best value in every layer: 320
```

Mixing corresponding layers is not equivalent to averaging complete
contributions. Neither picking the best parts nor averaging them should be
silently treated as an already-adopted application of the two-Axis rule.

These figures do not prove that one character can never supply physical means
while another contributes expertise. Such a relationship can be real. They
show why a formula must identify how that expertise actually controls the
shared activity rather than automatically constructing a composite character.
The current comparison does not adopt intact-calculation averaging as a
universal alternative either.

## Recommendation and Next Decision

Retain the existing compound architecture as the starting point for both
examples. Treat averaging complete participant contributions as a candidate
for coordinated execution, explicitly separate from the established two-Axis
modifier mean. Do not apply both calculations to the same contribution merely
to create another multiplier stage.

The substantive decision is whether cross-character resolution should
distinguish the means/capability supplied by the participants from the quality
of their combined execution. The shared lift makes that distinction necessary
to examine; otherwise one number is being asked to measure two different
things. Existing Access, Scale, Difficulty, and contextual tools may suffice
without a new team subsystem, but no specific mapping is adopted here.

Recommended direction: establish actual combined means first, then resolve
remaining uncertainty through the compound Expression. Compare the mean as
an execution candidate rather than treating it as a measure of how much force
the team possesses. No universal lifting weights or headcount conversion are
needed to discuss that architecture.
