## Question

What does a supplied area allow a distinct harm contribution to do? Compare
distributing one finite harm budget with applying one established strength
throughout a permitted area. Use the same procedure for one Source or multiple
Sources once the actual area and harm contributions are determined.

This is not a new delivery roll. It is an unresolved output-application decision.
Current conservation and Multiple-Source doctrine explicitly do not grant
automatic copies of full harm to every occupant. Adopting per-occupant strength
would require an explicit area-application authorization and a clarification
of conservation at this scope, not a claim that the rule already says so.

## Fixture

Two contributors coordinate a burst of freezing mist. One supplies area and
one supplies cold harm. Each has the necessary permission and commits an action;
each resolves their full contribution against the same complete declared
challenge. The resulting Final Ratings are 78 for this fixture. Rolls 40 and
60 generate four area Effects and two harm Effects, respectively.

The preceding radius candidate assigns the four area Effects to an ordinary
footprint plus three area expenditures, approximately 16 units of covered area.
Three occupants are genuinely exposed. Geometry is not used to decide value.

Their feasible personal defenses generate zero, one, and two Effects. A
numerical illustration is Final Rating 40 with rolls 50, 35, and 25. Ordinary
equipment contributions are already included in those Expressions. No extra
equipment multiplier or invented armor value is applied afterward.

The area-strength model produces three total remaining harm Effects here:
two to A, one to B, none to C. It increases total potential consequences, not
merely the wording. No equality between total character harm and the original
two-Effect strength is claimed.

## Occupancy And Scope Checks

With no defense, one, three, or ten exposed occupants face maximum totals of
two, six, or twenty harm Effects under area strength. A finite two-Effect budget
remains at two regardless of occupancy. This is a meaningful increase in crowd
effectiveness, not proof that the existing area-growth costs fully balance it.

For a common hazard such as freezing mist, fixed allocation makes exposure
depend on how the attacker divides a budget among occupants. Area strength
keeps the hazard's severity consistent while personal defenses determine
individual consequences. The growing number of exposures is the benefit of
the permitted area; it is not additional intensity against any one occupant.

Under the recommended candidate:

- Personal defense or protection changes only the exposure it can actually
  address. Changing one occupant's defense does not alter another's result.
- An earlier, feasible intervention against the whole harm effect can reduce
  its strength before personal exposures. Reducing strength from two to one
  changes the displayed outcomes to one, zero, zero.
- By contrast, distinct one-Effect local mitigation on A changes the outcomes
  to one, one, zero. This is a scope fixture, not a new mitigation grant.
- Resolving occupants in another bookkeeping order does not change outcomes.
- No exposure means no application. An ally inside a nonselective hazard is
  not automatically excluded. Actual permissions and established means govern.
- No successful harm contribution means no harm from that contribution. A
  missing area contribution follows actual dependencies and viable residuals;
  it does not arbitrarily select a replacement target after the roll.
- No additional targeting or delivery roll is inserted. Existing feasible
  opposition, mitigation, and counter eligibility remain applicable.
- The candidate covers one resolved application. It grants no automatic
  repeated harm, duration, extra actions, or repeating protection reset.

## Recommendation And Decision

Recommend that a permitted area effect carry its resolved strength throughout
its established footprint, with each genuinely exposed target resolving
applicable opposition and protection. Area Effects establish or expand scope;
harm Effects establish severity, without being added to area output as well.

This should be one general area-application rule, not a special benefit for
teamwork. It does not turn a single finite projectile into many projectiles or
grant area permission merely because a character requests several targets.

The real decision is whether to authorize this multi-target application and
clarify conservation accordingly. Finite-budget distribution remains the
comparison alternative. Do not adopt either by recording this test.
