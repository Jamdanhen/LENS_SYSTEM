# LENS Assets Two-Point Cross-Character Comparison

Date: 2026-09-05
Status: Conceptual comparison updated for the approved one-point package-equivalent reference.

## Question and Controls

The user approved the shopkeeper's counter trial and asked whether its two-point
value holds up against starting possessions for other Characters.

Sources: the approved Assets whole-provision references and the shopkeeper's
two-point package test. Use one grounded fantasy Profile and the same guaranteed
base equipment, including one professional kit. Hold Assets Rank at 2 for the
comparisons; this is not an increase to the saved guide or clinician, who each
have Assets Rank 1. No original Character file changes.

No dice are needed: the test compares starting facts and useful opportunities,
not uncertain execution. It cannot establish currency equivalence or long-term
economic balance without additional Profile assumptions.

## Equal-Cost Selections

| Example Character | Two-point selection | Actual added capability |
|---|---|---|
| Local trader | Approved trial counter: established market space, small secure storage, modest sewing-notions stock, and ordinary working resources | Conduct a modest local trade using controlled goods and an established workplace |
| Traveling guide | Accepted ordinary riding horse and tack | Mounted travel where the animal and terrain permit |
| Expedition supplier | Accepted pack animal and load gear | Carry additional expedition goods; those goods must still actually exist |
| Equipped traveler | Two ordinary starting-package equivalents, beyond the guaranteed package, at one point each | Substantial equipment reserves, Profile-defined alternatives, or equipment for two existing companions; no companions themselves are supplied |
| Local healer | NEW comparison candidate: established use of one treatment room, one couch, secure storage, and a modest initial reserve of ordinary dressings and cleaning supplies | Receive patients in a usable fixed space beyond the portable professional kit |

The healer's room is not an approved provision or a hospital. It supplies no
staff, rare remedies, inpatient ward, training, extraordinary healing permission,
or replenishing supplies. Its proposed two-point placement tests whether a
modest workplace has application beyond retail; it is not priced from a modern
clinic or a claim about real treatment costs.

These are selections, not occupation entitlements. Another Character may buy
the same available package on the same terms. Character expertise affects its
use, not its construction price. Owning a counter does not automatically confer
retail expertise, nor does owning a treatment room confer medical expertise.

## Situation Tests

### 1. A Week of Local Paid Work

The trader can use the counter and merchandise to conduct trade. The healer can
use the room for appropriate work. The guide may undertake paid mounted courier
work; the pack animal can support paid haulage when those opportunities exist.
An armed traveler may undertake suitable protection work.

Finding: earning opportunities are not unique to the counter. All require
appropriate competence, available work, and actual participation. None grants
guaranteed wages or automatic net profit. Ordinary established work need not
be repeatedly rolled merely because its proceeds matter.

### 2. The Party Must Depart Immediately

The riding horse, pack animal, and purchased equipment can accompany the party
when accessible and transport is available; two packages do not grant carrying
capacity merely because they were purchased. The trader can take controlled portable goods but
cannot take the market site. The healer can take portable supplies but not the
room. Do not invent extra restrictions on moving goods to protect the prices.

Finding: portable provisions have a clear advantage. The workplaces retain
value on return but do not follow the Character as universal bonuses.

### 3. Supplies Must Be Replaced

The trader buys replacement stock with actual business resources; the healer
replaces used dressings through an actual acquisition route. Animals need care
and weapons may need maintenance. No costs or maintenance schedule are assigned
here, and these different needs are not asserted to be numerically equal.

Finding: the counter's working resources must remain part of the finite stated
package. Reading 'working resources' as automatic restoration after any loss
would add a benefit the other selections do not receive. The approved description
does not grant that restoration.

### 4. Sell Everything and Change Direction

All owners may seek to sell their actual transferable property. The counter
contains stock and funds; the mounts and weapons are also saleable property.
Premises-use rights are transferable only if the established arrangement allows.
Selling property relinquishes it; no rule prevents a sensible exchange merely
because it changes the initial selection.

Finding: no obvious duplication of assets occurs, but financial equivalence
remains untested. If liquidating the counter routinely bought a mount plus
substantial extras while the reverse exchange could not, the same two-point
price would need review in that Profile. No exchange rate is invented to make
the present test pass.

### 5. Compare Against the Guaranteed Package

The counter, room, and mounts add functions absent from the portable professional
kit and ordinary adventuring equipment. The original comparison found two spare
weapons a weak alternative because everyone already received a weapon and an
auxiliary choice. The user subsequently approved replacing each one-point weapon
with one complete ordinary package equivalent.

Current comparison: two additional equipment packages can outfit two existing
people or provide substantial ordinary reserves and Profile-defined alternatives.
They do not create allies, a mount, or business premises. This is a stronger
material alternative than two spare weapons, although exact financial parity
remains uncalibrated. The original weakness is retained here as the reason for
the revision, not as an unresolved choice about the current one-point reference.

## Results

- Cross-character application passes: the package follows its actual resources,
  not an occupational label. The two-point counter is not a shopkeeper-only perk.
- Counter versus mount is plausible for continued testing: each supplies a
  substantial, distinct ordinary opportunity; neither includes the other.
- A modest healer's room is a plausible further candidate, not a newly accepted
  entitlement or a replacement for the five-point workshop.
- Campaign emphasis changes usefulness. A journey favors mobile assets; local
  play gives fixed workplaces more opportunity. No universal equal-utility claim.
- Liquidation values and net earning capacity remain unresolved. The comparisons
  do not prove exact financial parity or validate cost as acquisition Difficulty.
- The original two-spare-weapons comparison prompted the approved revision to
  two ordinary package equivalents. Neither the counter nor the Character's
  allocation was enlarged to address that weakness.

## Recommendation and Next Decision

Use the approved counter for the shopkeeper trial without a budget change.
The whole-provision model works across these Characters, but numerical equality
is only partly supported. Do not equate a conceptual pass with calibrated prices.

The lower equipment reference is now settled provisionally: one complete ordinary
starting-package equivalent per point replaces one ordinary extra weapon. The
working-animal-and-cart, pet, and broader companion candidates in the subsequent
equivalency test remain unadopted. Compare the cart package against the existing
pack-animal provision next; do not reopen Background, attach an automatic income
track, or price limitations.
