# Standalone capabilities, rated 1-20

Date: 2026-09-15

Status: user-authorized experiment, not adopted rules. The user proposed
removing the purchased parent Attributes and allowing their Derived Attributes
to reach 20. This comparison tests that architecture, separately from the prior
proposal to make Leverage components into supporting multipliers.

The floor of 1 is a test assumption preserving the existing minimum ordinary
foundation. The user's proposed maximum is 20. No capability renaming, starting
grant, price, conversion, new grouping, or sixth Attribute is adopted here.
Only this report and its executable comparison were created.

## Assessment

The proposal is mathematically exact for existing Expressions when each new
capability is assigned its old Attribute + Derived total. It removes a rating
layer and its parent-cap dependencies without reducing the ordinary numerical
ceiling or changing Difficulty. It can preserve Leverage's actual functions as
Assets, Network, Authority, Reputation, and Background; it need not turn those
functions into bonuses to personal persuasion.

It is not economically equivalent or identical in possible character builds.
Parents currently provide shared minimum capability, bundled improvement, and
development ceilings. Removing them removes those relationships. This is the
substantive design choice, not a failure of the Expression formula.

Recommendation: retain standalone 1-20 capability as a serious working candidate.
Use exact matched ratings first, then explicitly evaluate independent allocation
and advancement. Do not preserve invisible parents solely to reconstruct every
old development constraint. Conversely, do not describe an unchanged output
ceiling as proof that new builds and purchase choices are already balanced.

## What is being compared

Current foundation:

```text
Attribute A: 1-10
Derived D: 0-A
Foundation = A + D
```

Candidate foundation:

```text
Capability C: 1-20
For a matched comparison, C = old A + old D
Expression = C x applicable Task x Field x Specialty x Precision
             x the other established applicable factors
```

These are direct base ratings, not ranks converted to x(1 + C/10). Capability
20 does not mean a x3 multiplier. Training and Axis development remain 0-10
with their existing factors. No permission, Scale increase, or automatic new
manifestation follows from a high capability score.

For example, Might 8 + Strength 6 becomes Strength 14. All four other former
Might capabilities also absorb their own Might contribution, not merely their
previous Derived ranks. A former unranked Toughness under Might 8 becomes an
effective Toughness 8 in this matched comparison, not 0 or 1.

This is a mathematical mapping, not authorization to rewrite character records,
refund purchases, or convert historic grants from other rule versions.

## Expression results

All 65 legal current A/D pairs map to the 1-20 range; every integer in that
range is represented by at least one legal pair. Holding the other factors
constant makes the resulting Expression exactly equal before and after flooring.

The executable checks 58,500 comparisons: 65 pairs, five Training profiles,
three Axis ranks, ten Difficulty bands, and six modifier controls. Controls
include neutral, help, interference, reduced Function, x1.6, and numeric zero.
Exact rational arithmetic is used, not floating-point tolerance.

Four fixed character fixtures also compare Effects across all 100 initial
percentile results, using the same fixed continuation on natural 01. This is
400 resolution comparisons, not simulated encounters or a fairness study.
Equal Final Ratings imply equal downstream resolution under identical rolls,
opposition, and permissions. Keeping those rules is an explicit test condition.

| Matched maximum | Current | Candidate |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Ordinary foundation | 10 + 10 = 20 | 20 |
| Four applicable Training factors at x2 | 320 | 320 |
| Same route with an applicable Axis 10 at x2 | 640 | 640 |

The latter values are controls, not universal Final Rating caps. Character-
aligned enhancement remains a separate unadopted candidate; it is not added to
make the standalone proposal pass.

Illustrative direct-rating progression with all four Training ranks at 5:

| Capability | Raw Expression | Final at D3 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 5 | 25.3125 | 8 |
| 10 | 50.625 | 17 |
| 15 | 75.9375 | 26 |
| 20 | 101.25 | 35 |

These are the same outputs as current foundations of 5, 10, 15, and 20.
Removing parents does not inherently raise success at any fixed foundation.

## What changes about character concepts

A current group of five foundations can be represented by a shared parent A
only if every foundation lies between A and 2A, with A at most 10. Equivalently:

```text
ceil(highest foundation / 2) <= min(lowest foundation, 10)
```

Consequently, current Strength foundation 20 requires Might 10 and Strength
Derived 10. All its siblings then have foundations of at least 10 even with no
Derived investment. Standalone Strength 20 does not impose that sibling floor.

Narrative probes:

- **A powerful but easily exhausted character:** Strength can be much higher
  than Vigor without buying broad Might first. Established physiology, powers,
  and permissions still constrain what is actually possible; differing ratings
  need not be forbidden merely because they are no longer correlated by a parent.
- **An insightful but easily frightened scholar:** Insight can exceed Resolve
  by more than the former shared Psyche structure allowed. Knowledge still uses
  relevant Training; high Insight does not automatically create it.
- **An awkward wealthy patron:** Assets can be developed independently of
  Network, Authority, Reputation, and Background, while remaining a direct source
  of material capability. The patron need not pass an invented personal
  Manipulation check simply to exercise uncontested buying power.
- **A coordinated but slow craftsperson:** Coordination can be high without
  a high Movement foundation. This does not make Coordination a universal
  substitute for relevant expertise or every physical action.

The first three concepts already existed to degrees within the current game.
The new property is greater numerical independence, not permission to imagine
them for the first time. Narrative eligibility still applies without inventing
new numerical floors or hidden parent requirements for this test.

## Creation: match ratings before changing budgets

There are currently five Derived capabilities per parent. Before the ordinary
flexible allowance and Profile provisions, the protected construction reference
therefore supplies:

```text
Sum of 25 capability foundations
= 5 x sum of five Attributes + sum of 25 Derived ranks
= 5 x 25 + 50
= 175
```

The equivalent average is 7, not a proposed mandatory starting score for each
capability. Two checked legal current builds both map to 175: a balanced A5/D2
reference and a concentrated [8,6,5,4,2] parent allocation with legal Derived
investment. Every mapped capability is retained exactly.

Freely allocating 175 to standalone scores is a different construction rule
from mapping an existing legal build. It could produce Strength 20 and four
former siblings at 6, which the shared parent cannot represent. At the extreme,
seven scores at 20, one at 18, and seventeen at 1 also sum to 175. No eligibility
or balance verdict is implied by that arithmetic. It exposes concentration
choices a future construction procedure must deliberately accept or constrain.

Equal total foundation points do not guarantee equal practical power: how often
capabilities apply and how Training is concentrated still matter. The existing
system also permits concentration, but its parent relations impose a different
shape on it. Neither all-equal allocation nor forced broad distribution is
introduced here.

## Advancement and prices

Parents have a numerical job beyond organizing a sheet. Where eligible, one
Attribute rank costs 60 and adds one to all five child foundations. One Derived
rank costs 20 and adds one to one foundation. Their direct per-foundation prices
are therefore 12 for the bundle and 20 for focused improvement, before valuing
the parent's additional ceiling benefit.

Example: A5 with five D2 scores gives five foundations of 7. Buying A6 for 60
produces five foundations of 8. Buying just one eligible D3 for 20 produces one
8 and four 7s. With independent ratings there is no such automatic bundle.

No single flat standalone price preserves both existing marginal prices. The
following are analytical controls, not recommended or adopted price changes:

| Price per standalone +1 | Raise one capability | Raise five by one | Reproduce 175 from twenty-five supplied 1s |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 12 | 12 | 60 | 1,800 |
| 15 | 15 | 75 | 2,250 |
| 20 | 20 | 100 | 3,000 |

Current protected Attribute and Derived funding totals 2,200. The remaining
750 Training and 125 ordinary flexible allocations bring the current priced
reference to 3,075; they are not reassigned here.

If preserving that 2,200 foundation budget with the trial floor of 1, a price
of 20 permits a total of only 135 foundation points rather than 175. A price
of 15 permits 171 with 10 points remaining. Exactly matching 175 by one flat
price would require 2,200 / 150 = 14 2/3 per improvement. That awkward number
is a diagnostic, not a pricing recommendation. Giving 175 directly while using
20 for advancement instead deliberately changes the creation/development price
relationship; it is not a neutral accounting conversion.

At a flat 20, five independent improvements cost 100 rather than the old 60
parent purchase. A focused improvement remains 20 where the old Derived increase
was already eligible; it may avoid an old parent prerequisite where it was not.
A flat 15 makes each focused improvement cheaper and broad improvement cost 75.
These are different advancement incentives, not changes to a fixed Expression's
Difficulty curve. Prices and awards must be judged together before adoption.

Current HOLT Access and each Axis rank cost 20 under the September 15 revision;
other prices and Participation 10 with discretionary 0/3/5 remain unchanged.
No standalone price is inferred from that common HOLT price, and no automatic
refund or extra advancement award follows from removing a parent in this test.

## Scope, Capacity, and HOLTs without purchased parents

Removing a numerical parent need not remove the meaning that parent organized.
Resistance can still explicitly mean bodily resistance; Resolve can still
describe maintaining internal direction. They do not become interchangeable
because both oppose something. Conversely, explore a manifestation's actual
functions before using a label to reject it. This comparison preserves existing
capability scope without making the headings the only possible future taxonomy.

Direct scope and Capacity associations can preserve current behavior:

- The ten formerly Might/Mobility capabilities associate with Physical Capacity.
- The ten formerly Psyche/Influence capabilities associate with Mental Capacity.
- Assets, Network, Authority, Reputation, and Background associate with Framework.

No new condition ledger, health track, or per-capability Capacity is needed to
retain that mapping. Existing relevant Function and Access handling can stay
the same. The previous optional-support impairment experiment is not imported
into this direct-capability model.

Likewise, an existing HOLT Axis can remain related to the same named capability.
Axis ranks already lack a Derived-rank cap; no new cap or rank-20 Axis is implied.
The former Derived rank is now represented by a different base-rating convention,
not secretly retained as a second score for pricing or permission.

Movement was checked for a possible hidden dependency. The current six/twelve-
unit ground model does not multiply its ordinary allowance by Movement Rank;
pushes use an Expression that maps normally. The earlier 10-20-meter
Movement-rank table is explicitly superseded and is not a current conversion
objection. This targeted check is not a claim that every external Profile or
historical character package has been audited for parent-rank assumptions.

## The five-versus-six grouping question

Standalone ratings remove the requirement that each display group own a
purchased parent, five children, a shared ceiling, and a particular budget.
Five, six, or another number of teaching/display sections could organize the
same capabilities without altering their arithmetic. Grouping does not have
to be a new mechanical purchase.

This does not automatically discover a missing sixth function or solve the
visual imbalance of ten physical, ten mental, and five worldly capabilities.
That preference remains real. The present evidence supports separating the
organization decision from the numerical-parent decision, not dismissing it.

Character Aspects could eventually be a distinct character-sheet section, but
they are still an enhancement experiment, not automatically five new foundational
capabilities. Calling them a sixth Attribute family just to match the layout
would mix mechanical roles that this test has deliberately kept separate.

## Recommendation and next decision

The case for standalone ratings is stronger than the case for forcing all
Leverage into support multipliers: it simplifies every capability consistently,
preserves direct resource and institutional action, and exactly preserves
matched Expression output.

The meaningful choice is whether independent capability development is desired,
including the removal of automatic sibling improvement and inherited minimums.
If yes, pricing and creation can be calibrated to that choice rather than
recreating the parents under another name. If linked broad development is
important, the current parent model has a concrete advantage worth retaining
or deliberately replacing. Neither preference can be settled by more equality
assertions.

For the next playtest comparison, preserve matched capability totals first;
compare independently allocated builds separately. Treat 15 and 20 as clean
price controls rather than secretly changing the existing economy. Any actual
new construction, optional broad-development purchase, final grouping, and
terminology require their own substantive decision.
