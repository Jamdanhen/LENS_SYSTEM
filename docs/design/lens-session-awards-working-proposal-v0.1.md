# Session Awards Working Proposal

Date: 2026-09-06; updated 2026-09-13.
Status: User-approved working session-award card, revised to Participation 10
with discretionary 0/3/5 retained in the three-package core adoption.
This supersedes the earlier proportional-conversion card. Recipient controls
remain the working model. Optional milestone awards are approved at the recommended
12.5 points; absent-player handling and milestone recipient details remain open.

## Current Advancement Decision

On 2026-09-07 the user adopted common flat creation/advancement prices:
Attribute 60, HOLT acquisition 40, Derived Attribute 20, Axis 15,
Task/Field 10, Specialty/Precision 5. HOLT acquisition is a one-time purchase;
the other amounts are per purchased Rank. The 2026-09-13 core adoption removes
parent HOLT ranks and uses Attribute plus Derived as the foundation. This uses the branch's
times-five denomination. Task's relative reduction is deliberate, not just
currency conversion. The baseline contains the current working table.
Progressive pricing and earlier numerical examples below remain historical.

The current card's three-meaningful reference is 19 points per session; the
earlier 14 and 12.5 remain historical comparison controls. A table awarding
25 chooses faster advancement and a table awarding 2.5 chooses slower
advancement. Those are valid customizations, not
failures requiring additional constraints or compensation. The reference is
not a hard ceiling, mandatory award, or a guarantee under every award schedule.

Following the separate flat-price decision, the user approved the numerical
session-award card, then separately approved the optional milestone recommendation
below. Milestones are not required for ordinary advancement.

## Adopted Working Award Card

| Category | Points |
|---|---:|
| Participation | 10 |
| Progress | 0 / 3 / 5 |
| Discovery | 0 / 3 / 5 |
| Character Expression | 0 / 3 / 5 |
| Narrative Engagement | 0 / 3 / 5 |

For each discretionary category, use 0 when not demonstrated, 3 for a meaningful
contribution, or 5 for an exceptional contribution. Exceptional replaces 3;
it does not add to it. Assess each category independently with a concrete reason.
Quiet and outspoken contributions use the same substantive standard, not an
airtime or performance quota. Character Expression and Narrative Engagement
remain separate categories.

Participation plus three meaningful categories gives 19. All four meaningful
categories gives 22. The unmodified card spans 10-30 before milestones. Do not
force awards to total 19; it is a reference, not a quota.
Tables may change award amounts to choose faster or slower advancement.

The branch's earlier card (Participation 10; other categories 0/1/3) was not
adopted. The September 7 card was 5 and 0/3/5. The September 13 decision raises
only Participation to 10, retaining the other categories and recipients.
It raises the reliable floor and accelerates development for savers as well
as players who spend on immediate help; it is not a reimbursement rule.

The current supporting comparison is
`../tests/lens-participation-ten-mixed-development-test-v0.1.md`; the core
adoption is `lens-core-three-package-adoption-v0.1.md`. The older comparison,
`../tests/lens-session-awards-and-story-benefits-test-v0.1.md`, used a constructed
six-session sequence that awarded 90 rather than 82.5 under its preceding card;
that 9.09% increase is specific to the sequence, not a universal rate.

### Current Pacing And Shared Spending

With both engine packages adopted, save 19 per session to afford an eligible
Specialty 0-to-10 (50) in three sessions, an Attribute rank (60) in four, or
new HOLT Access plus Axis 10 (190) in ten. Spending four per session leaves 15;
those purchases take four, four, and thirteen sessions. These are exclusive
saving targets from zero, not deadlines, permissions, or activation rules.
Retain remainders; no milestones or story grants are assumed.

Boost, paid reroll, and defined narrative help retain their one-point prices
and current limits. They share the spendable development currency without
automatic reimbursement or required usage. Narrative eligibility governs when
advancement takes effect; a session break mid-scene does not itself justify it.

### Additional Story Awards

At the session summary, identify development already gained through play
alongside the points available to spend. For example, dependable support
valued provisionally at one point is one point's worth of development already
received, not a debt or an additional unspent point. It is not automatically
deducted from regular session awards. The value concerns actual support, not
the number of named people in a Network.

Optional earmarked training credit can be supplemented by ordinary points to
complete eligible development. It is not a separate currency or a fractional
Rank. A narratively earned full Assets Rank likewise records development
already delivered; no treasure-to-Rank table is implied.

Ordinary language and defined minor recurring support have their specific
one-point working provisions in the baseline. Other equipment, support, and
recovery prices remain unresolved.
No obligation to monetize every friendship, no automatic duplicate award, and
no one-point universal purchasing rule follows. The actual immediate-spending
benefits retain their current rules rather than acquiring new prices here. Story benefits need not be
received every session, and their quantity is not an award quota.

The working recipient model gives Participation to participating players,
Progress and Discovery to the participating group, and Character Expression
and Narrative Engagement individually. Multiple players may qualify; these are
not single-winner prizes. The established definitions and controls below apply.

Earlier proposal labels below preserve the development history. This current
card supersedes their statements that numerical session amounts are unapproved.

## Retained Categories

| Category | Recognition |
|---|---|
| Participation | A dependable baseline for taking part, not a performance grade. |
| Progress | Meaningfully advancing an objective, resolving a problem, or overcoming opposition. Partial progress and non-mission objectives count. |
| Discovery | Consequential learning that changes understanding or available choices. |
| Character Expression | Bringing the particular character's identity, commitments, motives, relationships, or contradictions into play. Plot advancement is not required. |
| Narrative Engagement | Helping develop the shared story through its threads, relationships, choices, and consequences, including another character's story. |

Progress asks what objective or situation advanced. Narrative Engagement asks
how someone helped develop the shared story; completion alone does not earn both.
Each discretionary award needs a concrete reason. One scene may support distinct
categories, but descriptions of the same contribution do not establish separate
reasons automatically. Not every session must demonstrate every category.

Character Expression and Narrative Engagement remain separate. Neither grades
acting ability, airtime, moral correctness, or adherence to a GM's planned plot.
System stewardship/table contribution is only a discussion candidate; it is not
a sixth adopted category. Campaign Emphasis also remains optional and unresolved.

## Constructed-Session Findings

- A straightforward escort job can support Participation and Progress without
  automatically supplying discovery, character expression, or narrative engagement.
- A failed rescue can still support consequential discovery, concrete partial
  progress, a commitment-driven choice, and development of shared story tensions.
- A quiet interlude can advance a personal relationship and develop another
  character's story without combat or a formal mission.
- A finale can receive ordinary session awards and, where the campaign uses
  them, a milestone award for the larger undertaking across sessions.

These are conceptual examples, not live player tests or approved point amounts.
The user agreed to retaining the five categories after their review.

## Milestones

Status: User-approved optional working recommendation, 2026-09-06.

When a campaign uses milestones, recommend 12.5 additional advancement points for
a substantial turning point or completed undertaking spanning multiple sessions.
This recognizes the larger development alongside the normal session award, not
a second rating of the final session's individual contributions.

A milestone need not involve a predetermined chapter, victory, or following the
GM's intended plot. Campaigns may omit milestone awards entirely. Their use,
frequency, and award amount remain adjustable by the table; 12.5 is the recommended
amount, not a universal ceiling. No new currency, character level, or Rank unlock
is created. The points enter the existing shared advancement pool.

Milestone recipient details, including absence or joining partway through an
undertaking, remain open; this decision does not establish attendance rules.

## Next Proposal: Recipients

Historical development below uses the former denomination and original candidate
statuses. It does not override the current card, recipient controls, or milestone
amounts above. Do not mix its unconverted numerical examples with current prices.

Proposed, not adopted: Participation goes to each participating player; Progress
and Discovery are shared among session participants; Character Expression and
Narrative Engagement are individual awards based on demonstrated contribution.
Multiple players may qualify for an individual category. These are not competitive
single-winner awards. Shared rewards are not assigned exclusively to the final
die roller, spokesperson, or character who supplied the last action.

Do not infer numerical amounts or settle absent-player handling from this proposal.
Confirm recipient treatment before testing point values and advancement pacing.

## Subsequent Discussion: Recipient and Tier Controls

Following the recipient proposal, the user emphasized that the assessment must
accommodate quiet and outspoken players, then instructed continuation. Use the
recipient split as the working test control, not a final award rule. Meaningful
and exceptional concern the contribution in context, not volume, acting skill,
frequency of speech, or a personality-specific performance quota. Multiple
players can qualify. A lack of opportunity must not be diagnosed as poor engagement.

## First Numerical Award Candidate

Proposed only:

```text
Participation: 10

Each of Progress, Discovery, Character Expression, Narrative Engagement:
Not demonstrated:         0
Meaningfully demonstrated: 5
Exceptionally demonstrated: 10
```

Use one band per category for the session; exceptional replaces meaningful
rather than adding to it. Repeated minor examples do not automatically justify
exceptional. Participation is not tiered. This yields 10-50 per participating
player before any optional milestone. No milestone price or extra category
is set here. These numbers are a new candidate, not user-approved awards.

## Constructed Session Totals

Each row shows one player's award where the specified individual contributions
occur; it does not give everyone the individual awards by default.

| Session | Participation | Progress | Discovery | Character Expression | Narrative Engagement | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Tactical escort | 10 | 5 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 15 |
| Failed rescue | 10 | 0 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 25 |
| Quiet relationship session | 10 | 5 | 0 | 5 | 5 | 25 |
| Chapter finale | 10 | 10 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 35 |

The escort achieves its objective but supplies no other specified triggers.
The rescue fails to secure a concrete advance toward rescue in this variant,
but reveals the organizer; a commitment-driven choice and engagement with the
informant's competing loyalties supply the two individual reasons. The quiet
session includes repairing a relationship and developing another character's
story. The finale ends a major threat, uncovers consequential information, and
includes distinct individual contributions. A milestone remains possible but
is not included in 35.

Style invariance check: replace a meaningful spoken portrayal with a concise
statement of the same consequential character choice. With the remaining
events unchanged, the award stays 25. More elaborate delivery alone does not
change the band. This demonstrates application of the proposed criterion;
it does not empirically prove different GMs will judge it consistently.

## Pacing Check and Denomination Control

Using the earlier guide portfolios and saving from zero:

| Repeated session award | Progressive Attribute portfolio (84) | Progressive mixed portfolio (98) | Progressive narrow portfolio (94) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 15 | 6 sessions | 7 | 7 |
| 25 | 4 sessions | 4 | 4 |
| 35 | 3 sessions | 3 | 3 |

At original flat prices, those portfolios cost 12, 14, and 12: all are affordable
after one of these sessions. This is not evidence that narrative awards require
progressive costs. As an explicit denomination sensitivity, multiplying every
flat price by seven gives portfolio costs 84, 98, and 84 while preserving all
flat-price ratios. At awards of 25 those also each take four sessions. Seven is
chosen to match the guide's one-Attribute purchase, not a new proposed law or a
global equivalence between flat and progressive development.

JavaScript summed bands and calculated funding dates. Session frequencies,
typical-player averages, exceptional-award frequency, and GM agreement are not
established by these constructed cases. Individual award differences may create
long-term advancement gaps; no compensating rule is invented in this test.

Recommendation: consider 10 baseline and 0/5/10 category bands as the first
concrete award card. Keep its adoption separate from advancement-cost selection.
