# Area Persistence And Exposure Comparison v0.1

Date: 2026-09-12

Status: user-authorized scenario comparison complete. The user subsequently
explicitly approved meaningful exposure scope and further applications for
continuing or renewed danger beyond that scope on 2026-09-12. The baseline and
terminology now record that rule. The comparison below retains its decision
context; no universal duration, damage interval, or maintenance cost is adopted.
This is a rule-behavior comparison, not a numerical encounter balance test.

## Existing Authority

- Resolution Scope and Repeated Attempts resolves an actual activity, not
  each sentence, turn boundary, or restatement. A further meaningful activity
  can form another Expression under normal accounting.
- Permitted Area Application applies resolved strength to each genuinely
  exposed target, subject to actual opposition, protection, and scope.
- Personal protection does not cancel other targets' exposures. An actual
  intervention against the whole effect follows its wider scope.
- Conditions already inflicted are distinct from their continuing cause.
  Stabilization can stop further generation without removing existing Load.
- Actual dependencies determine whether an effect requires maintenance or
  continues independently. Ending its Source does not retroactively erase
  independent consequences.

Area application by itself does not yet authorize repeated harm. This comparison
addresses that open temporal scope; it does not reopen the per-target rule.

## Three Interpretations

1. Once per target: an occupant faces the hazard only once, regardless of how
   long they remain. This avoids repeated accounting but can create immunity
   to continued exposure that the fiction does not support.
2. Every round: apply again whenever a round advances. This is easy for a
   hazard explicitly defined to act each round, but as a universal rule it
   changes harm when the same activity is narrated in different time slices.
3. Meaningful exposure scope: establish the hazardous activity or stretch of
   exposure being resolved before rolling. Further application requires actual
   continuing or renewed danger outside that scope. Neither elapsed rounds nor
   a previous success alone determines the answer.

Recommendation: the third interpretation as the general procedure. A particular
hazard may still have a short, explicit exposure interval where its behavior
supports one. Such an interval is not a universal area duration.

## Scenario Comparison

| Case | Once per target | Every round | Meaningful exposure scope |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Brief cold burst strikes a room | One application fits | Requires a separate check that no hazard remains | Resolve those exposed to the burst; its ending prevents later exposure to that burst |
| A character enters after the burst ends | A new target could be incorrectly charged if persistence is assumed | Could incorrectly extend the original roll into later rounds | No remaining effect means no application; an actual residual hazard would be a separate established fact |
| One crossing of a lingering cold mist is narrated in several segments | One application fits this case | May charge repeatedly merely for narration or turn slicing | The agreed crossing remains one resolution unless circumstances exceed or invalidate its scope |
| After crossing, the character stays to operate a valve in the mist | Previously affected status can grant false immunity | Charges by round whether or not that matches the exposure | Staying to work establishes further hazardous exposure beyond the resolved crossing |
| Another character enters the mist while it remains dangerous | First exposure allowed | First exposure allowed | Resolve that entrant's actual exposure using the extant hazard and their own protection |
| A character exits and genuinely re-enters for another rescue | Literal once-ever handling blocks renewed consequences | Depends primarily on clock position | A distinct return through the danger can be a new exposure; movement across a map boundary alone is not an automatic reset |
| A character remains inside but is no longer exposed because of effective protection | Can ignore protection scope | Can charge merely for occupancy | Actual exposure and protection govern; occupancy alone is insufficient |
| The mist disperses after injuring someone | Often leaves cause/consequence distinction unstated | Stops new ticks but may imply Load ends with the field | No further mist exposure; existing Conditions remain until addressed normally |

The crossing example does not impose one roll per crossing or one roll per
activity as a universal formula. It illustrates one agreed resolution scope.
A lethal, continuously renewed hazard may demand a shorter exposure scope;
a mild one may be meaningfully assessed over a much longer activity.

## Concrete Walkthrough

Assume the means explicitly support lingering dangerous mist, not merely a
brief cold burst whose visual description lasts. Its established harm strength
is two for this illustration. No new strength or time conversion is inferred.

1. A character crosses the mist to reach a valve. Establish before rolling that
   the resolution covers that crossing. One matching defensive Effect against
   the two-Effect application leaves one harm Effect before any distinct
   mitigation.
2. Narrating a pause, a footstep, or the next turn within that same resolved
   crossing does not award another harm application or defensive retry.
3. Having reached the valve, the character chooses to remain and work in the
   dangerous mist. That exposure was not covered by the crossing. Establish
   the next meaningful scope and resolve its actual danger. It is not automatic
   success because the earlier crossing succeeded, nor automatically another
   two harm because a round passed.
4. A different character enters later while the mist remains. That character
   faces their own exposure, not a share of the first character's damage and
   not immunity from the original creation having already been resolved.
5. Effective ventilation disperses the mist. It stops future exposure where
   it actually clears the hazard; it does not remove the earlier injury.

Where a continuing permitted effect has established strength, retain that
strength while its supporting facts remain. Reassess if those facts change.
Do not require its Source to repeat the original creation roll solely because
another target encounters it. An actor deliberately renewing an attack still
performs an actual activity with its normal action cost; calling it persistence
does not grant free repeated attacks.

For other environmental hazards, retain existing active/passive opposition
routing. This recommendation does not introduce a universal hazard Rank or
replace passive Dissonance with an invented attack roll. It also does not make
an earlier generation roll prove indefinite duration or repeatable harm.

## Boundaries To Preserve

- The GM and established fiction set the exposure scope before resolution;
  a player cannot claim that an arbitrarily long stay was already covered.
- Do not subdivide an already-resolved activity afterward to multiply harm,
  or merge genuinely new exposure afterward to avoid it.
- Re-entry is neither automatic additional damage nor an immunity reset.
- A longer or more intense exposure than the resolved scope can require a
  further resolution even if the character's nominal activity is unchanged.
- An existing Condition and a further harmful application are different
  things. Apply normal Condition and Load rules; do not award the old harm again
  merely because the Condition persists.
- A granted recurring hazard may create further consequences without a new
  attack action on every exposure. Whether the means grant that persistence,
  require maintenance, or expire must be established, not supplied by the word
  area. Duration purchasing and Effect-to-duration conversion remain open.
- Preserve feasible opposition, individual protection, actual intervention
  scope, and all explicit per-use or per-round Lens limits.

## Recommendation For Adoption

Resolve each genuinely hazardous exposure at an agreed meaningful scope.
Reapply only when the continuing or renewed danger extends beyond what was
already resolved. Use actual persistence, exposure, and protection; neither
round passage nor mere continued occupancy automatically creates another
application. A later entrant can face a still-existing hazard. Existing
Conditions do not disappear merely because exposure ends.

This answers repeat-application handling without setting a universal duration.
The next distinct question, after this recommendation is decided, is what a
particular manifestation must supply to remain dangerous: independently
persistent means, ongoing maintenance, or another permitted mechanism. Do not
presume that extra area Effects purchase time.
