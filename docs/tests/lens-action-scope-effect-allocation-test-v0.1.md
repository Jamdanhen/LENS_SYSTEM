# Action Scope and Effect Allocation Test v0.1

Status: User-authorized review and test, 2026-09-12. No new allocation permission,
Condition price, area rule, or baseline amendment is adopted by this record.

## Objective

Compare a gunshot, a wave attack, and a creature-filled wave to distinguish
ordinary components of one action from additional mechanical results. Use
existing Effect conservation rather than introducing a mandatory delivery roll.

## Existing Rules That Answer Part of the Question

The baseline sections Effect Resolution and Conservation, Resolution Scope and
Repeated Attempts, Harm as Dissonance, Capacity/Conditions/Load, and Multiple-Source
Output provide these controls:

- A successful Expression generates one baseline Effect Level plus one for
  each complete ten points of positive Margin. Failure supplies no successful
  Effects. Cancellation can remove the baseline as well as additional output.
- More descriptive components within one resolved action do not automatically
  require more rolls or generate more Effects.
- Harm is Dissonance, expressed through named Conditions, not a separate
  default hit-point loss to which every Condition must be added.
- Each Effect Level follows one traceable path. Its form, target, and time
  remain relevant. Full output is not copied across results or targets.
- A matching, genuinely reinforcing contribution can add its earned Effects.
  Distinct functions retain their identity and necessary dependencies.

Consequently, 'damage plus poisoning' needs inspection: Poisoned may name the
harm itself. A second independently imposed result is different from giving
one harm its fictional cause, relevant scope, and recovery requirements.

## Case 1: Gunshot

The declared action shoots the target. Its Expression includes the actual range,
means, and opposition. Ordinary projectile travel is not a second action or an
Effect toll paid before the attack can cause harm.

At Rating 80, roll 79 generates one Effect. If it survives, it supplies the
attack's minimum harm, not a delivery-only success awaiting another damage roll.
One matching cancellation removes it. Arrival alone does not guarantee injury
through protection or opposition.

## Case 2: Wave Attack

The declared wave reaches and strikes the target. Apply the same scope test as
the gunshot. Travel and impact are ordinary components of the attack, not
automatically separate rolled functions merely because they can be named.

The same Rating-80 / roll-79 fixture produces one Effect. The wave is not granted
one impact Effect plus a second independent allocation solely for delivering
itself. Carrying a separate character's contribution may be part of the declared
combined undertaking; it does not manufacture a mandatory extra delivery roll.

Independent displacement, restraint, or a persistent obstruction would need to
be identified as additional mechanical results if claimed. This test does not
declare that every impact automatically purchases all of those results.

## Case 3: Creature-Filled Wave

Two actors commit their actual actions. In the fixed-roll fixture:

- The wave actor generates four Effects.
- The creature actor generates three Effects.

If both genuinely reinforce one declared harm application, they can supply
seven compatible Effects under the existing shared-output rule. No eighth
Effect is awarded for delivery. Matching forms and actual protection still
control mitigation; calling the result shared does not bypass them.

Piranhas, jellyfish, and electric eels may all appear in the narration where
permitted. Species count does not determine output count. The test does not
settle a particular setting's summoning permissions or required Axes.

### One Harm Described in Several Ways

Three Effects may supply the creature contribution's declared biting-and-stinging
harm. The descriptive variety does not create three separate full allocations.
Likewise, a Poisoned Condition can be the harm produced, rather than an automatic
addition to an already fully allocated generic damage pool.

One Condition can have several fictionally relevant applications without being
copied into several ledgers. Its cause, scope, recovery, and severity still
matter. Renaming a Condition does not automatically add ongoing harm or close
Access; those consequences require their established fictional and mechanical
basis.

### Several Declared Harm Results

For a contribution permitted to produce multiple results, compare these
candidate allocations of its three earned Effects:

| Allocation | Accounting result |
|---|---|
| Three to one declared harm | Conserves three |
| One to wounding, two to poisoning | Conserves three |
| Three to wounding AND three to poisoning | Duplicates output; six from three |

The split demonstrates conservation, not that a particular poisoning, disabling
effect, or Condition always costs one Effect. The existing baseline does not
fully specify mixed-result allocation. Merely declaring an assortment of results
does not establish permission, appropriate Difficulty, or sufficiency of output.

Where actual types encounter different protection, preserve them. An illustrative
four impact / one wounding / two poisoning allocation remains seven generated
Effects. Matching mitigation of two / zero / one leaves two / one / one.
The matching mitigation values are fixture controls, not adopted armor ratings.

## Failure and Dependency

If the creature contribution fails, the wave may still strike normally.
If the wave fails, the creatures do not arrive through that missing wave.
In this fixture they cannot independently reach the target, although their
successful manifestation may remain locally. Other viable means would change
that fictional conclusion, not require a special species rule.

## Area-Only Contribution Control

For the current proposed fixed-declared-area interpretation, three surviving
Effects mean one baseline accomplishment plus two additional Levels. They do
not mean successful coverage plus three more Levels. Additional output must
improve the declared contribution; area-only output does not automatically add
damage. No unit-per-Level or precision-shaping price is adopted here.

## Result and Recommendation

Ordinary delivery inside an attack needs no separate roll or Effect payment.
Damage versus Condition is not the correct universal dividing line because
Conditions already express harm in LENS.

The useful test is whether the narration describes the already generated effect
or claims another independently consequential result. Preserve one traceable
allocation in either case.

Recommend allowing allocation among declared, permitted results of a contribution
without duplicating its output. This is the remaining candidate clarification,
not a new permission to exchange every Effect for any imaginable consequence.
Specific area/distance increments and ongoing or disabling effects remain open.

## Verification and Limits

`lens-action-scope-effect-allocation-test-v0.1.cjs` passed its completed-roll,
conservation, cancellation, matching-protection, and dependency assertions.
These checks verify arithmetic and the stated candidate accounting. They do
not independently prove the semantic scope or prices of mixed effects.

This is a doctrine-grounded scenario test, not an encounter or probability
simulation. No source baseline, glossary, or setting authority was modified.
