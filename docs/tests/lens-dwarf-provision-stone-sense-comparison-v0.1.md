# Dwarf Starting Provisions: Stone-Sense Comparison

Status: User-authorized scenario comparison. No universal species package,
new HOLT, numeric Resonance amount, provision allowance, or price adopted.

## Matched Packages

Keep all ordinary construction, Scale, developed capabilities, equipment, and
starting provisions equal except for the added sense.

Package A contains these current discussion candidates:

- Darkvision: sensory Access with setting-defined limits, held equal here.
- Resonance to Resistance Expressions against naturally occurring poisons.
- Resonance to Observation Expressions involving stone, stonework, gems, or
  precious metals.

Package B contains everything in A, plus the extraordinary sense below.
Resonance values remain unspecified and identical in both packages. This is
an Access comparison, not a success-rate test.

## B's Added Sense: Test Definition Only

By directly touching a section of stone, the Character can attempt to perceive
a concealed fracture within that section or a hollow immediately behind it,
even where no ordinary visual, audible, or tactile clue is available.

The scenarios concern local features of a touched block, support, or wall
section. They do not establish a numerical range or sensing through an entire
mountain. The sense does not reveal detailed room contents, identify every
mineral, establish market value, or provide engineering knowledge.

Those are boundaries of this test permission, not automatically separate
discounts from an assumed universal sense. No downside absent from A is added
to B merely to force equivalent value.

## Scenario Results

| Situation | Package A | Package B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visible tool marks on a stone surface | Observation with applicable Resonance | Same route and same Resonance |
| A fracture entirely within a touched support, with no ordinary clue | Cannot directly perceive it through the listed traits alone | Added sense permits a relevant Observation attempt |
| A hollow directly behind a touched wall section, with no ordinary clue | Cannot directly perceive it through the listed traits alone | Added sense permits a relevant Observation attempt |
| Search a whole hillside for distant hidden deposits | Listed traits grant no remote survey permission | This local contact sense also grants no remote survey permission |
| Determine the sale value of a visible gemstone | Noticing qualities does not automatically supply appraisal expertise | Same; the added sense does not grant that expertise |
| Identify a person or document inside a concealed room | No such permission from these features | Sensing a hollow does not reveal those contents |
| Resist an applicable natural poison | Resistance Expression with relevant Resonance | Identical provision; the sense adds nothing |

Unavailable direct perception does not prohibit Package A from trying another
fictionally available approach: tapping, drilling, research, inference from
other evidence, equipment, or a separately granted Lens. The hidden-feature
fixtures deliberately stipulate no ordinary clue to isolate the added sense.
They do not require a new roll or make all real inspections automatic failures.

## Resonance and Access Together

In B's hidden-fracture example, the added sense makes sensory information
available. The existing stone-observation trait can then provide its relevant
Resonance to that Observation Expression, under the normal aggregation rules.
These are distinct contributions: permission and improved execution.

The sense does not earn another numerical bonus merely because it granted
Access. Nor does this require separate sense, observation, and interpretation
rolls for one declared objective. Roll only when the existing uncertainty
procedure calls for it. Easy or automatic applications remain possible.

## Package Advantage and Estimate Check

B retains all of A's options and adds meaningful direct-perception options in
the concealed-fracture and hollow scenarios. There is no compensating loss in
this fixture. It therefore supplies more permission, although its practical
importance depends on the setting and play.

For the examined Observation routes, both packages can use Awareness. Adding
this permission need not add another Derived Attribute area at all. Even if
the HOLT scope estimator were used as a diagnostic analogy, the counted area
change could be zero while the granted information changes substantially.

This is not a new application of HOLT pricing to Classification provisions.
It illustrates why the approved estimator cannot, by itself, price different
permissions within the same area. Do not count each sensory feature as an
additional Axis or invent a new Derived Attribute to force the estimate upward.

## Construction Implications

"Granted by the HOLD" identifies where construction specifies a provision;
it does not establish zero value. Under the proposed whole-package comparison,
the extra sense must be visible in what that starting choice supplies.

Possible accommodation remains a setting decision:

- Include it within a sufficiently generous common starting provision.
- Exchange another benefit or introduce a genuinely meaningful package burden.
- Offer it as an additional acquisition rather than an automatic provision.

These alternatives do not require every benefit to have a matching defect or
every extraordinary sense to become a separately ranked HOLT. A single sense
may be a granted permission; a broader, developable Theme is a different
construction question.

## Finding and Return Point

The comparison establishes an additional provision, not its point value.
Package B is not an equivalent substitute for A merely because both use
Awareness. No numerical balance claim is possible without defining the common
starting allowance and the actual Resonance amounts.

Recommendation: keep A as the comparison baseline and B as an expanded option
while deciding what the setting includes in its common starting provision.
Do not automatically grant the addition, invent a balancing weakness, or
finalize a price through this test. The next real construction question is
whether expanded permissions like this are included in the shared starting
allowance or offered as optional additional provisions.

Verification: seven controlled narrative scenarios compared. No dice
simulation or empirical playtest was performed; the access differences follow
from the explicitly stated test definitions.

## Follow-On: Exchange Instead of Adding

Status: Assistant-proposed variant comparison under the approved package-test
lane. No equal exchange or final variant is adopted. The earlier comparison
above predates the selected modifier amounts and remains historical context.

For this comparison A is the current assembled test package, including both
x1.10 Resonance traits and x0.90 Dense Build (including jumping). B still adds
stone sense without surrendering anything. Candidate C instead replaces stone
Observation Resonance with the defined contact sense; its darkvision, poison
Resonance, and Dense Build remain identical to A.

Use the prior diagnostic pre-trait rating 60 for already uncertain, permitted
Expressions. Hold Difficulty and other contributions constant within each
matched row. These checkpoints are not universal Difficulty assignments for
the named actions. A lack of sensory Access is not a 0% roll; A may seek other
methods whose ratings are not evaluated by this direct-perception comparison.

| Declared application | A: Resonance package | C: sensory exchange |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observe available tool marks in stone | 66% | 60% |
| Notice a visible feature in a gem or precious metal | 66% | 60% |
| Directly sense the tested concealed fracture without ordinary clues | No direct Access from this package | Access; 60% at the diagnostic checkpoint |
| Directly sense the tested hollow behind touched stone | No direct Access from this package | Access; 60% at the diagnostic checkpoint |
| Resist an applicable natural poison | 66% | 66% |
| Jump a gap where Dense Build applies | 54% | 54% |
| Unrelated permitted action | 60% | 60% |

The exact chance values reuse the verified enumeration in
`lens-dwarf-package-modifier-comparison-v0.1.ps1`. No numerical outcome is
assigned to an unavailable direct-perception route.

C sacrifices broader, improved observation of available material evidence
for a narrower new channel of information. Unlike B's pure addition, C is not
a strict improvement over A. It loses six percentage points in the two matched
ordinary-observation examples and gains permission in the two hidden-feature
examples. Do not equate those illustrative row counts or six points with the
value of sensory Access. Campaign importance still governs the tradeoff.

Both variants keep the negative. Neither darkvision nor stone sense removes
the need to account for jumping, buoyancy, or weight-sensitive crossings.
The exchange is useful as a second complete package for further discussion,
not yet evidence that it fits the same starting allowance.

Return point: review A and C as genuinely different package candidates before
declaring the exchange available at the same provision level. No additional
trait catalogue or new player-facing point pool is needed to frame that choice.
