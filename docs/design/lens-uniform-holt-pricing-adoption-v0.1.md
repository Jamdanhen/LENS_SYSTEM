# Uniform HOLT Pricing Adoption

Date: 2026-09-15
Status: User-approved working rules.

After testing the proposal and comparing it with other development purchases,
the user approved implementation: "All right, word. Let's do it."

## Purchases

| Purchase | Base points |
| --- | ---: |
| Attribute rank | 60 |
| Derived Attribute rank | 20 |
| Defined HOLT Access, once | 20 |
| Axis rank | 20 |
| Task rank | 10 |
| Field rank | 10 |
| Specialty rank | 5 |
| Precision rank | 5 |

Only HOLT Access and Axis prices change. Twenty is the user's clean common
pricing unit for percentage arithmetic, not recovery of a former acquisition
charge. Access is purchased once per HOLT, not once per Axis. The Rank-0
purchase description means permission without an Axis bonus (x1.0), not a
ranked HOLT. Access does not automatically open every conceivable Axis.
No free Axis ranks, parent-rank layer, new cap, Scale, or permission is granted.

Derived ranks strengthen the additive foundation across applicable Expressions;
Axis ranks strengthen a particular HOLT's Derived relationship through their
existing x(1 + rank/10) multiplier. Equal price does not imply identical value.
Existing Rank horizons, Access conditions, and ordinary parent caps remain.

## Limitations

Assess the actual combined burden through GM negotiation in the applicable
campaign, then express the agreed reduction in ten-percent bands. Apply that
reduction once to each purchase it actually restricts. Whole-HOLT restrictions
can affect Access and its Axis development. One-Axis restrictions do not
automatically discount the whole Access purchase or unrelated Axes.

| Reduction | Affected purchase price |
| --- | ---: |
| None | 20 |
| 10% | 18 |
| 20% | 16 |
| 30% | 14 |
| 40% | 12 |
| 50% | 10 |
| 60% | 8 |
| 70% | 6 |
| 80% | 4 |
| 90% | 2 |

These values demonstrate arithmetic, not automatic approval of severe discounts
or a universal ceiling. A 100% reduction would yield free purchases and is not
authorized. Restriction names, calendar frequency, or counted boxes do not
determine fair reductions. Do not automatically stack limitation percentages.
Discounting affected Access and development once each discounts each affected
expenditure once; it does not compound the reduction. Do not add a duplicate
compensation credit for the same loss.

Scope prompts such as Duration, Area, Distance, Intensity, and Selectivity,
and conditions such as time windows or prerequisites, help describe actual
loss. No mandatory five- or ten-category equal-weight model is adopted.
Preserve the accepted negotiation guidance: losing meaningful area capability
or reach beyond contact can support a 20% comparison; unused extremes do not
automatically equal that loss. Preferring the restricted style does not erase
real limitations. Countermeasures and persistent outputs can affect practical
burden and must be considered in context, not automatically prohibited.

## Supersession And Preserved Boundaries

This replaces 40-point HOLT Access, 15-point Axis ranks, and the acquisition-only
25/50/75-percent discount schedule. Historical tests retain their inputs and
results. The existing acquisition-only buyoff formula does not settle repayment
for discounted development under this model.

Still unresolved: extreme reductions; transfers of restrictions between different purchases;
story-granted development without a paid basis; existing-character conversion and refunds;
legacy parent-rank grants; and any separate HOLD-defined returned credits or
reinvestment rules. No automatic refund, free Axis rank, or retroactive repricing
of characters is authorized. Narrative eligibility and non-negotiable setting
restrictions remain intact.

Awards, immediate point spending, other development prices, protected creation
allocations, and character balances are unchanged. Starting and advancement
purchases use the same new price schedule prospectively.

## Eligible Limitation Buyoff

Subsequently adopted on 2026-09-15 after the buyoff comparison, with the user's
explicit caveat: both full removal and each partial easing must make narrative
sense. The user then instructed continuation.

Pay the price difference for the affected Access and Axis purchases already
held, crediting prior purchase and buyoff payments. Do not charge unaffected
purchases, repay the same savings twice, or charge hypothetical future ranks.
Future purchases use the newly agreed remaining reduction. Assess the combined
remaining burden through GM negotiation rather than subtracting stacked labels.

The applicable HOLD and fiction determine whether gradual easing, all-at-once
removal, or no buyoff is possible. Points do not independently authorize any
of those changes. Temporary satisfaction of an Access Condition is not buyoff
and does not itself require payment. Non-negotiable restrictions remain binding.

At unchanged ranks, narratively permitted staged easing and full removal reach
the same total price. Access plus Axis 5 at 20% off costs 96; full removal costs
24. Easing to 10% costs 12, and removing the rest later costs another 12.
No extra surcharge applies. No character balances are changed by adoption.

`docs/tests/lens-uniform-limitation-buyoff-test-v0.1.cjs` passed 2,805
transitions and 11,220 staged paths, plus mixed-history and scope fixtures.
This settles eligible paid easing/removal, not legacy conversion or story grants.
Replacement and increased burden are addressed below.

## Limitation Replacement

Adopted 2026-09-15: allow narratively justified replacement at unchanged cost
when assessed combined burden and affected purchases remain equivalent. Charge
the difference on affected purchases already held when the replacement reduces
burden, crediting prior payments under the buyoff procedure.

The GM assesses the actual restriction and campaign context. Equal names,
claimed percentages, or merely convenient switching do not establish narrative
eligibility or equivalent burden. Six affected purchases moving from a 30%
reduction to 20% require 12 more points; equivalent 20%-to-20% replacement on
the same purchases requires no additional payment.

Transfers that relieve some purchases while restricting others remain unresolved.
No transferable credit follows. No new discount ceiling, award, or existing-
character conversion is authorized.

## Increased Limitation Burden

Adopted 2026-09-15: a newly increased permanent limitation burden can change
future affected purchase prices after narrative and GM assessment, but does not
refund previous spending or create spendable points. The user's clarification:
"you're not getting money back or points back for something bad happening."
Temporary Conditions and setbacks do not automatically reprice a capability.
Separate story rewards are not automatic refunds or duplicate point awards.

Preserve actual prior purchase and buyoff payments as the paid basis for the
same purchases. Do not erase this value or charge it again if the restriction
is later eased. Later ranks bought more cheaply retain their own paid basis.
This is not transferable currency or an authorization to move development.

Example: Access plus Axis 5 bought at 20% off cost 96. A later permanent 40%
limitation would price that package at 72, but returns no 24-point refund.
Future affected ranks cost 12. A narratively permitted return to the former
20% state at unchanged ranks costs no additional points because 96 was already
paid. Any subsequently bought ranks must be included at their actual paid basis.

## Evidence

`docs/tests/lens-uniform-twenty-point-holt-pricing-test-v0.1.cjs` passed 550
uniform-package cases and 1,210 mixed-scope cases. Its companion Markdown
records the comparison. These verify arithmetic and scoped application, not
every fictional valuation or full encounter balance.

Unrestricted Access plus one Rank-5 Axis costs 120; Rank 10 costs 220.
Five Rank-5 Axes plus Access cost 520. An agreed 20% whole-package reduction
gives Access plus Axis 10 for 176. An illustrative 80% gives 44, showing why
large reductions require meaningful restrictions rather than automatic awards.

At net savings of 19 or 15 per session, unrestricted Access plus Axis 10 takes
12 or 15 awards from zero. These are affordability references, not new award
rates or advancement gates. Protected allocations do not become HOLT funding.
