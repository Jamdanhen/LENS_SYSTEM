## Approved Test and Boundaries

The user approved comparing three character profiles across existing Difficulty
steps, including the attack sacrificed or reduced when pushing. Read local and
inherited guidance, the preceding movement tests, and current Difficulty and
continuation rules. Do not revive the closed two-ordinary-action experiment.

For this fixture:

```text
Movement only: 12 automatic units + one unit per push Effect; no attack.
Push with attack: 6 automatic units + one unit per push Effect.
Both independently resolved Expressions in the latter case receive /2.
```

Treat the movement-only push as part of the committed movement action for this
comparison, not as another separately charged action. These are the two modes
approved for testing, not a newly adopted universal action-accounting rule.

Use applicable training only, ordinary Scale, full function and neutral context.
No equipment, HOLT, preparation, paid point or new modifier is added. The attack
uses its own circumstances, not the movement Difficulty copied onto it. This
test measures movement output, not probability of landing the accompanying hit.

Units are approximately meters, not mandatory squares. Round duration remains
unresolved. No world-record speed or physiological calibration is claimed.

```text
Raw = Mobility x product(1 + each subsequent Rank/10)
Difficulty = 0.98 x 0.6^(D-1)
Push Rating = floor(Raw x Difficulty / action divisor)
Successful Effects = 1 + floor((Rating - adjusted roll)/10)
```

Only the final Rating is floored. D1 is disputed as a default and included as
a control, not adopted. D2, D3 and D4 use existing curve values unchanged.
Use the existing executable inclusive success boundary; the older prose
'under' ambiguity is not resolved by this record.

## Procedure

Enumerate ordinary faces02-98. Count99 and00 as push failures, with no extra
distance from success. Count01 using its adopted distance-from100 continuation,
including repeated01. There is no new exceptional-distance ceiling.

Medians include failures, rather than selecting successful rolls only. They
describe the nominal allowance before any separate fictional complication:
a failed push grants no extra movement but does not automatically erase the
base. A00 consequence that actually prevents travel must still be resolved;
the test does not grant immunity to such consequences.

For required E Effects, ordinary qualifying rolls satisfy
roll <= Rating - 10*(E-1). For initial01 followed by n additional01s and a
terminal r from02 through00 (100), adjusted roll is r-99-99*n. Sum qualifying
terminal probabilities until every remaining terminal qualifies; at that point
the whole remaining prefix probability qualifies, including all longer chains.
This calculates the infinite tail without an artificial repeat limit.

## Tactical Magnitude and Exceptional Results

At D2 the peak fixture reaches at least24 units on78% of movement-only attempts
and at least30 on18%. While attacking, reaching24 has only0.23% probability.
Those are stationary route-length thresholds, not attack-hit probabilities.

At D1 the peak fixture's median is39 without an attack and17 while attacking.
At D2 those medians are26 and11. At D3 they are19 and7. The action sacrifice
therefore materially changes the high-end assessment; large movement cannot
be treated as free distance accompanying an undiminished attack.

Initial01 followed by06 produces adjusted roll-93. For the peak D2 fixture this
means41 total units without attacking, or25 with the reduced attack. These are
special-result examples, not ordinary maxima. Longer01 chains remain possible
under the existing rule and are not capped to rescue movement calibration.

## Assessment and Decision Boundary

1. None of the tested Difficulty steps makes the modest fixture's push reliable.
   Even removing the D1 multiplier leaves floor(16.875)=16, the same success
   chance as its D1 control. Increasing Difficulty cannot repair this low end.
2. Higher Difficulty does reduce the peak fixture's extra distance, but also
   reduces highly trained reliability and the already small modest chance.
   The maximum/minimum tension cannot be solved by pretending Difficulty only
   affects top-end characters.
3. Reliable base travel remains available: six with an ordinary action, twelve
   when committed to movement. Low push probability is not an inability to run.
4. D2 is a useful candidate for a genuinely demanding push: it yields median26
   for the fully invested movement-only fixture and11 with an attack. That is
   not evidence to adopt it universally or proof that D1 is too generous.
5. There is no demonstrated need here for a cap, special divisor, new attribute
   rule, or changed Capacity. Do not add one merely to force a desired table.

Recommendation: retain the six/twelve base and one-unit Effect conversion as
test candidates. Do not choose push Difficulty solely to suppress the peak.
The next real judgment is whether an ordinary modestly trained character is
expected to exceed the twelve-unit committed allowance reliably, or whether
that allowance already supplies their dependable movement and pushing beyond
it is a genuinely uncertain extra. If reliability is required, none of these
Difficulty choices meets it under the present inputs. If not, the low success
rate need not be treated as a defect. No final Difficulty is adopted here.
